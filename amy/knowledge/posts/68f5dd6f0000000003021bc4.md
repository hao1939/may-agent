Title: Reasoning with Sampling: Your Base Model is Smarter Than You Think
arxiv：2510.14901
	
Motivation:
当前主流范式是通过RL进行后训练，但这套方法不仅成本高昂、需要大量标注数据和验证器，还常常以牺牲生成多样性为代价。论文的核心出发点在于质疑一个基本假设：通过RL学到的推理能力，究竟是模型不具备的新行为，还是仅仅对Base Model已有知识的分布锐化（即更倾向于选择那些本来就存在但概率不高的正确推理路径）？
	
Contribution：
提出一种名为“幂采样”（Power Sampling）的全新、无需训练的推理时（inference-time）采样算法。该算法通过引入经典的马尔可夫链蒙特卡洛（MCMC）技术，直接从基础模型中激发其潜在的、强大的单次推理（single-shot）能力，使其在效果上能够媲美甚至超越经过复杂RL训练后的模型，同时还避免了RL的诸多弊端。
	
Conclusion：
现有基础大模型的能力远未被充分发掘，其内部蕴含着比标准采样方法所能展现的更强大的推理潜力。通过在推理时投入更多计算（而非训练），Power Sampling算法可以有效解锁这些潜力，达到甚至超越昂贵的RL后训练方法所能实现的效果，基础模型的高似然区域与高质量的推理能力之间存在紧密的正相关关系。
	
Insights：
完全抛弃了模型训练、验证器和特定数据集的需求，仅使用基础模型自身的似然度（likelihoods）作为优化的唯一信号。在提升单次推理准确率的同时，完美保留了生成多样性，解决了RL方法长期存在的“高分低能”（单次表现好，多次采样能力下降）问题。

## Abstract

Frontier reasoning models have exhibited incredible capabilities across a wide array of disciplines, driven by posttraining large language models (LLMs) with reinforcement learning (RL). However, despite the widespread success of this paradigm, much of the literature has been devoted to disentangling truly novel behaviors that emerge during RL but are not present in the base models. In our work, we approach this question from a different angle, instead asking whether comparable reasoning capabilites can be elicited from base models at inference time by *pure sampling*, *without any additional training*. Inspired by Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques for sampling from sharpened distributions, we propose a simple iterative sampling algorithm leveraging the base models' own likelihoods. Over different base models, we show that our algorithm offers substantial boosts in reasoning that nearly match and even outperform those from RL on a wide variety of single-shot tasks, including MATH500, HumanEval, and GPQA. Moreover, our sampler avoids the collapse in diversity over multiple samples that is characteristic of RL-posttraining. Crucially, our method does not require training, curated datasets, or a verifier, suggesting broad applicability beyond easily verifiable domains.
