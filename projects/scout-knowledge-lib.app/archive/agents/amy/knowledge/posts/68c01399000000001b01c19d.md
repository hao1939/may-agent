🔍 内容导读:
针对大模型推理训练中SFT（监督微调）与RL（强化学习）脱节的问题，微软研究院提出了一种名为BRIDGE的协同训练新框架。该方法利用双层优化（bilevel optimization）让SFT学习如何指导RL，实现了两大范式的深度协作，在多个推理基准上显著提升了模型的性能和训练效率。
	
📚 背景与挑战:
提升大模型的数学和代码等复杂推理能力是前沿热点，强化学习是关键技术但训练效率低下。业界普遍采用“SFT预热、RL微调”的两阶段流程，但这导致了严重的“灾难性遗忘”问题，即RL阶段会忘掉SFT学到的知识。同时，SFT的指导是一次性的，无法在RL探索陷入困境时持续提供帮助，导致探索效率低下。
	
💡 方法与创新:
BRIDGE框架的核心创新在于将SFT与RL的结合建模为一个双层优化（bilevel optimization）问题。它将SFT视为上层“教师”任务，RL视为下层“学生”任务，通过一个分离的LoRA模块，让SFT学习如何调整参数以更好地指导RL的探索过程。其更新机制巧妙地设计了一个“协同增益”（cooperative gain）目标，确保SFT的每一步指导都能最大化“SFT+RL”组合相较于“纯RL”的性能优势，从而实现真正的双赢。
	
🎯 实验与成果:
研究团队在Qwen、Llama等3个不同尺寸的模型和5个数学推理基准上进行了全面实验。结果显示，BRIDGE在性能上稳定超越了所有基线方法，包括传统的“先SFT后RL”的两阶段范式。相较于主流的Cold-start方法，BRIDGE不仅在平均准确率上实现了高达11.8%至30.7%的显著提升，还在训练时间上节省了14%至44%。训练动态分析也证实，该方法有效避免了灾难性遗忘，实现了更高效的策略学习。

## Abstract

Reinforcement learning (RL) has proven effective in incentivizing the reasoning abilities of large language models (LLMs), but suffers from severe efficiency challenges due to its trial-and-error nature. While the common practice employs supervised fine-tuning (SFT) as a warm-up stage for RL, this decoupled two-stage approach limits interaction between SFT and RL, thereby constraining overall effectiveness. This study introduces BRIDGE, a novel method for learning reasoning models that employs *bilevel optimization* to facilitate better cooperation between these training paradigms. By conditioning the SFT objective on the optimal RL policy, our approach enables SFT to meta-learn how to guide RL's optimization process. During training, the lower-level performs RL updates while simultaneously receiving SFT supervision, while the upper-level explicitly maximizes the *cooperative gain*—the performance advantage of joint SFT-RL training over RL alone. Empirical evaluations across three LLMs and five reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms baselines and achieves a better balance between effectiveness and efficiency.‡

## 1 Introduction

The emergence of OpenAI's o1 [27] and DeepSeek-R1 [11] marks a significant advance in LLM reasoning capabilities, particularly for challenging tasks such as mathematics [9, 17] and programming [6, 10]. The key technique driving this progress is large-scale, rule-based RL. However, the inherently trial-and-error nature of RL renders the training process highly inefficient. An alternative approach is SFT on curated long chain-of-thought (CoT) datasets, which enables models to rapidly acquire effective reasoning patterns through imitation learning. While more sample-efficient, SFT typically exhibits poorer performance and generalization than RL [8].

In practice, production-scale training pipelines often adopt a multi-stage paradigm, using SFT as a warm-up phase before applying RL. For example, DeepSeek-R1 [11] undergoes multiple rounds of SFT and RL to refine reasoning performance. However, our experiments reveal that this approach fails to fully leverage the strengths of both methods. The fully *decoupled* two-stage setup can suffer from catastrophic forgetting and inefficient exploration. This raises a natural question:

*Can we design a training framework that enables meaningful synergy between SFT and RL, ensuring their cooperation yields performance superior to standalone RL?*
