总结整理了一下RL策略优化中常用的数学分析工具，内容较多，分几次来分享[暗中观察R]
	
主要包括以下三大部分：
1.性能比较与分布扰动层
PDL、PGT、概率距离（KL/TV）、Occ Bound、Concentrability 常数。把性能差异转化为分布差异，并提供安全的迁移度量。
2.几何与优化工具层
KL 球（类比欧氏球）与 DRO、DV 公式、镜像下降、自然梯度、Bregman 投影。把分布差转化为近端更新问题，并解释指数倾斜解的几何合理性。
3.稳定性与鲁棒性层
χ² 散度、次高斯变量、TRPO 下界、重要性采样、熵正则、条件假设。确保更新在误差和不确定性下仍然稳定，构成理论应用的安全底线。
	
接下来介绍前三小节：
1. 性能差异引理（Performance Difference Lemma, PDL）
PDL 是比较两策略性能的基本工具，它把性能差表达为新策略占用测度下旧策略优势的期望。这样将动态累积的回报差异转化为静态的分布—函数期望，从而提供了性能改进的基本刻度：若新策略在旧策略看来更多采取正优势动作，就能保证性能提升。几乎所有单调改进界与收敛性分析都从 PDL 出发，它是整个理论链条的起点。
	
2. 策略梯度定理（Policy Gradient Theorem, PGT）
PGT 给出性能函数对参数的梯度形式：梯度等于“对数策略导数与 Q 值”的期望。它解释了为何可以通过采样估计策略梯度，并利用基线减法降低方差。PGT 是从 静态性能比较（PDL） 过渡到 动态参数优化 的桥梁，构成基于梯度的近端方法的理论基石。
	
3. 概率距离与不等式（KL 散度、全变差 TV、f-散度）
为了度量策略分布的差异，需要概率距离。KL 散度、TV 距离和广义 f-散度提供了不同刻度。Pinsker 不等式把 KL 与 TV 联系起来，使得 KL 约束可以转化为期望差的上界。这些不等式的意义在于为信赖域（trust region）提供了数学解释：更新被限制在 KL/TV 半径内，保证了策略分布变化的可控性。

# 1 Notation and MDP Preliminaries

**Definition 1.1** (MDP). *An MDP is a tuple* (S, A, P, r, γ) *with state space* S*, action space* A*, transition kernel* P(ds'|s, a)*, reward* r : S × A → R*, and discount* γ ∈ (0, 1)*.*

**Assumption 1.2** (Measurability and domination). S, A *are standard Borel spaces; all kernels and densities are measurable. Whenever a divergence* D_KL(p‖q) *appears,* p ≪ q *(absolute continuity) is assumed.*

**Definition 1.3** (Policy, values, advantage, performance). *A (randomized) policy* π(da|s)*; value and Q-value*

$$V^\pi(s) = \mathbb{E}_\pi\Bigl[\sum_{t\geq 0} \gamma^t r(s_t, a_t) \mid s_0 = s\Bigr], \quad Q^\pi(s,a) = r(s,a) + \gamma\, \mathbb{E}_{s'\sim P(\cdot|s,a)}[V^\pi(s')].$$

*Advantage* $A^\pi(s,a) = Q^\pi(s,a) - V^\pi(s)$*; performance* $J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{s_0 \sim d_0}[V^\pi(s_0)]$.

**Definition 1.4** (Discounted occupancy measure). d^π *on* S *is*

$$d^\pi(\mathrm{d}s) = (1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t\,\mathbb{P}(s_t \in \mathrm{d}s \mid \pi), \qquad d^\pi \ \textit{is a probability measure.}$$

**Lemma 1.5** (Discounted expectation identity). *For any integrable* f : S × A → R*,*

$$(1-\gamma)\,\mathbb{E}_\pi\Bigl[\sum_{t\geq 0} \gamma^t f(s_t, a_t)\Bigr] = \mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^\pi,\, a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}[f(s,a)].$$

*Proof.* Exchange summation and expectation and apply the definition of d^π. □

*Remark* 1.6 (Alternative occupancy). One may also use the undiscounted visitation $d_T^\pi = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{P}(s_t \in \cdot \mid \pi)$; for ergodic chains it converges to the stationary law.

**Interpretation and Use.** This section fixes the measure-theoretic setting so that all expectations/divergences are well-defined. The discounted occupancy converts path sums to static expectations, enabling comparison theorems and bounds. Absolute continuity p ≪ q is crucial wherever D_KL is used; otherwise D_KL = ∞ and KL-based arguments are invalid.

**Assumptions and Pitfalls.** Check integrability of rewards and log-densities; ensure support overlap when moving between policies; for continuous actions, require densities and differentiability when gradients/natural gradients are used.

# 2 Probability Distances and Fundamental Inequalities

**Definition 2.1** (TV and KL). $\mathrm{D_{TV}}(p,q) = \frac{1}{2}\int|p-q|$*, and* $\mathrm{D_{KL}}(p\|q) = \mathbb{E}_p[\log(p/q)]$ *when* p ≪ q*.*

**Theorem 2.2** (Pinsker's inequality). $\mathrm{D_{TV}}(p,q) \leq \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{D_{KL}}(p\|q)}$.

**Proposition 2.3** (Expectation difference bound). *If |f| ≤ B, then |𝔼_p f − 𝔼_q f| ≤ 2B D_TV(p, q).*

*Proof.* Use sup_{‖g‖_∞≤1} |𝔼_p g − 𝔼_q g| = 2D_TV(p, q) with g = f/B. □

*Remark* 2.4 (Other useful inequalities). Bretagnolle–Huber: ‖p − q‖_1 ≤ √(2 − 2e^{−D_KL(p‖q)}). Jensen: 𝔼[√X] ≤ √𝔼[X] for X ≥ 0.

**Interpretation.** TV is a metric quantifying worst-case test discrepancy; KL is an information-theoretic divergence measuring log-likelihood loss when q pretends to be p. Pinsker links them: small KL ⇒ small TV.

**Typical Uses.** (i) Replace KL trust-region constraints by TV step-size bounds; (ii) bound expectation mismatch via Proposition 2.3; (iii) control reweighting variance and robustness.

**Assumptions and Edge Cases.** D_KL(p‖q) requires p ≪ q; Pinsker gives only a one-sided implication (small KL ⇒ small TV, not conversely). For heavy tails, TV may be large even with small KL if support is mismatched.

**Variants.** Bretagnolle–Huber can be tighter at moderate KL; D_α divergences interpolate KL and χ².

## 3 Performance Difference Lemma (PDL)

**Theorem 3.1** (PDL). *For any policies π, π',*

$$J(\pi') - J(\pi) = \frac{1}{1-\gamma} \mathbb{E}_{s \sim d^{\pi'}, a \sim \pi'(\cdot|s)}\Big[A^\pi(s,a)\Big].$$

*Proof 1: Telescoping.* Expand V^{π'} − V^π using Bellman equations, sum over t with discount, and invoke the occupancy identity. □

*Proof 2: Operator form.* Let T^π be the Bellman operator. Use V^{π'} − V^π = (I − γP_{π'})^{−1}(T^π V^π − V^π) and unravel. □

*Remark* 3.2. PDL converts performance comparison to an expectation over (d^{π'}, π') of the old advantage A^π.

**Interpretation.** If π' concentrates probability on actions with positive A^π, then J(π') > J(π). This formula is the workhorse behind monotonic improvement bounds and surrogate objectives.

**Assumptions.** Bounded rewards (or appropriate integrability) to justify exchanging sums/expectations; measurability of policies and kernels.

**Variants.** Symmetric forms using A^{π'}; occupancy reweighting forms under importance sampling; finite-horizon analogues.
