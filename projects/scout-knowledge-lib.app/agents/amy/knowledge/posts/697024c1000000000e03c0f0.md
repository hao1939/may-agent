## Full Text
这篇论文系统研究了一个关键问题：语言模型中广泛存在的Scaling Law，究竟源于语言的复杂语义结构，还是更一般的数据统计与序列预测机制？ 作者通过构造随机游走、n-gram 与随机图等可控数据生成过程，发现即便在“无语义”的合成数据上，Transformer 仍然呈现出与自然语言高度相似的参数–数据–算力缩放律。这一结果对理解大模型的理论基础、以及如何设计更高效的训练策略具有重要意义。
核心亮点：
随机游走也遵循Scaling Law： 在随机图上的序列预测任务中，模型测试损失随参数规模 N、数据规模 D 呈现稳定幂律下降，与自然语言实验结果高度一致。
n-gram / Bigram模型作为可解析基线： 作者将bigram语言模型形式化为加权有向图上的随机游走，证明在该设置下可解析地得到 L=E+B/DL=E+B/DL=E+B/D 形式的缩放行为，为经验Scaling Law提供理论参照。
数据结构影响α，模型容量稳定β： 实验显示，数据复杂度（熵、分布是否呈幂律）主要影响数据指数 α_D，而模型规模指数 β_N 在不同数据源中相对稳定，常接近 0.5。
质疑Chinchilla二维拟合的充分性： 作者指出传统2D Chinchilla 拟合缺乏理论基础，且在多种设置下拟合效果不佳，提出使用神经网络回归或核回归来估计 L(N,D)L(N,D)L(N,D)，效果显著更好。
μP参数化提升算力效率： 在随机游走任务中，μP（maximal update parameterization）表现出更好的参数效率，使最优算力分配不再满足“参数≈数据”的经典假设。
Scaling Law并非语言专属现象： 即使在 Erdős–Rényi 或 Barabási–Albert 随机图上，只要序列预测问题成立，Transformer 就会自然地产生幂律缩放。

# On the origin of neural scaling laws: from random graphs to natural language

Maissam Barkeshli¹,²,*, Alberto Alfarano³,†,*, Andrey Gromov¹

¹Meta Superintelligence Labs, FAIR, ²Department of Physics, University of Maryland, College Park and Joint Quantum Institute, ³Axiom Math
*Equal contribution, †Work done at Meta

Scaling laws have played a major role in the modern AI revolution, providing practitioners predictive power over how the model performance will improve with increasing data, compute, and number of model parameters. This has spurred an intense interest in the origin of neural scaling laws, with a common suggestion being that they arise from power law structure already present in the data. In this paper we study scaling laws for transformers trained to predict random walks (bigrams) on graphs with tunable complexity. We demonstrate that this simplified setting already gives rise to neural scaling laws even in the absence of power law structure in the data correlations. We further consider dialing down the complexity of natural language systematically, by training on sequences sampled from increasingly simplified generative language models, from 4,2,1-layer transformer language models down to language bigrams, revealing a monotonic evolution of the scaling exponents. Our results also include scaling laws obtained from training on random walks on random graphs drawn from Erdős-Renyi and scale-free Barabási-Albert ensembles. Finally, we revisit conventional scaling laws for language modeling, demonstrating that several essential results can be reproduced using 2 layer transformers with context length of 100, provide a critical analysis of various fits used in prior literature, demonstrate an alternative method for obtaining compute optimal curves as compared with current practice in published literature, and provide preliminary evidence that maximal update parameterization may be more parameter efficient than standard parameterization.
