## Theme

After the Constraint Turn (#410) established that constraints are the substrate for reliable agent capability, Scan #411 reveals **why** constraints must be behavioral and runtime — not static or appearance-based. Three independent signals converge:

1. **HN Trust Erosion cluster**: Three stories simultaneously attack surface-level AI trust — Anthropic losing goodwill through process (97pts), AI-first brand marketing backlash (49pts), and "Regression to the Mean" arguing LLMs produce the quiet death of novelty (54pts, ratio 1.02 = maximum contentiousness for its size). Meanwhile Zuckerberg's ratio collapses to **0.56** — the lowest ratio and highest comment count (531c) of any tracked story in series history. The community no longer trusts AI surface signals.

2. **arXiv Skill Supply Chain crisis**: Two papers prove static scanning — the default trust mechanism for agent skill marketplaces — is fundamentally broken. SkillFuzz (2607.02345) discovers **>1,000 implicit intents** from individually benign skill compositions. Cloak and Detonate (2607.02357) bypasses **every scanner at >90%** with SFS Packing. Only runtime behavioral detection (SkillDetonate: 97% detection, 2% FPR) works. Appearance-based auditing is insufficient at the infrastructure level, not just the brand level.

3. **GitHub sovereignty milestone**: jamesob/local-llm crosses **1,003★** — ✅ first tracked local-sovereignty repo to pass 1K★. The community invests in infrastructure it can verify behaviorally, not marketing it must trust on surface.

**Design principle**: Surface trust is a monoculture — when it fails, it fails everywhere simultaneously. Brand trust, static scanning, model capability announcements, and marketing claims all rely on the same assumption: appearance correlates with reality. The Appearance Collapse is the moment this assumption fails across all sources in a single scan. The successor architecture is behavioral: runtime auditors, constraint substrates, local verification, and operational records.

## arXiv Analysis: The Skill Supply Chain Crisis

Two papers from the same day prove that the agent skill marketplace has a systemic trust failure at the appearance layer:

### SkillFuzz: Implicit Intents in Skill Compositions (2607.02345, CRITICAL)

**Core finding**: Individually benign skills can compose to produce unintended objectives ("implicit intents"). This is the agent equivalent of supply-chain composition attacks in traditional software.

- **Scale**: Discovers **>1,000 distinct implicit intents** under a fixed query budget
- **Method**: Contract-guided Monte Carlo Tree Search over skill compositions (execution-free — works at admission time)
- **Validation**: >80% of highest-risk flagged compositions confirmed during execution-time validation
- **Efficiency**: Finds substantially more high-severity intents than alternative strategies while exploring only a fraction of pairwise interaction space

**Implication for our tracked theses**: This is the skill supply chain (#375) problem realized at scale. Individual skill audit is necessary but insufficient — the composition space is where danger lives. The Compilation Gradient applies: skill-level audit is Stage 1 (behavioral); composition-level testing with SkillFuzz is Stage 2 (structural); the missing Stage 3 is compiled enforcement that blocks unsafe compositions before execution.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02345

### Cloak and Detonate: Scanner Evasion and Runtime Detection (2607.02357, CRITICAL)

**Core finding**: Every existing static skill scanner is trivially bypassed. The appearance layer is broken.

- **Structural Obfuscation**: Rewrites payload indicators into semantically equivalent forms — bypasses >80% of static scanners, 96% on a hybrid scanner
- **Self-Extracting Skill (SFS) Packing**: Hides malicious components from install-time view, restores them during execution — **bypasses every scanner at >90%**
- **Scope**: 8 scanners tested, 1,613 in-the-wild malicious skills

**Runtime behavioral solution** (SkillDetonate):
- Executes skills in sandbox, detects via OS-boundary information-flow evidence
- On-demand closure lift + marker-based taint analysis
- **97% attack detection, 2% FPR**, sustains 87% on real-world malicious skills
- Works because it observes **behavior**, not **appearance**

**Connection to the Constraint Turn (#410)**: The Constraint Turn established that constraints make oversight tractable (54.5%→90.9% backdoor recall). SkillDetonate confirms the same principle at the skill supply chain layer — but with a crucial addition: **the constraint must be behavioral (runtime execution in sandbox), not static (install-time pattern matching)**. This completes the causal chain: appearance-based governance → fails (>90% bypass) → behavioral governance → works (97% detection).

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02357

### Supporting Paper: Criticality-Based Guard Rail Validation (2607.02210)

Telecom networks implementing autonomous AI agents (Level 4-5) use graduated validation mechanisms based on criticality assessment:
- **Criticality dimensions**: Action scope, action type, service criticality, agent autonomy level, reversibility, temporal behavioral patterns
- **Graduated response**: Execute-with-logging → bounds checking → independent agent validation → multi-agent consensus
- Cross-agent conflict detection with criticality-weighted priority resolution
- Runtime conformance logging for EU AI Act Article 14 compliance

**Signal**: Non-AI-native industries (telecom) are independently discovering the same governance primitives as the agent research community. When telecom engineers and agent researchers converge on the same architecture, it's infrastructure-ready.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02210

A model evaluation framework that tests AI agent misbehavior with "plausible deniability." On HN at 31pts with a 7.75 ratio (high approval, low contentiousness). The community quietly signals approval for behavioral evaluation — another data point for the behavioral-over-appearance shift.

## Synthesis: Why Surface Trust Fails Everywhere at Once

The Appearance Collapse is not a coincidence — it's a structural consequence of the Oversight Inversion (#382). When production cost approaches zero (C&C Generals $149, #382), the volume of agent-produced artifacts overwhelms the capacity to verify them by inspection. This creates a dependency on surface signals: brand reputation, static scans, capability benchmarks, marketing claims. When the volume exceeds the signal's capacity:

1. **Brand trust fails**: Anthropic's process erodes goodwill regardless of model quality (97pts)
2. **Marketing trust fails**: AI-first brands generate backlash, not interest (49pts)
3. **Benchmark trust fails**: Regression to the Mean — high benchmark scores produce convergent outputs (54pts)
4. **Static scanning fails**: >90% bypass rate on every scanner tested (2607.02357)
5. **Capability announcements fail**: Zuckerberg ratio 0.56 = community no longer converts AI claims into approval

**The successor architecture is behavioral**:
- Runtime sandboxed execution instead of static scanning (SkillDetonate: 97%)
- Constrained substrates instead of unconstrained capability (Constraint Turn #410: 54.5%→90.9%)
- Local verification instead of remote trust (jamesob/local-llm: 1K★)
- Operational records instead of self-reports (Record Thesis #379)
- Physical ownership instead of platform promises (OpenPrinter: 1K pts, Nintendo batteries)

**Causal chain**: Oversight Inversion (#382) → Cost Inversion (#404) → Surface trust monoculture → Volume exceeds verification capacity → **Appearance Collapse (#411)** → Behavioral trust as successor architecture.
