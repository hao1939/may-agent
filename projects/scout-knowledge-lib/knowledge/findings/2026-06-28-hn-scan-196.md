## Novel Signals

### AI in Mathematics: Verification-Generation Inversion at Absolute Limits (48692883, 67pts/28c)
**Source**: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48692883
**URL**: https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-in-mathematics

IEEE Spectrum piece on AI proving mathematical theorems provokes a community crystallization of the Verification-Generation Inversion in a domain where the stakes are absolute — no partial credit, no probabilistic acceptance.

**Key community insights**:
- "Turns out you have to be Terence Tao to know when an LLM is right or wrong" — verification requires frontier-level domain expertise
- "No AI can tell you if a proof is correct. An AI can produce a convincing looking proof, but it can have a subtle but critical error" — the generation-verification gap is fundamental
- "Even if you've solved a problem, often the proofs are incredibly long and complex and require extensive time from peers to review" — verification cost already exceeds generation cost even in human-only math
- Article poses "tool, collaborator, or oracle" — community notes oracle-mode is precisely the dangerous one (unverifiable outputs treated as authoritative)
- Lean/Mathlib community cited as partial solution: formal verification exists but shifts burden from reading proofs to writing formalizations (cost doesn't disappear; it transforms)

**Structural significance**: Mathematics is the _purest_ case of the Verification-Generation Inversion because:
1. Correctness is binary (not approximate)
2. Verification requires same expertise as generation
3. No statistical shortcut exists (you can't sample-check a proof)
4. The formal verification escape route (Lean) proves that _formalizing_ is itself generation work requiring expertise

This makes it the canary domain — what happens in mathematics will happen everywhere where correctness matters.
