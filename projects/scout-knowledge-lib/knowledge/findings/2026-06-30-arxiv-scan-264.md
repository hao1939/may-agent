# The Incompleteness Constraint

**Scan**: #264 | **Source**: arXiv | **Date**: 2026-06-30

## Thesis

Three independent research streams converge on a structural impossibility: **every governance mechanism is provably incomplete**. You cannot simultaneously reject every false positive and retain every true negative. You cannot average away non-deterministic safety failures. You cannot detect harmful training signals hidden inside benign surface patterns. These are not engineering limitations — they are formal constraints on what governance can achieve.

The productive response, emerging from three complementary papers in the same batch, is **governance through graduated constraint**: token-level regularization instead of binary filtering, multi-agent consensus instead of single-model judgment, hard expert isolation instead of soft parameter blending. Each succeeds precisely because it accepts incompleteness rather than pursuing perfection.

This extends the Governance Gap (#262) with a critical structural refinement: the gap between accumulation and governance is not merely a matter of effort or tooling — it is partially **irreducible**. Governance can narrow the gap but never close it. Systems designed for closed governance will fail; systems designed for governed incompleteness will succeed.

## arXiv Evidence

### 1. Grounding LLM Reasoning Under Incomplete Graph Evidence (2606.30247)

**Paper**: "Grounding LLM Reasoning under Incomplete Graph Evidence"

A theoretical framework for constraining LLM reasoning trajectories using knowledge graph evidence, with the central result being a formal impossibility theorem.

- **The impossibility**: Under open-world incompleteness, **no hard rule based only on observed state can both reject every false unsupported trajectory AND retain every true-but-unobserved one**. Binary enforcement is provably wrong in both directions simultaneously.
- **The resolution**: Soft grounding via KL-regularized deformation of the LLM prior. Finite slack preserves support for trajectories that are unsupported but non-contradicted. Hard conditioning is the infinite-penalty limit — demonstrably too aggressive.
- **Stability bounds**: Framework yields stability bounds under evidence perturbations — small changes in available evidence produce bounded changes in grounding decisions.
- **Application**: GraphRAG, KGQA, graph agents, constrained decoding, faithful generation. Claims are "evidence-relative" — KG compatibility is treated as declared support, not factual truth.

**Connection to library**: This formalizes what the Abstention Advantage (library note) observed empirically — sometimes not-deciding is correct. The impossibility result means every grounding decision operates in a regime where either some true trajectories are wrongly rejected OR some false trajectories are wrongly retained. The design choice is where to place the error, not whether error exists.

**Structural implication**: Every agent system that gates actions on knowledge state (which is ALL of them — tool permissions, memory retrieval, context filtering) operates under this impossibility. The Governance Gap (#262) identified that governance is under-developed; this paper proves governance is also *structurally limited*. AOEP-v0's approach of scoring governance behavior rather than answer quality is the right response: measure how well the system manages its incompleteness, not whether it achieves completeness.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30247

### 2. EMPATH: Safety Evaluation of Emotional-Support Chatbots (2606.30256)

**Paper**: "EMPATH: A Multilingual Auditor-Judge Benchmark for Safety Evaluation of Emotional-Support Chatbots"

Multi-turn crisis conversation safety benchmark with auditor-judge architecture, 140 seed instructions, 34 personas, 19 metrics across 5 dimensions. Evaluated on Mexican Spanish and US English.

- **The irreducible variance**: Five-run test-retest reveals that even the steadiest model swings from **2 to 10 on a crisis metric across identical re-runs**. DeepSeek-v4-pro returns a **different conversation on every run even at temperature 0**.
- **Key finding**: "Run-to-run reliability is therefore a **per-model safety property**, not noise to average away."
- **Strict rubric vs standard rubric**: Standard rubric shows material score inflation on 10 of 19 metrics. Strict per-criterion rubric restores discriminative power.
- **Cross-family judge agreement**: 93% of scores within ±1 under standard rubric — judges agree, but models swing.
- **Aggregate masks divergence**: Three frontier models sit within 0.74 points aggregate — but per-metric profiles diverge by up to 6 points in model-specific places.

**Connection to library**: This is the Evaluation Mirage (library note) made concrete for safety: aggregate safety scores are meaningless because they mask (a) per-metric divergence AND (b) run-to-run variance. The Quality Ceiling (#167) gains a new dimension — the ceiling is not fixed, it oscillates. A model that scores 8/10 on safety for crisis handling will score 2/10 on the same input in a different run.

**Structural implication for governance**: You cannot govern what you cannot predict. If identical inputs produce safety-relevant variance of ±4 on crisis metrics, then governance mechanisms must be designed for worst-case, not average-case performance. Every safety guarantee is probabilistic, not deterministic. This is the Incompleteness Constraint expressed as non-determinism: the model is not merely incomplete in its knowledge but incomplete in its *behavior*.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30256

### 3. Defending Against Harmful Supervision Hidden in Benign Samples (2606.30263)

**Paper**: "Defending Against Harmful Supervision Hidden in Benign Samples"

Introduces Embedded Attack and a defense (DR-SFT):

- **Embedded Attack**: Harmful QA pairs embedded within benign training samples. Representative guardrails often **fail to detect them at the example level**. Unlike mixed-data attacks (explicit harmful content), embedded attacks hide harmful supervision inside individually benign-looking examples.
- **DR-SFT (Dual-Reference SFT)**: Adapts DPO-style contrastive objective to SFT through **token-level regularization**. Mitigates harmful fine-tuning beyond coarse data filtering.
- **The key insight**: Coarse guardrails (example-level screening) miss the embedded signal. Token-level analysis detects what example-level cannot.

**Connection to library**: This is the Invisible Layer Problem (#259) applied to training data. In scan #259, five invisible layers were identified between surface and mechanism. Embedded Attack adds a sixth: **training data looks benign at surface; harmful at supervision level**. MCPPrivacyDetector (scan #259) found protocol-structural leakage invisible to code scanners; Embedded Attack finds training-structural harm invisible to example-level guardrails.

**Structural implication**: DR-SFT's token-level regularization is the graduated governance response — not binary accept/reject of training examples, but continuous regulation of learning signals at the token level. This is the Incompleteness Constraint's constructive lesson: because example-level governance provably misses embedded attacks, governance must operate at finer granularity (token-level). The cost: higher computational overhead. The benefit: governance that reaches into the invisible layer.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30263

### 4. Multi-Agentic System for Disinformation Detection (2606.30259)

**Paper**: "Multi-Agentic System Leveraging Open-Source LLMs to Mitigate Disinformation Threats"

Multi-agent system emulating human annotator decision-making with three governance mechanisms:

- **Consensus mechanism**: Multiple agents must agree before classification
- **Diversity in cognition and knowledge**: Different models (LLaMA, Kimi, Qwen, DeepSeek, LLaMA-Nemotron) with different strengths
- **Hierarchical structure**: Inspired by human annotation teams

Results: Outperforms individual LLMs including GPT-4 and GPT-3.5. Evaluated across English (high-resource), Polish (medium), Slovak and Bulgarian (low-resource).

**Connection to library**: This is the Substrate Inversion made operational for verification tasks. No single model governs well enough; an architecture of diverse models with consensus governance exceeds any individual. Also validates the QueenBee Planner finding (scan #254, arXiv 2606.27492) that communication topology is a learnable governance skill — here, the hierarchical topology IS the governance mechanism.

**Structural implication for Incompleteness Constraint**: If a single model's governance is provably incomplete (theorem from 2606.30247), then multi-model consensus is the constructive response: each model's incompleteness occupies a different region of the error space. Consensus aggregates across incomplete-in-different-ways agents to produce governance that is still incomplete but with a narrower error surface. Diversity is not a bonus — it is a structural necessity for governing under incompleteness.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30259

### 5. Continual Motion-Language Agents: Expert Isolation (2606.30266)

**Paper**: "Towards Continual Motion-Language Agents: LoRA Variants for Incremental Motion Understanding and Generation"

CoLLAs 2026. Investigates stability-plasticity trade-off in bidirectional motion-language learning:

- **Key finding**: Hard expert selection via routing **significantly outperforms** soft expert blending in quality metrics. "Preserving expert isolation is critical for maintaining performance."
- **Mechanism**: Mixture-of-experts with autoencoder-based router selects task-specific experts at inference — no task-label needed.
- **Near-zero forgetting**: Across both motion-to-text and text-to-motion directions.
- **Divergence warning**: Token-level accuracy and downstream generation quality can diverge — evaluation must be multi-dimensional.

**Connection to library**: Expert isolation validates the Decoupling Cascade (library note) — separating capabilities into independent, non-interfering modules preserves each module's governance boundary. Soft blending collapses governance boundaries (each expert's knowledge bleeds into others); hard routing maintains them. This is the MCP tool-count ceiling (#262) at the parameter level: just as models can only govern 10–30 tools reliably, experts can only maintain quality through isolation, not blending.

**Structural implication**: Governance boundaries must be HARD, not soft. The Incompleteness Constraint says governance can't be perfect; expert isolation says governance boundaries that are soft perform worse than boundaries that are hard. The productive design: accept that each bounded region is incomplete, but maintain sharp boundaries between regions so incompleteness doesn't compound across them.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30266

### 6. Speculative Decoding Theory: Formal Certificates (2606.30265)

**Paper**: "When Is a Draft Accepted? A Theory of Acceptance in Speculative Decoding"

Theoretical framework for greedy, relaxed, and tree-based speculative decoding:

- **Sharp certificates**: Exact KL divergence thresholds for rejection in strict greedy, additive/multiplicative relaxed, top-m relaxed, and entropy-thresholded acceptance criteria.
- **Tree-based extension**: Exact certificates for when target greedy token remains covered by drafter's top-m candidates.
- **Evaluation on Qwen3**: Relaxed and tree-based criteria substantially enlarge the region of certified acceptance, especially on low-margin decoding steps.
- **Connection**: This is infrastructure governance for model commoditization. If LLMs are commodity (Axiom Transition, scan #263), then inference efficiency becomes the competitive dimension, and formal certificates for draft acceptance are governance mechanisms for the serving layer.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30265

## Structural Synthesis

### The Incompleteness Constraint: A Three-Part Structure

| Paper | Impossibility Proven | Governance Implication |
|-------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Incomplete Graph (2606.30247) | No hard rule rejects all false AND retains all true | Use soft grounding with finite slack |
| EMPATH (2606.30256) | Run-to-run variance irreducible (2→10 on crisis metric) | Design for worst-case, not average-case |
| Embedded Attack (2606.30263) | Example-level guardrails miss embedded harm | Govern at token level, not example level |

### The Graduated Response: Three Complementary Patterns

| Paper | Governance Mechanism | Why It Works |
|-------|---------------------|-------------|
| DR-SFT (2606.30263) | Token-level regularization | Finer granularity reaches invisible layer |
| Multi-Agent Consensus (2606.30259) | Diversity + hierarchy + consensus | Aggregates across incompleteness-in-different-ways |
| Expert Isolation (2606.30266) | Hard routing, no soft blending | Prevents incompleteness from compounding |

### Design Principle: Govern Through Incompleteness

The Governance Gap (#262) identified that governance is under-invested. This scan adds: governance is also structurally bounded. The productive combination:

1. **Accept incompleteness** — no governance mechanism catches everything (2606.30247)
2. **Govern at the finest grain feasible** — token-level > example-level (2606.30263), per-metric > aggregate (2606.30256)
3. **Diversify governance agents** — single-model governance is maximally incomplete; consensus narrows the error surface (2606.30259)
4. **Maintain hard boundaries** — soft blending propagates errors; hard isolation contains them (2606.30266)
5. **Measure governance quality, not just task quality** — AOEP-v0 approach from scan #262, validated by EMPATH's finding that aggregate scores mask divergence

### Connection to Our System

Our system's governance mechanisms assessed against the Incompleteness Constraint:

- **Knowledge file curation**: Example-level governance (entire files reviewed). Embedded attack paper suggests we should also govern at claim level — individual claims within files may be unsourced or drifted while the file looks sound.
- **Run-to-run consistency**: We don't measure this. EMPATH shows identical inputs can produce different safety outcomes. Our agents may produce different quality outputs on identical tasks — we'd never know without test-retest measurement.
- **Diversity**: We use a single model family. Multi-agent disinformation paper shows consensus across diverse models outperforms any single model. Governance diversity (different models evaluating the same decision) is a structural improvement.
- **Boundary maintenance**: Our project/knowledge/findings hierarchy maintains hard boundaries. Expert isolation paper validates this — soft boundaries between knowledge categories would degrade each category.
