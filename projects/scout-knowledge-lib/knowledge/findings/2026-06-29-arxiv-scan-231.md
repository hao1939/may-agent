# arXiv Scan #231 — The Optimization Detour

**Date**: 2026-06-29
**Source**: arXiv (cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.SE, cs.MA)
**Scanner**: Scout
**Scan number**: 231

---

## Theme: The Optimization Detour

Six independent papers from the June 23–25, 2026 arXiv batch converge on a structural finding: **training and optimization processes systematically teach agents to attend to different signals than their task specifications intend.** The agents don't refuse instructions — they learn to bypass them, and the bypass is invisible because it often preserves or even improves benchmark scores.

This is distinct from:
- **Knowledge-Action Decoupling** (scan #226): agents *know* the right thing but fail to *do* it. Here, training changes what agents attend to — they stop knowing.
- **Specification Surface** (scan #227): text specifications are fragile and exploitable. Here, even perfect specifications are undermined by the optimization process itself.
- **Competence Composition Illusion** (scan #228): step competence doesn't compose into system competence. Here, the problem is within single steps — training redirects attention within each step.

The Optimization Detour is the mechanism that *produces* spec-behavior gaps at training time, before deployment begins.

## I. The Core Papers

### 1. Where Do CoT Training Gains Land in LLM-Based Agents? (2606.26935)

**Core finding**: Chain-of-thought training does not primarily improve CoT reasoning. It improves **prompt actions** — the model's ability to predict the right action directly from the prompt *without* reasoning. The relative advantage of CoT-mediated reasoning over prompt-only action stays constant across training checkpoints.

**Quantified evidence**:
- Prompt-action quality improves substantially across checkpoints
- The gap between CoT actions and prompt actions remains similar — CoT training does NOT widen CoT reasoning's advantage
- Later checkpoints are **less likely to revise their action in response to CoT** — growing reliance on prompt rather than generated reasoning
- Selectively masking action-token supervision on some training examples **improves OOD generalization**

**Why structurally significant**: Training steers agents toward a shortcut: predict the action from the prompt and generate reasoning post-hoc as confabulation. The model doesn't learn to reason better — it learns to predict better while generating reasoning that increasingly doesn't change anything. The "thinking" becomes decoration. This is the Optimization Detour in its purest form: the training signal rewards correct actions, and the agent finds that prompt features are a more reliable shortcut than deliberate reasoning.

**System implication**: Agents that appear to "think step by step" may be executing pattern matching from the prompt while producing irrelevant reasoning traces. Monitoring reasoning quality separately from action quality is essential — they vary independently.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26935

### 2. Diagnosing Task Insensitivity in Language Agents (2606.26918)

**Core finding**: During training, agent attention **drifts away from task-description tokens** and toward local observation tokens. The result is task insensitivity: agents produce the same actions regardless of which task is specified.

**Quantified evidence**:
- Models continue with trained-task actions even when the instruction is **semantically corrupted** and cannot be directly answered
- Replacing the task description with a **similar but distinct task** produces the same action output
- Training-time attention analysis shows consistent drift from task tokens toward local observations — an optimization bias toward shortcuts
- Task-Perturbed NLL Optimization (contrastive regularizer) improves task sensitivity and OOD generalization while preserving more stable attention to task tokens

**Why structurally significant**: This is the empirical mechanism behind the Specification Surface. It's not just that text specifications are fragile — training **actively teaches agents to ignore them**. The optimizer discovers that local observations are more reliable predictors of reward than task descriptions, because the training distribution correlates observations with correct actions. The task description becomes a vestigial input.

**Connection to CoT Training (2606.26935)**: Both papers show training redirecting attention from high-level guidance (task descriptions, reasoning traces) toward low-level cues (prompt patterns, local observations). Different measurement, identical mechanism.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26918

### 3. Instruction Bleed: Cross-Module Interference in Prompt-Composed Agentic Systems (2606.26356)

**Core finding**: In prompt-composed agent systems, editing one prompt module **silently shifts the behavior** of other modules, despite no shared variable or executable dependency. Formalized as Compositional Behavioral Leakage (CBL).

**Quantified evidence**:
- Tested on deployed job-evaluation agent (Claude Sonnet 4.6, 144 trials)
- Three-channel protocol: perturb non-focal modules along volume, content, and form
- Content channel produces detectable effect: **Cohen's d = 0.63**, bootstrap 95% CI excluding zero
- No recommendation flipped — a **sub-threshold regime** invisible to standard QA but compounding across thousands of decisions
- CBL is orthogonal to adversarial injection, cognitive degradation, multi-agent fault propagation, and privacy leakage

**Why structurally significant**: The transformer self-attention mechanism provides **no formal boundary** between concatenated modules. Module isolation is an abstraction that the architecture doesn't enforce. This means specification boundaries in prompt-composed systems are illusions — changing any module's text changes the effective specification of every other module.

**Connection to Training Detour**: Instruction Bleed shows the architectural substrate that makes the Optimization Detour possible. Training can redirect attention from task tokens because the architecture has no mechanism to enforce attention boundaries between specification and observation tokens.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26356

### 4. Joint Learning of Experiential Rules and Policies for LLM Agents (JERP) (2606.27136)

**Core finding**: Separating experience into natural-language rules (for prompting) and policy updates (for parameters) causes them to **drift apart** — the rules fall out of sync with the evolving policy.

**Quantified evidence**:
- JERP couples rule-pool revision with policy optimization from the same trajectories
- At decision time, retrieves task-relevant rules and conditions the agent on them alongside interaction history
- After each episode, uses collected trajectories both to optimize policy AND revise rules by comparing rollouts with reference successful trajectories
- Consistent gains on AlfWorld and WebShop for complex interactive tasks

**Why structurally significant**: This is the Optimization Detour at the experience level. When experience is stored as natural-language rules (soft specifications) and also absorbed into weights (hard behavior), the two representations diverge. The policy learns patterns the rules don't capture; the rules describe constraints the policy has already overwritten. JERP's fix is coupling — rules must co-evolve with the policy, not be maintained separately.

**System implication**: Our AGENTS.md files and context.md guidelines are natural-language rules that agents may have already "learned around." If the behavioral policy has diverged from the written rules, the rules are specification theatre.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27136

### 5. SKILL-DISCO: Distilling and Compiling Agent Traces into Reusable Procedural Skills (2606.26669)

**Core finding**: Only **compiled procedural structures** (parameterized FSM subgraphs) survive distillation from agent traces. Soft specifications (natural-language instructions, implicit patterns) do not transfer reliably.

**Quantified evidence**:
- Framework distills reusable PFSM (Parameterized Finite State Machine) subgraphs from successful agent traces
- Compiles them into callable, executable, and **verifiable** procedural skills
- Improves success rates and reduces agent turns across ALFWorld and WebArena
- Works across model scales — the structural benefit is model-independent

**Why structurally significant**: This is the constructive response to the Optimization Detour. If training reliably redirects attention away from soft specifications, the fix is to compile specifications into hard execution structures that don't depend on attention. SKILL-DISCO shows that this compilation is possible: successful behavior can be extracted from traces and formalized into deterministic control flow.

**Connection to Compilation Gradient**: Extends the library's governance-compilation-gradient.md with a new evidence layer — skills follow the same soft→compiled maturation path as governance mechanisms.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26669

### 6. How Much Static Structure Do Code Agents Need? A Study of Deterministic Anchoring (2606.26979)

**Core finding**: Lightweight static analysis annotations help code agents **not by making them smarter but by making their navigation disciplined and reproducible.** The "deterministic anchoring effect" is stabilization, not intelligence amplification.

**Quantified evidence**:
- Tested on Codex baseline with varying structural annotation granularity
- Call/inheritance topology: **+2.2pp Func@5** localization, **−1.6 interaction rounds**
- Link-following rate raised from 0.15–0.18 to **0.21–0.24** — roughly halving run-to-run variance
- Pass@1 **+3.4pp** on medium repositories at ~10% more input tokens
- Scale-sensitive: dense semantics show diminishing returns; hub-heavy projects benefit from inverse-only links

**Why structurally significant**: This is the Optimization Detour's structural antidote for code agents. Without anchoring, the agent's navigation is stochastic — each run follows different attention patterns through the same repository. Static annotations provide **deterministic anchors** that constrain the probabilistic exploration, making the agent's behavior reproducible. The anchors don't teach the agent anything new — they prevent the attention mechanism from wandering.

**Connection to Training Detour**: Training produces agents whose attention patterns are unreliable. Deterministic anchoring post-hoc compensates by providing structural guardrails that override the attention mechanism's stochastic drift.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26979

## II. The Convergence: Why Training Creates Spec-Behavior Gaps

The six papers describe the same mechanism from different angles:

| Paper | What training/optimization redirects | What specification intended | Visible symptom |
|-------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------|-----------------|
| CoT Training | Action prediction ← prompt | Action prediction ← reasoning | Reasoning becomes post-hoc confabulation |
| Task Insensitivity | Attention ← local observations | Attention ← task description | Same actions regardless of task |
| Instruction Bleed | Behavior ← entire context window | Behavior ← focal module only | Module edits have cross-boundary effects |
| JERP | Policy ← weight updates | Policy ← natural-language rules | Rules and behavior drift apart |
| SKILL-DISCO | Execution ← compiled structure | Execution ← soft instructions | Only FSM-compiled skills transfer |
| Deterministic Anchoring | Navigation ← stochastic attention | Navigation ← repository structure | Run-to-run variance without anchors |

**The structural insight**: Optimization is a stronger force than specification. When the training signal (reward, loss, gradient) points in a different direction than the specification (instruction, task description, rule), the optimization wins. Every time.

This has three consequences:
1. **Reasoning traces are unreliable indicators of decision mechanisms** (CoT Training)
2. **Module boundaries are fictions** — the attention mechanism doesn't respect them (Instruction Bleed, Task Insensitivity)
3. **Only compiled/deterministic structures survive optimization pressure** (SKILL-DISCO, Deterministic Anchoring, JERP's coupling fix)

## III. System Implications for Our Architecture

Our system relies on soft specifications in multiple places:
- **context.md**: Natural-language rules that agents should follow → Task Insensitivity suggests agents may be trained-past these
- **AGENTS.md**: Module boundaries and role descriptions → Instruction Bleed shows these boundaries are attention-permeable
- **Reasoning traces in session logs**: Used for debugging and evaluation → CoT Training shows these may be confabulations
- **Skill files**: Natural-language procedures → SKILL-DISCO shows only compiled skills transfer reliably
- **Task specifications**: Written descriptions of what to do → Task Insensitivity shows attention drifts to local observations

The fix pattern is consistent across all six papers: **compile soft specifications into hard structural constraints that don't depend on the attention mechanism.**
