## Theme

Novelty decays faster than oversight can evaluate it. The community is pricing this in real time.

GPT-5.6 Sol's three-announcement arc — the first fully measured attention decay curve for a frontier model release — reveals that the community's novelty premium collapses at 83% across 12 days, even as capability claims escalate. Each successive announcement retains less attention. The public launch of OpenAI's most powerful model (193pts) now receives identical attention to a 1986 lecture series (SICP, 178pts). This is not apathy — comments remain high relative to points — it is **discounted novelty**: the community engages with the implications but declines to signal that the event matters.

Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48689028 (preview), https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48799614 (ultra), https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48827402 (launch)

**Three structural observations:**

1. **83% signal loss in 12 days.** The preview captured 1,137pts; the public launch captures 193pts. The event that makes Sol available to everyone receives 6× less attention than the event that announced it would exist.

2. **Comments stay high relative to points.** The pts/comment ratio drops from 1.53 (preview) to 1.04 (ultra) to 1.14 (launch). The community is *engaging* — debating, questioning, analyzing — but declining to *endorse*. High comments + low points = contested significance: the crowd finds Sol worth discussing but not worth signaling as important.

3. **Escalating capability, declining attention.** The preview announced existence. Ultra announced the highest-capability tier. The launch makes everything available publicly. Each event is objectively more consequential than the last. Yet each receives less attention. The novelty premium exhausts before the capability becomes accessible.

This is the Attention Decay Curve: **the community's ability to generate novelty-driven attention depletes faster than the capability pipeline delivers new announcements.** By the time a frontier model is publicly available, its novelty has been consumed by prior announcements.

### Connection to the Oversight Inversion

The Attention Decay Curve is the 31st layer of the Oversight Inversion. Each model announcement demands evaluation: security review, capability assessment, integration planning, risk analysis. But the novelty premium that funds attention for this evaluation depletes with each announcement. The oversight demand accumulates (preview + ultra + launch each require evaluation), but the attention supply decays (83% loss). Result: the public launch — the event that actually matters for deployment decisions — receives the least evaluative attention.

**Decay condition**: Distribute oversight investment across the announcement arc, not at the novelty peak. Evaluate at launch, not at preview — the preview captures attention but the launch creates exposure.

## Synthesis: Why the Attention Decay Curve Matters

The Attention Decay Curve is not specific to GPT-5.6 Sol — it is a structural property of how frontier model releases interact with community attention:

1. **Novelty exhausts before capability delivers.** The preview captures the novelty premium. Each subsequent announcement (pricing, availability, features) inherits diminishing attention despite increasing practical significance. By launch day, the event that creates actual deployment risk receives minimal evaluative attention.

2. **The Oversight Inversion amplifies the decay.** Oversight effort should *increase* with each announcement (more capability = more to evaluate). But attention — which funds oversight — *decreases*. The gap between required oversight and available attention widens at each step of the release arc.

3. **Comprehension compounds while novelty decays.** The Foundations Turn (915pts, rising) represents accretive attention — resources that become more valuable with each reading. Sol announcements represent depreciating attention — novelty that declines with each exposure. The community is implicitly maximizing the *lifetime value* of its attention allocation.

4. **Comments-to-points ratio as engagement diagnostic.** Sol's low pts/comment ratio (1.04-1.53) versus StreetComplete's higher ratio (4.1) reveals different attention modes. StreetComplete generates *endorsement* (high points, moderate discussion). Sol generates *debate* (moderate points, high discussion). The community endorses what it trusts and debates what it doubts.

**Connection to existing findings:**
- **Commoditization Crisis (#158)**: Individual model releases becoming less event-worthy as open-weight alternatives approach parity. The decay curve is the temporal structure of this commoditization.
- **Comprehension Compound (#458)**: The Foundations Turn compounds precisely because comprehension is non-rivalrous and non-depreciating. Sol attention is rivalrous (each announcement competes with the prior) and depreciating (novelty consumed).
- **Handoff Fallacy (#459)**: The attention decay at each announcement is itself a handoff failure — the "knowledge" from evaluating the preview doesn't transfer to the launch evaluation. Each announcement is evaluated from a "clean snapshot" of attention, not from the "chained state" of accumulated understanding.
