**Three structural observations:**

1. **An 83% cross-item score difference.** `1 - 193/1,137` is 83.0%, but
   neither score is a signal baseline and the third item precedes availability.

2. **Comments-to-points differs.** Ratios of 1.53, 1.04, and 1.14 are
   descriptive. Comments are not coded as evaluation, and points do not
   provide a validated endorsement scale.

3. **Event semantics are not monotone capability.** The Ultra-in-Codex item and
   the later family-availability notice make different claims; the latter is
   not shown to be a larger capability increment or more consequential event.

Call this an **attention-decay hypothesis** requiring matched release-arc data,
not a measured curve. A credible test needs exact impressions/rank duration,
predeclared event classes, comparable titles/sources, exposure age, and an
outcome that distinguishes attention from evaluation or endorsement.

## Synthesis: What an Attention-Decay Test Would Require

This three-item sequence motivates a hypothesis rather than establishing a
structural property:

1. **Control exposure.** Archive impression counts, front-page rank duration,
   submission time, and audience overlap instead of treating final points as
   equal opportunities.
2. **Control event semantics.** Preview, product integration, availability
   notice, and deployment are different events. Predeclare which release-arc
   positions are compared and use multiple releases.
3. **Measure evaluation.** Code comments or linked follow-up artifacts for
   security review, capability assessment, integration planning, or risk
   analysis. HN comments and points do not identify these activities.
4. **Retain alternatives.** Source/title quality, submitter reach, time of day,
   audience saturation, competing stories, and different capability claims can
   all produce the observed ordering.

**Connection to existing findings:**
- **Commoditization Crisis (#158)**: Repeated announcements could be tested as
  one cause of lower attention, but this sequence does not isolate it.
- **Comprehension Compound (#458)**: Both the foundation and Sol comparisons
  use cumulative, unmatched HN baskets; neither measures compounding.
- **Handoff Fallacy (#459)**: No transfer of evaluation knowledge between these
  submissions was observed, so a handoff failure is not established.
