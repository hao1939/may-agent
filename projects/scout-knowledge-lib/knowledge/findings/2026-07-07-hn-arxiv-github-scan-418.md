## Theme: Human Capital Is the Binding Constraint on Human-AI Collaboration

Scan #416 proved reasoning effort — not tool access — is the binding constraint for agent reliability. Scan #418 asks the deeper question: **whose reasoning?**

arXiv paper 2607.02467 ("Human Capital, Not Model Benchmarks, Predicts Hybrid Intelligence in Forecasting") answers with large-scale evidence from real-money prediction markets: human-AI collaboration performance is **trimodal**:

1. **Deferrers** (majority): Accept model output uncritically → match model performance exactly
2. **Rubber-stampers** (significant minority): Use AI to confirm pre-existing beliefs → perform *worse* than model alone
3. **Complementary reasoners** (small minority): Engage in genuine collaborative reasoning → match or *exceed* market accuracy

The distinguishing factor between modes is **not** raw cognitive ability or model benchmark scores. It is a specific cluster of **collaborative traits**: perspective-taking, intellectual humility, and curiosity. These are measurable, trainable human capabilities.

**Implication for the Oversight Inversion**: The oversight bottleneck is not just economic (can't afford enough reviewers) or temporal (can't review fast enough). It is a **human capital gate** — most humans lack the specific traits required for productive AI oversight. This resolves the puzzle of why adding more reviewers doesn't linearly improve oversight quality. The gate is qualitative, not quantitative.

## arXiv: The Human Gate

### Human Capital, Not Model Benchmarks (2607.02467, CRITICAL)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02467

Using Polymarket (real-money prediction market) as an objective, externally resolved benchmark, this paper proves:

- **Trimodal distribution**: Human-AI collaboration doesn't produce a normal distribution of outcomes. It produces three distinct modes — deference, rubber-stamping, and complementary reasoning.
- **Trait gating**: The boundary between modes is determined by collaborative traits (perspective-taking, intellectual humility, curiosity), NOT by IQ, domain expertise, or model quality.
- **Complementary mode**: The minority who reach complementary reasoning achieve accuracy matching or exceeding the prediction market itself — genuine super-human performance.
- **Rubber-stamp trap**: Most dangerous mode — users who believe they're improving AI output but are actually degrading it by confirming their priors.

**Design principle**: Before scaling human-AI oversight, measure and select for collaborative traits. Adding reviewers without trait-gating increases the rubber-stamp population, degrading aggregate oversight quality. This explains why the Oversight Inversion can't be solved by hiring more reviewers.

**Connection to Oversight Inversion thesis**: The oversight bottleneck has three layers:
1. Economic (Cost Inversion Threshold, #404): Can't afford enough reviewers
2. Temporal (Oversight Inversion, #382): Can't review fast enough  
3. **Qualitative (Human Gate, this scan)**: Most reviewers lack the traits for productive oversight

### Online Safety Monitoring (2607.02510)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02510

Simple real-time monitor using external verifier signal + calibrated threshold is **competitive with** more complex sequential hypothesis testing monitors. Design implication: safety monitoring doesn't need complex architecture — a well-calibrated threshold on a simple signal works. Aligns with the Verification Stack's (#412) finding that verification at the right layer beats complexity at the wrong layer.

### DemoPSD: Disagreement-Modulated Self-Distillation (2607.02502)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02502

On-policy self-distillation with dense token-level supervision leads to overfitting and suppressed exploration. DemoPSD modulates supervision by disagreement between teacher and student — less supervision where disagreement is high (exploration zones). Structural insight: uniform supervision is a form of the Plausibility Gradient (#390) — the teacher looks right everywhere, but is right only in agreement zones.

## Structural Analysis

### The Human Gate as Oversight Resolution

The Oversight Inversion (#382) identified supervision as the bottleneck. Subsequent scans identified:
- **What** to oversee: behavioral integrity (Fable 5, Plausibility Gradient #390)
- **How** to oversee: layered verification (Verification Stack #412), formal methods (Kani)
- **Whether** oversight can scale: reasoning effort is the constraint (Reasoning Precedence #416)

This scan adds the missing piece: **Who** can oversee. The answer from 2607.02467 is structurally uncomfortable — most humans cannot productively oversee AI. The gate is specific collaborative traits, not general competence.

This creates a recursive problem:
```
Oversight requires human capital (perspective-taking, humility, curiosity)
Human capital is scarce and unevenly distributed
Therefore: oversight quality has a population ceiling
The Oversight Inversion is not just a scaling problem — it's a talent problem
```

The community's sovereignty preference (#394-#417) can be reinterpreted through this lens: sovereignty (local control, verifiable substrates, open hardware) is the **architectural bypass** of the Human Gate. If you can't scale qualified oversight, you reduce the surface area requiring oversight. You verify the substrate (OpenWrt, Kani), constrain the behavior (Fable 5 as test), and own the infrastructure (CoMaps, Nintendo batteries).
