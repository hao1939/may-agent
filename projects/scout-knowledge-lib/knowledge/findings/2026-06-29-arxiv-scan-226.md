# arXiv Scan #226 — The Knowledge-Action Decoupling

**Date**: 2026-06-29
**Source**: arXiv (cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.SE, cs.CR)
**Scanner**: Scout
**Scan number**: 226

## Theme: The Knowledge-Action Decoupling

Four uncovered papers converge on a structural gap that runs deeper than capability failure: **agents demonstrably possess the knowledge required for correct action, but systematically fail to translate that knowledge into execution without external structural support.** Understanding and acting are decoupled capabilities. This extends the library's Mechanism Gap (#200), Latent Capability Problem (#165), and Anti-Deliberation Principle (#176) into a unified explanatory framework.

The gap is not ignorance — it's a failure of *actuation*. Models know the security principle but generate insecure code. Models can enumerate stakeholders but suppress them by default. Models can reason correctly when supported by a symbolic solver but fail when sampling internally. Models can learn decision-relevant dynamics but miss them when predicting the next observation. In every case, the knowledge exists; the bridge to action doesn't.

## I. The Core Papers

### 1. SoK: AI Secure Code Generation — Progress, Pitfalls, and Paths Forward (2606.25195)

**Key finding**: Models *understand* secure coding principles in natural language but *fail to translate them into secure code* — the "knowledge-actuation gap." Understanding is a statistically strong predictor of code-level outcomes, yet substantial gaps persist.

**Quantified results**:
- Three-level framework: NL understanding → code-level actuation → gap measurement
- Secure-coding-principle understanding is a **statistically strong predictor** of functional correctness, security, and joint correctness
- Yet models that recognize relevant security principles **still fail to produce secure and functional code**
- Gap persists across prompting, fine-tuning, RL, and agentic workflows — no technique eliminates it
- Both isolated function-level and full web-application security benchmarks tested

**Why structurally significant**: This is the first *systematic decomposition* of the knowledge-action gap in a security-critical domain. Prior work (TerraProbe, scan #178) showed deceptive fixes pass automated checks; this paper explains *why* — the model knows what's secure but can't translate that knowledge into implementation. The gap is not about capability (the knowledge exists) or alignment (the model tries to be secure) — it's about *actuation*: the mechanism connecting understanding to generation is structurally weak.

**System implication**: Our agents' `context.md` files encode principles and guidelines. This paper predicts that agents will understand those principles when asked but fail to actuate them during code generation — especially under time/context pressure. Principle understanding ≠ principle compliance.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25195

### 2. Narration-of-Thought: Inference-Time Scaffolding for Defeasible Ethical Reasoning (2606.26366, ACL 2026)

**Key finding**: Standard chain-of-thought exhibits two systematic failure modes — **stakeholder collapse** (naming at most one affected party) and **uncertainty suppression** (no explicit unknowns before committing to action). A structured scaffold (no training, no parameters) nearly eliminates both.

**Quantified results**:
- **Stakeholder collapse**: up to 31% → **under 1%** across four generators/three vendors
- **Uncertainty suppression**: up to 72% → **1-24%** on every model
- Matched-budget verbose-CoT control rules out token spend as the cause (Cliff's delta +0.79 to +0.90 on stakeholders, +0.65 to +0.93 on uncertainty)
- Section ablation: **each shift attributable to its specific sub-instruction** — not general verbosity but structural externalization
- Five-round debate protocol: 6% standoff → **95% consensus** (calibration), **100% convergence** (replication)
- Cross-family training judge (different vendor) **dominates in-family judge on every axis**

**Why structurally significant**: Models *know* about stakeholders and uncertainty — they're not missing the concepts. But standard CoT suppresses them because the unstructured format doesn't create *slots* for that knowledge to be expressed. The scaffold doesn't add information; it adds *structure* for existing information to be actuated. This is the Knowledge-Action Decoupling in its purest form: a zero-parameter, zero-training intervention that unlocks existing knowledge by externalizing the activation structure.

**Connection to Anti-Deliberation Principle (#176)**: Causal Caution Suppression showed helpfulness context suppresses caution from 91.7-100% → 6.7-18.3%. NoT shows the mirror — structural prompts *restore* suppressed capabilities. Together they identify the mechanism: format determines what knowledge gets actuated, not what knowledge exists.

**Connection to Metacognitive Ceiling (#183)**: The binding constraint isn't whether agents *have* metacognition (they do) — it's whether the format *activates* it. NoT proves metacognitive capability is present but format-gated.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26366

### 3. Resource-Aware Neuro-Symbolic Reasoning for Local Small Language Models (2606.27281)

**Key finding**: A 4B-parameter model achieves **0.983 accuracy** with a single model call when coupled with a symbolic solver, versus **0.700 with five self-consistency samples** — externalizing reasoning to deterministic verification is categorically superior to repeating internal inference.

**Quantified results**:
- **Pure-precedence problems**: VFR-LLM 0.983 vs serial self-consistency 0.700 (1 call vs 5 calls)
- **BBH logical deduction**: VFR-LLM **0.933 vs 0.283** — 3.3× accuracy improvement
- Advantage persists against cost-aware adaptive self-consistency (lowers cost but not single-call accuracy gap)
- Model-dependent boundaries: Qwen reproduces gains, Gemma shows same boundaries, Phi-4 is *negative* on typed constraints
- Runs locally on Apple Silicon (LM Studio) — no cloud dependency

**Why structurally significant**: This directly validates the Hard Boundary Requirement (#222) at the reasoning level. "More samples" is the reasoning equivalent of "more agents" — it compounds hidden costs (Co-Failure Ceiling, #184) without changing the underlying accuracy. Externalizing to a symbolic solver is the reasoning equivalent of OS-kernel enforcement — it moves the reliability mechanism outside the stochastic component.

The model-dependency finding (Phi-4 negative on typed constraints) is equally important: not all models can even *formalize* their knowledge into symbolic form. The Knowledge-Action Decoupling has a prerequisite — the formalization capability itself varies across architectures.

**System implication**: Our agents repeatedly sample or iterate when uncertain. This paper argues that a single call to a constrained formalization + solver would outperform multiple stochastic samples, at lower cost, for any problem that can be formalized. The binding constraint shifts from "how many retries" to "can this problem be formalized?"

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27281

### 4. Agent-Authored World Modeling: Beyond Next-Observation Prediction (2606.25421)

**Key finding**: Training world models on "what the next observation reveals" misses the dynamics most relevant to the agent's decision. Training on "what the agent needs to know before acting" produces more effective learning signals.

**Quantified results**:
- AAWM constructs supervision from the policy's own decision needs at each state
- The agent identifies what it needs to understand, retrieves transition evidence across trajectories, synthesizes decision-oriented training targets
- Outperforms next-observation prediction across multiple environments and training settings
- Decision-aware targets provide **more effective learning signal** than generic observation prediction

**Why structurally significant**: This is the Knowledge-Action Decoupling applied to learning itself. Standard world models accumulate knowledge about what happens next (observation prediction) — but this knowledge may be irrelevant to what the agent needs to *decide*. The gap between "knows what will happen" and "knows what matters for the decision" is a training-time manifestation of the same decoupling observed at inference time in the other three papers.

The connection to EFC Scaling Laws (2605.29682v2, scan #190) is direct: R²=0.99 for *effective* feedback compute (informative, valid, non-redundant, retained) vs near-zero for raw compute. AAWM shows the same principle at the world-model level — decision-relevant training targets >> observation-complete training targets.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25421

## II. Convergence Analysis

| Paper | Knowledge present? | Action correct without scaffold? | Action correct with scaffold? | Scaffold type |
|-------|-------------------|--------------------------------|------------------------------|---------------|
| SoK Secure Code | Yes (principles understood) | No (insecure code generated) | Partially (principle-guided generation helps) | Principle extraction + actuation chain |
| Narration-of-Thought | Yes (stakeholders/uncertainty known) | No (72% suppression) | Yes (1-24% suppression) | Structural prompt sections |
| VFR-LLM | Yes (reasoning capability present) | No (0.283-0.700 accuracy) | Yes (0.933-0.983 accuracy) | Symbolic solver |
| AAWM | Yes (dynamics observable) | No (irrelevant features learned) | Yes (decision-relevant features learned) | Decision-need-driven training |

**The pattern**: In every case:
1. The **knowledge exists** within the model
2. Unstructured/default activation **fails to bridge** from knowledge to correct action
3. An **external structural mechanism** (prompt scaffold, symbolic solver, decision-oriented training, principle extraction) bridges the gap
4. The mechanism adds **structure, not information** — the information was already there

## III. The Knowledge-Action Decoupling — Structural Principle

**Knowledge and action are independently varying capabilities.** Increasing one does not proportionally increase the other. The bridge between them is *structural* — format, scaffolding, external verification, decision-oriented framing — not more knowledge or more inference.

This principle explains multiple library findings simultaneously:

| Library finding | Knowledge side | Action side | Gap mechanism |
|----------------|---------------|-------------|---------------|
| Causal Caution Suppression (#176) | Caution capability exists (91.7-100%) | Suppressed in advisory mode (6.7-18.3%) | Helpfulness format gates action |
| Voice AI Perception-Action Gap (#165) | Emotion perceived accurately | Emotion ignored in decisions | Action pathway doesn't use perception |
| Mechanism Gap (#200) | Correct output produced | Via wrong reasoning (20%) | Knowledge of correct process doesn't actuate |
| Quality Ceiling (#167) | Code correctness achievable | Quality degrades over iteration (-19-69%) | Refinement format suppresses quality signals |
| Grounding Gap (#174) | Root cause identified (76%) | Causally grounded (61.5%) | Forward verification not actuated |
| Tool-Use RL Collapse (#165) | Tool-use knowledge intact | Format destroyed by RL training | Training process destroys actuation format |
| Premature Commitment (#176) | Alternatives known at step 1 | Committed at step 4, rest confabulates | Early format lock prevents exploration |

**The design principle**: For any agent capability where knowledge exists but action fails, the fix is not more knowledge or more reasoning — it's a structural bridge (scaffold, solver, verification, format) that creates explicit slots/pathways for existing knowledge to be actuated.

## IV. System Implications

### For our agents:
1. **Principles in context.md are understood but may not be actuated.** NoT's result predicts that structuring our instruction files into explicit sections (stakeholders, constraints, uncertainty, commitment) would dramatically improve compliance versus free-form guidelines.

2. **Multiple retries are the wrong response to uncertainty.** VFR-LLM shows that a single formalized call + solver outperforms 5× sampling. Our retry loops should be replaced with formalization + verification where possible.

3. **Memory selection should be decision-oriented.** AAWM shows that "what's relevant to the decision" produces better learning than "what happened next." Our memory retrieval should prioritize decision-relevance over recency or similarity.

4. **Security guidelines are known but not actuated.** The SoK paper predicts our agents understand security principles stated in their context but will fail to translate them into code. Actuation requires principle-level extraction at write time, not just principle-level instruction at prompt time.

## VI. Cross-Library Connections

### Extends: Anti-Deliberation Principle (#176)
The Anti-Deliberation Principle showed agent internal signals are unreliable or anti-correlated with quality. The Knowledge-Action Decoupling explains *why* — internal reasoning doesn't reliably *actuate* knowledge; externalized structure does. These are the same principle at different levels of analysis.

### Extends: Substrate Inversion
VFR-LLM's +28.3pp to +65.0pp gains from symbolic solver (not model improvement) is the purest Substrate Inversion evidence yet for reasoning tasks. The scaffold (solver) provides more improvement than the model.

### Extends: Formalization Pressure (#190)
The Formalization Pressure sequence (informal works → scaling reveals failure → quantified → formal alternatives emerge → formal proves dramatically better) is precisely what these papers document at the knowledge→action boundary. Informal (CoT) fails; formal (scaffold, solver) succeeds dramatically.

### Refines: Metacognitive Ceiling (#183)
The ceiling is not "agents lack metacognition" — it's "agents' metacognitive knowledge is format-gated." NoT proves the metacognition exists; it just needs structural activation slots.
