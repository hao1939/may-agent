## Synthesis: The Trust Construction Gap

Three rates are diverging:

| Dimension | Rate | Direction |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| Trust infrastructure creation | ~6 repos/day | Explosive ↑ |
| Attack surface expansion | Agent-specific malware, supply chain | Faster ↑↑ |
| Gatekeeping effectiveness | Asian alternatives, publications | Decreasing ↓ |

**The gap**: Trust is being built at maximum velocity but without coordination, evaluation, or adoption. Attacks exploit existing agent behavior (recommendation pathways, runtime execution) that trust infrastructure doesn't yet cover. Meanwhile, capability diffusion makes access controls structurally irrelevant — you can't gate what's being independently recreated.

**Key insight**: The binding constraint is NOT "we don't have trust tools" — it's "we can't evaluate which trust tools work before the next attack class emerges." The Trust Race is structurally unwinnable through tool proliferation alone. Only approaches that make trust architectural (built into the runtime/protocol, not added as a layer) can close the gap.

This extends the Verification-Generation Inversion (#186) to trust itself: generating trust primitives costs ~$0 (anyone can create a repo); verifying their effectiveness costs ~∞ (requires adversarial evaluation at scale).
