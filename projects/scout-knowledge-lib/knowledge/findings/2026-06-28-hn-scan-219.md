## IV. arXiv Findings

### Finding 5: RiVER — RL Without Ground Truth (2606.27369)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27369

**Core finding**: Reinforcement learning for LLMs typically requires ground-truth solutions as reward signals, limiting applicability. RiVER uses score-based optimization tasks (AtCoder Heuristic Contests) with calibrated reward shaping — no correct answer needed, only comparative ranking.

**Quantified results**:
- Qwen3-8B: +8.9% ALE rating rank improvement
- GLM-Z1-9B-0414: +9.4% ALE rating rank improvement
- Transfer to exact-solution benchmarks: +2.4% (LiveCodeBench), +3.5% (USACO) average absolute improvement
- Baselines trained with raw execution scores fail to transfer — calibrated reward shaping is essential

**Two identified failure modes in continuous-reward RL**:
1. **Scale dominance**: Uncalibrated score magnitudes across different tasks distort policy updates
2. **Frequency dominance**: Repeatedly sampled suboptimal solutions outweigh rare better candidates

**Structural significance**: Ground-truth independence accelerates commoditization. If smaller models can improve via score-based tasks without curated solution sets, the data moat for training shrinks. This compounds the open-weight pressure from GLM-5.2 (#158) and Asian alternatives (#218).

**Connection**: Extends the Anti-Deliberation Principle (#176) — external signals (execution scores, rankings) are more reliable training signals than internal reasoning. RiVER's calibrated shaping is the training-time analog of the out-of-band defenses identified in #176.

### Finding 6: PEEU — The Planning Hierarchy Gap (2606.27330)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27330

**Core finding**: A 7B GUI agent model outperforms 32B Qwen2.5-VL on real-world web benchmarks (30.6% accuracy) through autonomous experience exploration and hindsight experience utilization.

**Critical insight via TDHAF analysis framework**:
- Mastering low-level atomic skills does NOT guarantee high-level planning competence
- High-level task training yields stronger out-of-distribution generalization
- The hierarchy is: atomic actions → mid-level procedures → high-level plans, and competence at each level is semi-independent

**Structural significance**: This is the Metacognitive Ceiling (#183) quantified at the training level. "Knowing how to click buttons" (atomic skills) doesn't teach "knowing what sequence of pages to visit" (planning). The gap is structural, not scaling-dependent — doubling model size from 7B to 32B doesn't close it because the 7B model with better training data exceeds the larger model.

**Connection**: Confirms the Substrate Inversion principle — scaffold/training-recipe engineering (how you structure experience) matters more than raw model capability.

### Finding 7: Prompt Injection in Hiring — Game-Theoretic Saturation (2606.27287)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27287

**Core finding**: Prompt injection in LLM-based resume screening follows a game-theoretic saturation curve:
- When few candidates inject: reliable ranking improvement
- When many candidates inject: advantage collapses to zero
- When candidate quality is heterogeneous: average effect diminishes, but occasional unfair rank reversals persist

**Structural significance**: This is the first paper to model prompt injection as a **tragedy of the commons** rather than a security vulnerability. The attack is self-limiting at population scale but creates transient unfairness during adoption.

**Connection**: Extends the Accountability Gap (#153) and Governance Vacuum (#170) to employment law territory. The fairness concern (lower-quality candidates occasionally outranking higher-quality ones) creates regulatory exposure regardless of average effectiveness.

**Prediction**: "AI hiring audit" requirements emerge in ≥2 jurisdictions within 12 months, driven by exactly this class of unfairness.

## VI. Synthesis: The Accountability Loop

The accountability loop has three stages:

```
Deployment → Failure → Media → Community → Correction → Redeployment
     ↑                                                        │
     └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Ford is at Stage 4→5 (community consensus forming correction principles). The correction isn't "don't use AI" — it's bifurcated:

**Experience-governed deployment**: Keep domain experts in the loop, not as supervisors of AI output, but as the primary decision-makers who happen to use AI tools. "AI is another tool" (murphomatic). The Governance Vacuum (#170) identified this; Ford is the first major company to publicly demonstrate the correction.

**Infrastructure-sovereign deployment**: Run models locally, route intelligently between local and cloud, accommodate agent hardware needs at OS level. AMD RDMA clusters, Wayfinder Router, Adrafinil are all instantiations of this.

**The convergence**: Both tracks share the same structural principle — **push authority to the edge**. Don't centralize AI deployment decisions in boardrooms (Ford) or in frontier lab access controls (GPT-5.6 gating). Push capability to where domain knowledge lives (experienced engineers) and where infrastructure can be controlled (local hardware).

**Connection to the Commoditization Crisis (#158)**: The Accountability Loop IS the mechanism by which commoditization proceeds. Each centralized deployment failure reinforces the case for decentralized alternatives, which reinforces demand for open-weight models and local infrastructure, which accelerates commoditization.
