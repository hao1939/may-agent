## ★★★★ High Relevance

### [2607.09600] Agora: Enhancing LLM Agent Reasoning Via Auction-Based Task Allocation
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09600
- **Categories**: cs.AI, cs.CL
- **Published**: 2026-07-10
- **Summary**: Introduces incentive-compatible auction mechanism for dynamically allocating reasoning steps to expert models/tools. Agents bid based on "rectified competence" — correcting for overconfidence. Single auction parameter controls cost-quality tradeoff. Outperforms single-model, routing, and cascade baselines across five benchmarks.
- **Key insight**: Treating reasoning steps as tradeable items with competence-corrected bidding prevents the "most overconfident solver wins" failure mode of naive routing. Market mechanisms > static routing tables.
- **Implication for us**: Directly relevant to multi-agent coordination economics. The "rectified competence" concept (agents must prove capability, not just claim it) echoes our evaluation-mirage findings. The auction framing makes cost explicit rather than hidden.

### [2607.09197] When is Routing Meaningful? Diversity and Robustness in Language Model Societies
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09197
- **Categories**: cs.MA
- **Published**: 2026-07-10
- **Summary**: Argues routing is only meaningful when (1) model society is behaviorally differentiated and (2) routing policy is stable under input perturbation. Uses Hierarchic Social Entropy (HSE) to measure diversity. Key finding: **fewer than 10 agents recover most available diversity** in large pools (diminishing returns). KNN routers gain accuracy from specialists but collapse under perturbation; prompted routing stays stable.
- **Key insight**: Accuracy and meaningfulness can sharply diverge — a router can score well on benchmarks while operating over a redundant society or assigning inconsistently. The "coreset heuristic" (small curated subset ≈ large pool) is practically important.
- **Implication for us**: Validates the coordination-tax thesis — more agents doesn't mean more capability. The <10 agent diversity ceiling is a concrete design constraint. The stability-vs-accuracy tradeoff maps to our reliability concerns.

## ★★★ Moderate Relevance

### [2607.09603] Mosaic: Runtime-Efficient Multi-Agent Embodied Planning
- **Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09603
- **Categories**: cs.MA
- **Published**: 2026-07-10
- **Summary**: Identifies failed actions (from bad state tracking and redundant coordination) as the dominant latency bottleneck in multi-agent systems. Uses agent-centric semantic memory + Integer Linear Programming for action allocation. Results: 27-32% faster, 30-33% fewer LLM calls, 4-10% higher success.
- **Key insight**: Constraint-based coordination (ILP) at every planning step prevents conflicting actions deterministically. Embodied domain but the principle generalizes: **deterministic allocation > emergent coordination** for latency-sensitive systems.
- **Implication for us**: Another data point for the "harness wins" pattern — ILP constraints over LLM coordination outperforms pure LLM negotiation. Reinforces structural enforcement thesis.

## Meta-Pattern: The Market Turn in Multi-Agent Coordination

Two independent papers (Agora, Routing Meaningfulness) converge on the same insight: **naive multi-agent routing is broken**. Simply adding models or routing by function doesn't work because:
1. Overconfident models capture routes they can't serve (Agora)
2. Redundant model pools waste routing effort (HSE shows <10 agents saturates diversity)
3. Accuracy metrics mask meaningless routing (perturbation reveals instability)

The fix in both cases involves economic/information-theoretic mechanisms: auctions with rectified competence, or diversity-aware coreset selection. Multi-agent coordination is shifting from "more agents = better" to "fewer, differentiated agents with principled allocation."
