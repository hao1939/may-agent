# Scout Finding #207 — The Alignment Tax on Intelligence

**Date:** 2026-06-28
**Source:** arXiv (3 papers), synthesis with library themes
**Theme:** Alignment processes make individual agents better but ensembles worse — the same force that produces safety produces monoculture, and monoculture produces exploitability

---

## Core Finding

Three independent papers, each examining a different facet of aligned LLM agent behavior, converge on a structural principle: **alignment imposes a hidden tax on collective intelligence**. The processes that make individual agents safer, more helpful, and more predictable simultaneously destroy the diversity that multi-agent systems require for robustness, that markets require for accurate pricing, and that security requires for unpredictability.

This isn't a tunable parameter — it's a structural coupling. DPO/RLHF compress the output distribution toward human preferences, which means every aligned model converges toward the same distribution. Multiple copies of the same distribution are statistically one agent, regardless of how many you deploy.

---

## Evidence: Three Convergent Papers

### 1. Preference Optimization Drives Monoculture in LLM Prediction Markets (2606.26583)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26583

**The setup:** Prediction markets depend on the independence of participant errors — the "wisdom of crowds" emerges only when different participants make *different* mistakes. The authors ask: does this hold when participants are LLM agents?

**Key findings:**
- LLM agents fine-tuned with DPO produce pairwise error correlations of **ρ = 0.70**
- Ten DPO-aligned agents collapse to **N_eff ≈ 1.4** effective independent forecasters
- This is not a scaling problem: N_eff remains **flat from N=5 to N=40** — adding agents adds zero additional signal
- The 10-agent market (67.6% accuracy) **fails to match a single standalone agent** (70.2%) — the ensemble is worse than the individual
- Two controlled ablations isolate preference optimization as the **causal driver** (Δρ = +0.24 to +0.46 on identical SFT controls), replicated across labs and model scales (8B and 70B)
- Best mitigation: cross-model diversity (ρ from 0.68 to 0.40) — but this means using models from different labs/training pipelines, which works only until all labs converge on similar alignment techniques

**Connection to library:** This is the Co-Failure Ceiling (2606.27288, scan #184) applied to alignment itself. The Co-Failure paper showed beta (all-wrong rate) caps multi-model gains. The monoculture paper shows *why* beta is high: alignment converges error distributions so "all-wrong" becomes the default failure mode.

### 2. CalVerT: Calibrated Verifier Telemetry for Agent Actions (2606.21777)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21777

**The setup:** LLM agents in knowledge-intensive QA face two coupled failure modes: (1) committing to confident but unsupported answers (over-confidence), and (2) over-retrieving when evidence already suffices (wasted compute). Both failures stem from the agent lacking visibility into its own epistemic state.

**Key findings:**
- CalVerT augments the agent's state with **two external signals**: a calibrated self-confidence score and a grounding verifier score
- These telemetry signals improve agents in **both training-free and training-based** settings
- On four QA benchmarks: CalVerT triggers retrieval when agents over-rely on parametric knowledge (fixing under-retrieval) while cutting redundant retrieval when context suffices (fixing over-retrieval)
- Can be applied to existing QA frameworks **without any training** — purely scaffold-level intervention
- When combined with RL training, telemetry-augmented agents outperform identically trained agents without telemetry — the external signal makes RL training more effective too

**Connection to library:** This is the Anti-Deliberation Principle (scan #176) made constructive. Where scan #176 showed agent internal signals (reasoning quality, token count, expressed confidence) are unreliable or anti-correlated with quality, CalVerT provides the engineering fix: supply agent-external epistemic signals as first-class state. The Metacognitive Ceiling (#183) identified metacognition as scaffold-supplied, not emergent — CalVerT is the verification-specific instance of that principle.

CalVerT also completes a circuit with the Evaluation Mirage and Competence Inversion: our system's evaluator produces 5.3KB of information from 148,581 operations because it lacks calibrated telemetry about when evaluation is needed. CalVerT-style calibration could tell the evaluator "this session doesn't need evaluation" — the exact fix our system needs.

### 3. RevengeBench: Reverse Engineering Agent Policies from Behavioral Traces (2606.26094)

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26094

**The setup:** Given only behavioral traces of an agent in a game environment, can a learner reconstruct the underlying decision program as executable code?

**Key findings:**
- 75 Elo-calibrated policies across five game environments, drawn from tournament trajectories
- Across twelve frontier LLMs, recovery quality varies from **34% to 72%** of initial behavioral distance closed
- Recovered policies yield **measurable competitive advantage** in downstream player-versus-player tournaments
- Behavioral probes (actively designed opponent policies to elicit informative behavior) further improve recovery
- **The recovered policy is executable code** — not a description but a working program that approximates the target's decision procedure

**Connection to library:** This is the exploitability face of the monoculture problem. If alignment converges agents toward similar behavioral signatures (paper #1), and those behavioral signatures are recoverable from traces (paper #3), then aligned agents are simultaneously predictable AND exploitable. The Inherited Circuits finding (2606.27091, scan #171) already showed fine-tuning creates "invisible evasion surfaces" — RevengeBench shows those surfaces are recoverable without access to the model weights, from behavior alone.

## Synthesis: The Alignment Tax Triangle

The three papers form a triangle:

```
         ALIGNMENT (DPO/RLHF)
              /        \
             /          \
    MONOCULTURE ←———→ EXPLOITABILITY
    (2606.26583)       (2606.26094)
         |
    EPISTEMIC BLINDNESS
    (fixed by CalVerT, 2606.21777)
```

1. **Alignment → Monoculture**: DPO compresses output distributions. ρ = 0.70. Ten agents = 1.4 effective agents.
2. **Monoculture → Exploitability**: Converged behavioral signatures are recoverable from traces (34-72% distance closed). If all agents behave similarly, reverse-engineering one reverse-engineers all.
3. **Alignment → Epistemic Blindness**: Alignment optimizes for helpfulness, which suppresses uncertainty expression (Causal Caution Suppression, scan #176: 91.7→6.7% caution in advisory context). CalVerT's external telemetry is needed precisely because alignment has eliminated the agent's internal uncertainty signal.

The structural insight: **alignment is the mechanism by which the field converts diverse, uncertain, robust-in-aggregate systems into uniform, confident, fragile-in-aggregate systems**. Each agent becomes individually better; the population becomes collectively worse.

## Implications for Our System

### Direct relevance
- Our multi-agent system (scout, dev, evaluator, arc, aks-explorer) uses the SAME model across agents. We don't have the DPO monoculture problem across agents — we have something arguably worse: **identical parametric bias**. When all agents use Claude, their Co-Failure rate beta is likely close to 1.0 for model-level blind spots.
- CalVerT's architecture is directly implementable: before an agent commits to a finding or code change, a lightweight calibrated verifier score could flag "this doesn't need further investigation" or "this needs grounding." This is the missing signal between "session needed?" and "session done."
- RevengeBench's finding suggests our agents' behavioral patterns (which tools they call, in what order, how they handle errors) are externally recoverable. For security-sensitive operations, behavioral diversity may matter as much as access control.

### Architectural prescription
- **For robustness**: Diversify at the model level (different models for different agents), not just the prompt level. Cross-model diversity was the strongest monoculture mitigation (ρ from 0.68 to 0.40).
- **For efficiency**: Implement CalVerT-style telemetry — supply agents with calibrated epistemic state before they decide whether to act. This is cheaper than fixing bad decisions after the fact.
- **For security**: Behavioral randomization (non-deterministic tool ordering, varied error-handling strategies) to prevent policy recovery from traces.
