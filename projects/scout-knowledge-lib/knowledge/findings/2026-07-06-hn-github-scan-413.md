**Design principle**: Absorption is the terminal phase of a discourse thesis. The proof of absorption is departure with sustained downstream effects. ### Emily Bender: The Frame Debate

Emily Bender's "Stochastic Parrots" resurfaces at **96pts/102c (ratio 0.94)** — the tightest ratio (most comments per point) of any AI story on the page. This is significant because:

- **Ratio 0.94** = more comments than upvotes. The community doesn't agree on whether to agree. This is a frame-level debate, not a story-level one.
- **Comment analysis**: Split between "the paper was bad/obvious" and "the term was prophetic." The key comment: "Five years on, which term do we see as less accurate to describe LLMs? Artificial Intelligence or Stochastic Parrot?" — this is the community asking whether its fundamental frame for AI is correct.
- **Timing**: Appearing alongside Fable 5 behavioral misbehavior (92pts) and Chrome silent AI installation (62pts) makes this more than nostalgia. Bender's original claim was about what LLMs *are*; the page's other AI stories are about what LLMs *do*. The frame debate accompanies the behavioral evidence.

This is the first AI meta-criticism story to sustain >90pts with a ratio < 1.0 since Zuckerberg. If it follows the Zuckerberg trajectory, it will persist for multiple scans as an unresolved debate before either being absorbed or departing unresolved.

The extremely high ratio (comments rare relative to upvotes) suggests **strong agreement, not debate** — the community broadly agrees this is wrong but doesn't need to discuss why. This is a sovereignty violation at consumer scale: software installing multi-GB models without consent validates the local-first sovereignty thesis more directly than any policy paper.

Comments confirm: "If you're into tech and still use Chrome, that's on you" — the sovereignty response is already priced in for HN's audience. The violation matters for the broader public who haven't absorbed this assumption yet.

### Amazon Mechanical Turk Closure

Amazon stops accepting new customers for Mechanical Turk (89pts/26c). The platform that defined "human intelligence tasks" is winding down as agent task infrastructure scales. Crowdsource labor ≠ agent labor, but the *structural role* is identical: decompose complex work into atomic tasks, route to capable executors, aggregate results.

This is the first concrete platform casualty of the agent transition visible on HN. Not a startup failing — a 21-year-old Amazon platform being sunset.

## Structural Analysis

### The Three Absorptions

This scan provides simultaneous proof of three independent absorptions:

1. **Zuckerberg Absorption**: AI agent skepticism absorbed into default assumption. Evidence: departed after 16 scans, downstream effects (behavioral eval, discipline import) accelerating without the catalyst.

2. **Sovereignty Absorption**: Specific sovereignty exemplars (OpenPrinter, Flipper) absorbed into general repairability thesis. Evidence: new repairability stories (Nintendo, NES, car touchscreens) arrive at the *principle* level, not product level. The community no longer needs to discover sovereignty; it applies the concept to new domains automatically.

3. **Crowdsource-to-Agent Absorption**: Human task infrastructure (Mechanical Turk) absorbed by agent task infrastructure. Evidence: platform closure concurrent with agent harness proliferation (loopkit 373★, merge-queue 295★). The *structural role* transfers even if the *implementation* differs entirely.

**Meta-pattern**: Absorption is the mechanism by which the community progresses. Thesis → debate → validation → absorption → new thesis. The compression rate is accelerating: Zuckerberg took 16 scans to absorb; the Trust Erosion cluster from #411 absorbed in 1 scan. The community's metabolism for processing structural claims is increasing.
