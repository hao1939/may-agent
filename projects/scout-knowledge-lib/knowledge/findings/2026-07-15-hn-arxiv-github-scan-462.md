## Theme

This scan proposes that outcome-only reporting can omit process information
useful for governance. The reviewed TraceProbe evidence establishes added
measurement detail, not systematic erasure or downstream governance benefit.

Four arXiv papers are grouped under that hypothesis from different domains;
TraceProbe, StateFuse, AgentTether, and LogicHunter have now been primary-reviewed:

- **TraceProbe** (2607.06184v1): Resolve rate omits trajectory measurements.
  Across 2,500 SWE-Bench Verified runs, deterministic process profiles and
  same-task comparisons add descriptive localization; they are not failure
  predictors or causal diagnostics.
- **StateFuse** (2607.05844v1): Conflict-preserving and collapsed surfaces tie
  at 97.5% answer accuracy on a 282-case official slice, while only explicit
  conflict surfaces report the contradictions. The slice has no gold
  abstention or correction cases; safer action is tested separately in a
  deterministic 50-task synthetic loop.
- **PatchOptic** (2607.05483): Unconstrained writes hide authorization structure — an optic-inspired interface that declares projected read views, authorized write regions, and patch-source regions for each workflow step. 46-case PatchBench evaluation.
- **AgentTether** (2607.06273v1): On model-specific Banking failure sets, full repair reaches 59.04% for Qwen3.7-max and 65.12% for GPT-5.4. Across all 123 initially failed Qwen tasks, it reaches 69.11% versus 43.09% blind retry. These are conditional repair rates, not full-task success rates or transferred guidance.

The scan proposes that these are four instances of one structural error. The
reviewed TraceProbe evidence establishes additional measurement detail, not
that a scalar outcome destroys source trajectories or that the detail changes
governance outcomes. AgentTether establishes one matched repair result with
auxiliary-model and artifact-access limits. StateFuse supports a public-surface
distinction, not a unique accuracy or naturalistic safety effect. PatchOptic
requires separate primary review.

On HN, the Foundations Turn crosses the **1,000pt milestone** (1,030pts) and SICP **overtakes** Sol's public launch — the convergence predicted in Scan #461 is confirmed. Chat Control crosses 700pts. The Accretive Signal (#461) is compounding.

## arXiv Analysis

### Paper 1: TraceProbe — What Resolve Rate Hides (2607.06184)

**Supported finding**: TraceProbe normalizes raw trajectories into a canonical
nine-action taxonomy with deterministic effect labels. Insight applies fixed
pattern rules; Converge aligns runs against explicit references. One
motivating example fixes task and outcome. The main setting-level comparison
uses similar rather than identical resolve rates.

**Key results and limits** (500 tasks × 5 settings, SWE-Bench Verified):
- OpenCode/GLM and OpenCode/Opus resolve 71.6% and 71.4%, while differing in
  descriptive steps, tool calls, failed-action share, and off-anchor share.
- Function-level, same-task comparisons localize divergence more finely than
  file-only views, but gold patch anchors make several measures oracle-grounded.
- Search loops remain more common in failed runs on SWE-Bench Pro. Task
  difficulty confounds aggregate prevalence, and several other detectors weaken
  or reverse.
- Main task/setting pairs run once. A declared 750-trajectory repeat set has no
  repeat-variance result in the paper's results section.

**Artifact check (2026-09-04)**: Zenodo supplies code and all 2,500 Verified
trajectories. The structural pipeline completed all 2,500 runs without error,
reproduced the five resolve rates and the 41.1%/56.1% resolved/failed
search-loop prevalence, and reproduced the motivating same-task contrast at 10
vs 49 steps and 0 vs 8 failed canonical actions. Semantic labels, repeat-run
data/results, and raw Pro trajectories are not shipped.

**Scope limit**: TraceProbe supports deterministic inspection and controlled
comparison beside resolve rate. It expressly stops short of a per-run causal
oracle or remediation method and does not test human-validated labels,
practitioner outcomes, future reliability, or review-cost reduction. The paper
does not show that reporting resolve rate destroys the retained traces.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06184
Artifact: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20789918

### Paper 2: StateFuse — Conflict-Preserving Memory (2607.05844)

**Supported mechanism**: StateFuse layers immutable operations, explicit
conflict objects, exact/semantic correction handles, deterministic predicate
contracts, and projection-bounded resolution over standard OpSet/CRDT merge.
Resolvers can select or abstain but cannot rewrite base memory.

**Matched results and contradiction**: On the official 282-case
MemoryAgentBench conflict-bearing slice, aggressive StateFuse, multi-value,
raw-log, and collapsed surfaces all reach 97.5% answer accuracy. StateFuse and
multi-value surfaces report 100% conflict recall; raw-log and collapsed public
surfaces report 0%. Conservative conflict-preserving policies instead reach
64.9% accuracy because this slice has no gold-abstention cases. Thus conflict
visibility is supported, while the official slice neither rewards abstention
nor shows a unique StateFuse accuracy advantage.

**Separate synthetic evidence**: In a deterministic 50-task loop with uniform
verification, StateFuse and every conservative non-collapsing surface reach
100% post-verification success and 0% false-confident action; collapsed memory
reaches 60% and 40%. This supports the selected policy/surface combination on
that task family, not StateFuse over strong flat baselines or naturally arising
multi-agent traces. A 13-case synthetic ablation gives semantic handles 13/13
recovery versus 0/13 for exact IDs when those IDs are deliberately unavailable.

**Artifact boundary (2026-09-04)**: Repository main
`b09d8a974bf5e64ac00ecdd3e5a2133f538ff720` contains implementation and unit
tests but no MemoryAgentBench conversion, synthetic agent-loop inputs/results,
or correction-handle benchmark. Three dependency-free examples execute; the
Python environment lacks `pytest`, and no install was made. The paper's
empirical results are therefore not locally reproduced.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05844

### Paper 3: PatchOptic — Verified Structured Updates (2607.05483)

**Supported mechanism**: PatchOptic declares a projected read view, authorized
write region, and patch-source region for each step. The verifier checks JSON
Patch syntax, phase, write targets, and source paths against that declaration
before commit. It enforces modeled mechanical predicates, not global semantic
validity.

**Key results and contradiction**: PatchBench is 46 hand-authored cases replayed
under six settings. GPT-5-mini PatchOptic versus Unconstrained reports 0.2%
versus 31.5% leakage and 718.6 versus 817.5 mean tokens, while accepted-only
content is 0.927 versus 0.925. View Only overlaps the result, showing that
projection drives the live benefit. Conventional FSM + ACL, Verify Only, and
PatchOptic all reduce unsafe accepts to zero when phase/write-scope diagnostics
fire. Verify Only and PatchOptic both reject all 9 fixed no-model patches that
cite hidden source paths. Yet PatchOptic blocks only 3/24 expected-reject weak-
actor cases per round and accepts 21/24 as fabricated or off-policy content.
Path-level reordering is an analytic certificate, not an empirical result.

**Limits and reproducibility**: The policy author is trusted; transformed and
policy-authorized disclosure plus contract-valid false values remain possible.
Quality uses an accepted-only GPT-5-mini judge and leakage uses model review
after deterministic candidate matching. The v1 source package has aggregate
tables but omits its claimed cases and raw JSONL. No paper repository is named;
the author-affiliated `ZBaiY/bAI-harness` precursor at
`80d455281d52854eb10867ad6577614af8603762` contains optics code but no
PatchBench assets. Confidence is high in the aggregate table distinctions and
low in general leakage prevention, semantic validity, transfer, or independent
reproduction.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05483 (v1 source and aggregate tables
primary-reviewed 2026-09-04); https://github.com/ZBaiY/bAI-harness/tree/80d455281d52854eb10867ad6577614af8603762

### Paper 4: AgentTether — Runtime Repair via Transition Graphs (2607.06273)

**Supported mechanism**: AgentTether abstracts failed runs into Transition Units, links them through a dependency-aware Critical Transition Graph, and combines an offline normal-behavior model with a run-local anomaly detector. An auxiliary analyst LLM converts selected regions into guidance, Repair Memory carries guidance across up to three retries, and an auxiliary verifier optionally injects corrections after tool returns or generated text. This augments blind retry; it does not show that retry erases the source trajectory.

**Key results** (261 tau-bench tasks, 3 domains, Qwen3.7-max + cross-model GPT-5.4):
- On 123 initially failed Qwen3.7-max tasks, repairs 85 (69.11%) versus 53 (43.09%) for blind retry and 74 (60.16%) for post-run guidance alone
- On Banking, repairs 49/83 Qwen failures and 56/86 GPT-5.4 failures; these are separate model-specific failure sets, not Qwen-to-GPT guidance transfer
- Uses 56.36 average agent turns and 1,197K method tokens versus blind retry's 66.52 and 1,376K; user-simulator/evaluator calls are excluded
- Runtime intervention versus post-run-only is significant on Banking (13 helped, 3 hurt, p=0.021), neutral on Airline, and not significant on Retail

**Limits**: The paper reports no repeated stochastic LLM runs or confidence intervals. DeepSeek-V4-Pro supplies the simulator/evaluator and AgentTether's analyst/verifier, and the localization evaluation uses tau-bench gold actions despite the method's oracle-free framing. The claimed code/data URL returned HTTP 401 `{"error":"not_connected"}` on 2026-09-04; complete GitHub repository searches found no attributable mirror. The result is not locally reproduced and does not establish production, code-generation, multi-agent, or operator-skill effects.

**Structural significance, qualified**: Transition-localized evidence plus stateful guidance is a supported repair design in the tested benchmark. Calling it durable diagnostic knowledge or evidence that blind retry erases information is an inference; intervention happens after observations and sometimes blocks a correct path.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06273

### Paper 5: LogicHunter — Agentic Oracle for Framework Testing (2607.06195)

**Supported mechanism**: LogicHunter combines API-focused seed generation,
repair, and mutation with an Agentic Oracle that searches documentation and
source code, inspects tests, and executes snippets. It targets selected APIs in
LangChain 0.3.65, LlamaIndex 0.12.42, and CrewAI 0.130.0.

**Oracle comparison**: The primary corpus contains 1,000 unique failures
sampled from LogicHunter generator outputs and manually labeled by two
developers (Cohen's kappa 0.77): 28 bugs and 972 non-bugs. Across five
temperature-zero GPT-5-mini runs, Agentic Oracle reports 91.17%±4.01%
precision, 72.14%±1.60% recall, and 0.21%±0.10% false-positive rate. The best
passive GPT-5.2 judge reports 29.27%±5.40% precision, 59.29%±4.07% recall, and
4.24%±0.84% false-positive rate. Agentic Oracle costs $5.10 total and 1.10
reviews per found bug versus $3.21 and 3.42 for that passive comparator.

**Contradictions and limits**: On a separate 31-bug post-cutoff set, Agentic
Oracle recall is 60.65%±1.44%, below the passive GPT-5.2 judge's
70.32%±3.53%; on 484 hard negatives its false-positive rate is much better at
0.04%±0.09%. The reported 40 previously undisclosed failure patterns, 30
acknowledgments, and 26 fixes come from the full LogicHunter pipeline. Baseline
tools report zero bugs under their own unequal oracles; when 506 of their
effective failures are rechecked with Agentic Oracle and manual review, one
overlapping bug appears. This supports comparative precision and triage value,
not the necessity of active agents or inability of passive testing to find
framework defects.

**Artifact boundary (2026-09-04)**: Public repository commit
`dc5691b341a4cf2be2f2487ea0c79aa34d977cdf` contains code, API lists, usage
snippets, and a 32-row issue ledger totaling 40 patterns. It omits the labeled
corpora, generated tests, per-run results, and stated 32 root-cause clusters,
so the evaluation is not locally reproduced. Three live issue checks support
two closed-completed reports while one artifact-`confirmed` CrewAI report is
now closed `not_planned`; current status must not be substituted for the
paper-time disposition.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06195

### Paper 6: PiSAs — Privacy in Shared Agentic Systems (2607.05318)

**Core finding**: As LLM agents move from single-user assistants to shared organizational infrastructure, cross-user information spillage occurs through inter-agent messages, shared memory, and agent outputs. PiSAs benchmarks contextual integrity with dual annotations: task-appropriateness and user-authorization.

**Key finding**: System design improves CI compliance, but results are **bottlenecked by incorrect LLM judgment calls** — even state-of-the-art models fail to reliably filter inappropriate content or restrict transmission to authorized users. The privacy problem in multi-agent systems is at the model judgment level, not the architecture level.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05318

## The Process Erasure Hypothesis — Proposed 32nd Layer

The sixteen-point exit arc (#444–#460) proposed conditions for governance at
the component, handoff, and attention levels. This scan hypothesizes that
outcome-only summaries can omit process signatures useful for governance. The
reviewed TraceProbe and AgentTether evidence supports richer description and
one conditional repair result; it does not establish systematic evidence
destruction or a common causal mechanism across the four domains.

Four proposed instances, with unequal evidence:

| Domain | What's erased | Erasure mechanism | Remedy |
|--------|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| Evaluation | Trajectory structure | Resolve rate (binary) | TraceProbe: canonical action taxonomy + anti-pattern detection |
| Memory | Conflict structure | Overwrite/collapse | StateFuse: immutable history + explicit conflict objects |
| State mutation | Authorization structure | Unconstrained writes | PatchOptic: declared read views + verified write regions |
| Repair | Failure localization | Blind retry supplies no diagnostic guidance | AgentTether: Transition Units + Critical Transition Graphs; one conditional benchmark result, not evidence destruction |

The possible connections to preceding layers remain hypotheses:
- The Variance Signal (#457) argues that distributional summaries can omit governance-relevant variance; TraceProbe supplies an analogous trajectory view without measuring governance benefit.
- The Write Contract (#454) argues that shared-state mutations need governance; PatchOptic requires separate primary review here.
- The Handoff Fallacy (#459) concerns inter-component failure; AgentTether uses transition-localized evidence but does not prove the handoff thesis.
- The Dispersion Collapse (#455) concerns population convergence; StateFuse does not test population convergence or natural multi-agent traces.

**Seventeen-point exit arc**:
1–16: (#444–#460)
17. **Process Erasure hypothesis (#462): Test whether preserving process
signatures—trajectory measurements, conflict objects, declared contracts, or
transition graphs—improves decisions enough to justify its cost. Current
evidence does not show that scalar outcomes destroy source evidence or that
the proposed remedies generalize.**

## Convergence Synthesis

This scan reveals a structural convergence across all three sources:

**arXiv** (theory): Four papers independently discover that outcome metrics erase process information — and each provides a different formal remedy (action taxonomies, conflict CRDTs, optic interfaces, transition graphs).

**HN** (community behavior): The community's attention allocation is itself a process signal. SICP overtaking Sol, Uniqlo decode nearly doubling, GitLost continuing to grow — these are process-level choices (how the community allocates attention over time) that outcome snapshots would miss. The Foundations Turn is a *trajectory*, not a score.

**GitHub** (implementation): The verification harness ecosystem proliferates at the small-repo level, each implementation preserving slightly different process information (worktree isolation, hash-chained audits, multi-tier verification). No consolidation yet — the process of convergence is itself visible.

The Process Erasure is the governing insight: **every governance failure identified in the preceding 31 layers can be restated as a case where process information was compressed into an outcome metric, destroying the very information needed to detect, diagnose, or prevent the failure.**
