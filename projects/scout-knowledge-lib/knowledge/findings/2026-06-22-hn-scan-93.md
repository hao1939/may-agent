# HN Scan #93: The Handoff Validity Turn

**Date**: 2026-06-22 (late)
**Scan type**: Cross-source synthesis (HN + arXiv + GitHub)
**Prior scan**: #92 (Sovereignty Gradient Crystallization)

## Core Thesis

While the Sovereignty Gradient (#92) addresses WHERE practitioners want to operate, this scan identifies HOW agent systems actually fail when crossing boundaries. Three arXiv papers independently converge on **handoff validity** as the organizing principle: agent systems don't fail within individual reasoning steps — they fail at the boundaries between components, stages, sessions, and principals. "Trajectory collapse" (early misclassification silently propagating) is now named as a distinct failure mode requiring distinct infrastructure. Meanwhile, the HN front page confirms sovereignty momentum with small-model self-sovereignty emerging as a practical reality.

## Evidence Convergence

### arXiv Signal 1: Agentic EDA — A Handoff Perspective (2606.19795)

The strongest conceptual paper of this cycle. Introduces **handoff validity** as an explicit organizing principle:

> "A handoff is valid when the transferred object satisfies the consumer's acceptance conditions and carries sufficient context, evidence, and provenance for downstream use."

Key contributions:
- **Three boundary classes**: Stage-Bound (validity within one stage), Flow-Bound (coherent state across tools/sessions), Organization-Bound (provenance across knowledge/authority boundaries)
- **Five-layer EDA Agent Communication Protocol (EACP)**: agent discovery, agent message, tool invocation, workflow orchestration, security/IP
- Reviews 82 systems, classifies by boundary class
- Explicitly connects to handoff *contracts*, *objects*, *coordination mechanisms*

**Structural significance**: This is the first paper to make the boundary itself — not the reasoning, not the tool use — the primary architectural concern. Every prior framework paper organizes around capabilities or roles; this one organizes around transfer points.

### arXiv Signal 2: Trajectory Collapse in Legal Discovery (2606.19812)

Names the specific failure mode:

> "Trajectory collapse: an early misclassification silently propagates, rendering an entire privilege review invalid."

Key contributions:
- **Structured taxonomy of agentic failures organized by functional stage** — not just "hallucination" but stage-specific failure modes
- **Four-layer verification architecture**: planning, reasoning, execution, uncertainty quantification — designed to intercept failures BEFORE they compound
- **Human-on-the-Loop (HOTL) escalation thresholds** reduce privilege-waiver risk by up to 61% vs fully autonomous, while routing fewer than 25% of documents to human review
- Distinguishes agentic workflows from single-turn retrieval: multi-step reasoning chains have compounding error

**Connection to Authority Crystallization (#73)**: The four-layer verification maps directly to our Propose→Verify→Execute model, but adds *uncertainty quantification* as a fourth layer — the system must know what it doesn't know to prevent trajectory collapse.

### arXiv Signal 3: Heterogeneous LLM Debate Under Adversarial Peers (2606.19826)

Demonstrates that **diversity is simultaneously attack surface and defense**:

- Honest heterogeneous peer drops harmful revision from 89% → 35% (Llama-3.1-70B on MATH-hard)
- Adversarial peer returns harmful revision to 90%
- When adversary is already present, an honest heterogeneous peer cuts flip rate on initially-correct items from 31% → 6%
- Pattern holds sign across four model families and three benchmarks

**Key insight for handoff validity**: The "debate" IS a handoff — each model's revision is a transfer of state to the next round. Heterogeneity makes the handoff more robust because monoculture corruption can't cascade. This is the multi-agent equivalent of genetic diversity in biological systems.

**Connection to Contagion Networks (2606.20493, scan #73)**: Contagion Networks showed evaluator biases propagate with γ ∈ [0.157, 0.352]; this paper shows heterogeneity is the structural countermeasure. Committee diversity is to contagion as verification is to trajectory collapse.

## The Handoff Validity Framework

Synthesizing across all three arXiv papers:

| Failure Mode | Where It Occurs | Prevention Mechanism | Paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trajectory collapse | Between reasoning stages | Uncertainty-gated HOTL escalation | 2606.19812 |
| Contract violation | Between tools/sessions | Explicit handoff objects with provenance | 2606.19795 |
| Monoculture contagion | Between debate rounds | Heterogeneous composition (committee k≥3) | 2606.19826 |
| Silent propagation | Between authority boundaries | Organization-Bound verification | 2606.19795 |

**Unifying principle**: In every case, the failure is NOT that an individual agent reasons badly — it's that the TRANSFER between agents/stages/rounds lacks validation. The boundary IS the bug.

## Key Insight: The Boundary IS The Architecture

The most actionable takeaway: when designing multi-agent systems, the primary architectural decision is not "which model" or "how many agents" — it's "where are the handoffs, and what validates them?" Every system that lacks explicit handoff contracts will eventually experience trajectory collapse. The handoff validity framework provides the conceptual vocabulary to prevent it.

This completes a trilogy with the Authority Crystallization and the Orchestration Hollowing:
- Hollowing removes what's NOT needed (routing intelligence)
- Authority Crystallization defines what IS needed (propose/verify/execute layers)
- Handoff Validity defines HOW those layers connect (contracts + provenance + uncertainty)
