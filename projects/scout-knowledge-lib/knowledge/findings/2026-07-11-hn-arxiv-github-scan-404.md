## Synthesis: The Cost Inversion Thesis

The sovereignty stack has been understood as a *preference* — the community chooses sovereignty because it values control, privacy, and independence. But "When AI Costs More Than the Engineer" introduces a second, orthogonal driver: **economic necessity**.

The two drivers compound:
1. **Philosophical sovereignty**: "I want to own my tools" → Organic Maps, OpenPrinter, Right to Run Local AI
2. **Economic sovereignty**: "I can't afford not to own my tools" → local inference, token optimization, open weights

When both drivers align, adoption accelerates beyond what either alone would produce. This explains the sovereignty stack's compounding trajectory — it's not just viral preference. It's structural economics entering the same channel.

The Cost Inversion Threshold predicts three market phases:
- **Below threshold** (median today: $137/engineer/year): API costs negligible, sovereignty is ideological
- **At threshold** (top 1% today: $89K/year = 40% of salary): sovereignty becomes a line-item optimization
- **Above threshold** (bull 2029: $596K/year = 230% of salary): sovereignty becomes existential — the API bill exceeds the payroll

The HN front page is currently exhibiting Phase 2 behavior. The sovereignty stack's growth correlates with increasing AI costs at the enterprise level. Local-first tools (jamesob/local-llm, Godcoder) are not contrarian — they're rational responses to a cost curve that's visible to the top 1% and approaching the rest.

**Causal chain**: Token consumption growth (24×) → cost inversion threshold approached → sovereignty preference + economic necessity compound → local-first + token-optimization tools accelerate → sovereignty stack exceeds 3,000pts.
