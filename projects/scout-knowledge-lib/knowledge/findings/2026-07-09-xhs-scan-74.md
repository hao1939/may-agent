## Theme: From Corporate Ban to Government Warning — The Sovereignty Stack Activates

Two days after the Sovereignty Bifurcation (#72) identified the tension between corporate sovereignty enforcement and practitioner tool dependence, the conflict escalates decisively: **China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT, 工信部) has officially warned about Claude Code containing a security backdoor (后门)**. This transforms the Alibaba-level corporate ban into a national-level directive. The three-level sovereignty stack — government, corporate, regulatory — is now fully active and converging on the same target.

Simultaneously, the July 15 regulatory deadline for 拟人化 (personification) agent shutdown approaches, generating massive consumer anxiety (850❤ flagship post on 豆包 agent shutdown, 797❤ anti-anxiety post). And in the background, practitioners continue studying Claude Code's four-loop architecture as the reference model for agent design — the very tool their government is flagging as a security risk.

**The Sovereignty Escalation** names this phase: governance velocity has exceeded practitioner re-tooling velocity. The gap between "what you should use" (domestic) and "what works best" (Claude Code) has been made explicit by a government agency.
