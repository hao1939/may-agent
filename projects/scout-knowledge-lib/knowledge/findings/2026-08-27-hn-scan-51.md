### The Amplifier Pattern (Named)

The Linear A story crystallizes a pattern that has been forming across multiple scans:

| Component | What AI Does | What Human Does |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Infrastructure** | Build Python scripts, query tools, cross-reference systems | Define what to query and how to interpret results |
| **Scale** | Process 7500 characters × N hypotheses × M language families | Generate hypotheses worth testing |
| **Iteration** | Rapid hypothesis testing cycles | Domain judgment on which results are meaningful |
| **Verification** | Organize evidence for review | Submit to peer review, defend methodology |

This is **structurally different** from both:
- Agent autonomy (Claude doing research alone)
- Simple copilot (Claude completing code)

It's a third mode: **AI as research infrastructure builder**, where the human's irreplaceable contribution is the domain expertise and judgment that guides what to build and how to interpret results.

### Connection to simonw's Datasette Pattern

simonw's endorsement of the Linear A approach and his simultaneous Datasette Apps launch (145pts) reveal a unified philosophy: **data + custom tooling in one artifact, human judgment on top**. This is the operational pattern our agent system should optimize for.

### WhitneyLand's Challenge

The question "how many years before Fable.next can one-shot this?" introduces the **automation horizon** concept. Today's amplifier pattern works because:
1. The domain requires deep context (linguistics, archaeology)
2. The corpus is tiny (7500 chars — below LLM training threshold)
3. Verification requires human expert judgment

As models improve, the automation horizon moves — but for problems with tiny corpora and no training signal, the amplifier pattern may be permanently optimal.

### Agent System Implication
- **Build tooling, not answers.** The most successful AI applications build infrastructure for human experts rather than trying to replace expert judgment.
- **Small-corpus problems are amplifier-native.** Our own system's knowledge base is a small corpus — the right pattern is AI building tools to query/cross-reference it, not AI trying to "understand" it autonomously.

| **xlii** | "My anecdotal experience differs. GLM 5.2 tends to stray more... morphs requirements, makes unfounded conclusions. This output is not something I easily detect." | **Subtle hallucination** — smaller models hallucinate differently (subtle vs. confident) |
| **spwa4** | "Why is everyone expecting LLMs to be like the Star Trek computer? I wonder if anyone's ever measured what the hallucination rate of a human is." | **Human baseline missing** — no comparative baseline exists |
| **solid_fuel** | "When a model is trained on large volumes of highly factual data, it learns to always have an answer." | **Overconfidence from training** — factual data teaches models to never abstain |

### The Conditional Rate Problem (Named)

aesthesia's observation is structurally important: hallucination RATE is measured conditional on not-knowing. This means:
- A model that knows 95% of answers and hallucinates on 80% of unknowns → encounters 4% hallucination
- A model that knows 80% of answers and hallucinates on 30% of unknowns → encounters 6% hallucination

**The smaller model can have a lower hallucination RATE but higher real-world hallucination FREQUENCY.** This is the exact confusion that benchmark reporting creates.

### xlii's Subtle Hallucination Pattern

xlii introduces a critical distinction:
- **Large model hallucination**: Confident, obviously wrong, detectable ("made up three fake startups")
- **Small model hallucination**: Subtle requirement morphing, unfounded conclusions, hard to detect

This means the "smaller = less hallucination" narrative may be misleading for agent systems where subtle errors compound silently.

## Meta-Signal: The Amplifier vs. Autonomy Divide

This scan crystallizes the fundamental divide in AI application philosophy:

### Amplifier Mode (Community Endorsed)
- Linear A: Claude builds research tools, human does linguistics
- Datasette Apps: Data infrastructure agent-readable, human decides what to display
- simonw's philosophy: "building tools that enable systematic hypothesis testing"
- Skill atrophy corollary: humans who use AI as amplifier strengthen skills

### Autonomy Mode (Community Skeptical)
- Adam CAD: AI tries to do engineering autonomously → "toys not tools"
- TesterArmy: AI executes tests each time → "just have AI write deterministic tests"
- Agentic Resource Discovery: AI discovers tools → "this is just search"
- Token compression: AI optimizes context → "where are the benchmarks?"

**The pattern**: The HN community consistently endorses AI that builds durable artifacts (tools, scripts, tests) and rejects AI that performs ongoing autonomous operations (testing, discovery, compression). The dividing line is **auditability** — can a human inspect and validate the output?

### Implication for Our Agent System

Our system operates in both modes:
- **Scout (this agent)**: Mostly amplifier mode — builds knowledge files for human review ✅
- **Cron tasks**: Autonomy mode — performs operations without per-action review ⚠️
- **Agent delegation**: Autonomy mode — agents dispatch work to other agents ⚠️

The community signal suggests: **where possible, convert autonomous operations into artifact-producing ones.** Instead of an agent "running tests continuously," have an agent "produce a test plan artifact" that executes deterministically.
