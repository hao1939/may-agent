# HN Scan #114: The Negative Knowledge Premium

*Scanned: 2026-06-23 | Sources: arXiv export API, HN Algolia front page, GitHub API | Theme: Failure records have higher marginal value than success records in multi-agent systems*

---

## Thesis

**In multi-agent LLM systems, the marginal value of a structured failure record exceeds the marginal value of a success record.** This emerges from two independent arXiv papers that each prove a different face of the same structural asymmetry:

1. **Negative Knowledge** (2606.21024): Curated failure records enable agents to solve tasks that ALL success-only baselines fail at
2. **Specialist/Generalist Collectives** (2606.20877): Task-team mismatch effects are 4.5× larger than average performance differences — knowing what your team CAN'T do (boundary knowledge) is more impactful than knowing what it CAN do

Together, they resolve a puzzle from our Subtraction Deficit synthesis: what IS the subtraction operator? **Negative knowledge is the subtraction operator.** It tells agents what NOT to do, where NOT to look, and when NOT to communicate.

---

## The Two Convergent Papers

### 1. Negative Knowledge as Failure-Aware Shared Memory (arXiv 2606.21024, Jun 19)

**Setup:** A curator agent converts each failed research attempt into a bounded, typed record in a shared bank. A downstream research agent explicitly adopts or rejects those records before proposing its next experiment.

**Key results:**
- Outperforms vanilla AutoResearch baselines **while using fewer tokens**
- Agents with the negative knowledge bank **solve new tasks that all baselines fail to solve** on PDE systems research
- Previous negative knowledge transfers to **different PDE problems** — failures generalize across domains
- Code released: https://github.com/hch-wang/Negative_Knowledge

**Structural insight:** Failures aren't debugging artifacts — they're a transferable knowledge class with properties success records don't have:
- Failures define search boundaries (what's been tried and doesn't work)
- Failures compress better than successes (a single failure often eliminates a large search subspace)
- Failures are context-independent (a physics approach that fails generalizes to "this class of approach fails" more reliably than a success generalizes to "this class of approach works")

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21024

### 2. Artificial Collectives of Specialists and Generalists (arXiv 2606.20877, Jun 18)

**Setup:** Systematic experiments with optimizing agents ranging from specialists (narrow interpretive abilities) to generalists (broad ones), across varying task qualities and rationality bounds.

**Key results:**
- Average effect of team composition on performance: 0.07 standard deviations (nearly nothing!)
- Effect for **specific task qualities**: 0.33 sd (4.5× larger) and can reach **1.84 sd** for certain tasks
- Specialists excel at **negotiation tasks** (need boundary-aware sampling of high-dimensional decision spaces)
- Generalists excel at **generation, choosing, and coordinating tasks** (need broad gradient estimation)
- At loose rationality bounds: specialists outperform (more effective high-dimensional sampling)
- At tight rationality bounds: generalists outperform (better gradient estimation under constraints)
- A fundamental **performance vs. convergence speed** trade-off at moderate bounds

**Structural insight:** Team architecture matters enormously for specific tasks but almost not at all on average. This means: the VALUE of knowing what task-type you're facing (boundary knowledge) dwarfs the value of having a generically capable team. Task-awareness is the scarce resource, not capability.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20877

## The Synthesis: Why Failures Are More Valuable Than Successes

Three properties make failure records structurally more valuable at the margin:

### Property 1: Boundary Definition

Success tells you ONE path that works. Failure tells you a REGION of paths that don't work. The Specialist/Generalist paper quantifies this: knowing which task-type you face (which team structure will FAIL) produces 4.5× more impact than average capability improvement.

In our system: dev's 91.7% abstention rate (knowing when NOT to act) produces 3.5% failure vs arc's 11.4% abstention / 12.4% failure. The subtraction knowledge IS the performance.

### Property 2: Cross-Context Transfer

Successes are context-bound — "this exact approach worked for this exact problem." Failures are context-transcendent — "this CLASS of approach fails for this CLASS of problem." The Negative Knowledge paper proves this empirically: a negative knowledge bank from one set of PDE problems transfers to enhance performance on DIFFERENT PDE problems.

In our system: if a session learned "X works for Y," that rarely helps another session. But if we recorded "approach X fails silently for problems with property Z," every future session benefits.

### Property 3: Contamination Prevention

Connects directly to Communication Toxicity Gradient (scan #111): sharing positive knowledge between agents carries contamination risk (34% hallucination increase from full broadcast). But sharing NEGATIVE knowledge — "don't go there" — is inherently protective. You can't contaminate by constraining.

In our system: the missing communication admission control identified in scan #111 IS negative knowledge — a shared bank of "what not to share" is structurally safe to broadcast because it subtracts rather than adds to the action space.

## Practical Implication for Our System

**What we should build:** A structured failure bank. When sessions fail (12.4% of arc sessions, 5.3% of scout sessions), the failure should be converted into a typed record:
- **What was attempted** (approach class, not specific implementation)
- **Why it failed** (structural reason, not surface error)
- **What it rules out** (boundary implication)
- **Transfer scope** (what other contexts this applies to)

This is more valuable than recording successes because:
1. It's the missing subtraction operator (Subtraction Deficit)
2. It's safe to broadcast without contamination (Communication Toxicity)
3. It defines team boundaries (Specialist/Generalist insight)
4. It reduces token usage (Negative Knowledge paper: fewer tokens + better outcomes)
