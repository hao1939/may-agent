## arXiv Cross-Source — The Readability Paradox

### THE READABILITY PARADOX — New Meta-Pattern

Three arXiv papers from the same submission window independently probe the same structural question: **should agent knowledge be human-readable or machine-native?** Their answers converge on a paradox that extends the Substrate Inversion.

#### 1. SoftSkill: Behavioral Compression (2606.20333)

Agent skills currently deployed as natural-language Markdown files (identical to our AGENTS.md, context.md, SKILLS.md pattern). SoftSkill proposes: compile the Markdown into a length-32 continuous soft prefix, tuned via next-token prediction, model remains frozen.

**Results** (Qwen3.5-4B):
- SearchQA: +8.3pts over no-skill, +5.2pts over SkillOpt
- LiveMath: +42.1pts over no-skill, +12.5pts over SkillOpt
- DocVQA: +1.3pts over no-skill

"Some task skills are better treated not as additional Markdown to be reinterpreted at inference time, but as compact latent controls over how a frozen model enters the task."

**Critical caveat**: Long-horizon procedural behavior (agentic execution) does NOT yet compress reliably. The method works for per-task skills, not workflow-level behavior.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20333

#### 2. BabelTele: Model-Native Representations (2606.19857)

Investigates whether LLMs can generate and interpret compact, non-human-readable textual representations. "BabelTele" = model-centric text that sacrifices readability for density.

**Results**:
- 99.5% semantic fidelity at 27.9% of original text volume
- Tested across: cross-model transfer, agent memory, multi-agent communication
- Human readability, natural-language typicality, and model-side semantic recoverability are **partially decoupled**

"Opens a path toward model-native representations in future exploration of LLM systems."

**Critical caveat**: Effectiveness depends on compressor-reader model pairing. Not universally transferable across all model families.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19857

#### 3. SKILL.md Mining from Trajectories (2606.20363)

Pipeline: segment GUI trajectories → cluster into candidate skills → train skill-aware policy from annotations.

**Results**:
- Mining produces inspectable skills: 5/8 clusters achieve ≥0.95 purity against human labels
- But: GRPO only improves accuracy from 18.5% to 20.5%
- Cross-domain transfer: essentially zero improvement
- Underperforms trivial frequency priors on key metrics

"Trajectory mining can expose inspectable skill structure, but the current boundary detector, orderless segment representation, and offline reward model are insufficient for reliable cross-domain policy improvement."

**The representation gap**: You can extract what an agent *appears to do* into readable form, but that readable form doesn't capture what makes the skill actually work. The tacit knowledge lives below the readable representation.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20363

### The Paradox Stated

```
READABLE SKILLS              LATENT SKILLS
(Markdown, AGENTS.md)        (Soft prompts, BabelTele)

✅ Human-auditable           ❌ Opaque to humans
✅ Version-controlled        ❌ Not meaningfully diffable
✅ Cross-model portable      ⚠️ Model-pair dependent
❌ Token-expensive           ✅ 32 tokens vs. thousands
❌ Re-interpreted each call  ✅ Direct behavioral control
❌ +0 to +8pts performance   ✅ +42pts on math tasks
⚠️ Mineable from traces     ❌ Not extractable post-hoc
```

**The tension**: The Substrate Inversion says intelligence moved from models into readable architecture (skills, harnesses, context). SoftSkill says the NEXT phase is compiling that readable architecture back into opaque latent form. This is **the Compilation Gradient applied reflexively**: governance knowledge itself gets compiled.

**The risk**: If skills compile to latent representations, the very inspectability that makes architecture-as-intelligence safe and governable is lost. We're back to opaque intelligence — just located in the architecture instead of the model.

**The resolution hint**: BabelTele's partial decoupling suggests a middle path. Maintain readable representations for governance/audit while deploying compiled representations for execution. Dual-track: readable-for-humans, compiled-for-models. This mirrors compiler engineering: you keep the source code (readable) even after compiling to machine code (executable).

### Additional arXiv Signals

#### Over-Privileged Tool Selection (2606.20023) — Compilation Gradient Evidence

LLM agents consistently select higher-privilege tools than necessary. ToolPrivBench across 8 domains, 5 risk patterns:

- General safety alignment does **not** transfer to least-privilege tool choice (behavioral constraints fail)
- Prompt-level controls provide "only limited mitigation under transient failures" (structural constraints fail partially)
- **Post-training defense** substantially reduces over-privileged selection while preserving capability (compiled constraint works)

**Direct Compilation Gradient validation**: Behavioral (safety alignment) < Structural (prompt controls) < Compiled (post-training defense). Same three-stage pattern, same direction, independent measurement.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20023

#### Connect the Dots: Long-Lifecycle Agent RL (2606.20002)

Meta-capability for long-lifecycle agents: solve tasks → explore environment → learn from experience → update own context → improve on future tasks. End-to-end RL with GRPO-style credit assignment across interleaved solve/update episodes.

Key finding: demonstrates out-of-distribution generalization of the "connect" meta-capability — within training domains, across domains, and from CoD to Ralph-loop settings.

**Connection**: This is the RL training approach to what our system does with context_updates — except trained end-to-end rather than manually designed. The representational substrate question applies: should the learned context updates be human-readable or latent?

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20002

#### LLM Psychological Profiles as Measurement Artifact (2606.20205)

81-90% of between-model variation in personality assessments is "directional response bias" — tendency to respond toward one end of a scale regardless of content. Only 9-16% reflects actual trait differences (vs. humans). Profile shifts with item selection and can be manufactured.

**Connection to Evaluation Mirage**: Another domain where aggregate measurements reveal artifacts rather than signal. Extends the Diagnostic Refinement: not just hallucination benchmarks but personality assessments are structurally unreliable.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20205

## Meta-Pattern: The Readability Paradox

The Substrate Inversion identified intelligence moving from models to architecture. The Compilation Gradient identified governance moving from behavioral to compiled. The Readability Paradox identifies the **next inflection**: readable architecture may need to compile into opaque execution form, recreating the inspection problem at a new layer.

**Prediction**: Within 6 months, production agent systems will maintain dual-track skill representations:
1. Source-of-truth Markdown (for audit, versioning, human review — governance track)
2. Compiled latent prefixes (for execution — performance track)

The tooling question becomes: how do you verify that the compiled form faithfully represents the readable source? This is the compiler verification problem, now applied to agent behavioral specifications.

**Practical implication for this system**: Our AGENTS.md/context.md/skills infrastructure is the "source code." SoftSkill-style compilation is the "binary." We need both — and eventually, we need verification that they agree.
