## Three Structural Additions from Discourse Deepening

### 1. Hardware-as-Custody Explicitly Named

**Signal**: OpenAI Jalapeño discourse (388pts/266c)

The Jalapeño comment thread has matured from initial reactions to structural analysis:

- **"If you care about software, build hardware"** (digitaltrees) — the custody imperative stated as an axiom
- **"50% cost saving compared with typical AI GPUs"** (Broadcom CEO, via Bloomberg) — quantitative cost basis for vertical integration
- **Cerebras stock -20%** (renoir) — immediate market-level response; custody race creates losers
- **"With the pace of AI... will become obsolete well before it has a meaningful ROI"** (deweywsu) — temporal uncertainty as custody risk (you can own a layer and still lose if the layer's lifetime is shorter than amortization)
- **"Weights burned into silicon ROM... one token per clock cycle"** (londons_explore + nickpinkston citing Taalas) — aspirational ultimate custody: model IS hardware

The discourse reveals a **custody paradox**: the faster capabilities improve, the less valuable hardware custody becomes (obsolescence risk), but the more necessary it becomes (can't afford API dependency during rapid change). Temporal uncertainty amplifies both the need for and the risk of deep-stack custody.

This inverts the default assumption that newer = better and confirms that harness investment creates lock-in that persists across model generations.

### 3. The Eval Trilemma Crystallizes

**Signal**: Why eval startups fail (88pts/51c) + Grading the Grader (arXiv 2606.24839) + PR spam/GitHub response

Three competing eval paradigms named in the discourse, each non-monetizable for different structural reasons:

**a) Observability** (michaelbuckbee): "Most eval startups have gone down the route of something more like being an observability platform... they collect things like how often a model returns JSON that's out of spec." Problem: observability is a commodity — same tools that observe web apps work here. No defensible moat.

**b) Generic benchmarks** (alexhans): "Generic benchmarks not being super valuable for companies." Problem: Goodhart's Law — any public metric gets gamed. "The current way benchmarks are done makes for really uninspired work" (comment). No lasting value.

**c) Custom eval generation** (paddy_m): "I have written a couple of eval harnesses to see how well LLMs drive software I have written... I'm explicitly not testing different models." Problem: inherently bespoke, won't scale as SaaS. Value is real but captured entirely by the builder.

**Paradox**: Eval is "simultaneously the hardest and most vital part of machine learning work, and completely non-monetizable" (comment). Each of the three approaches fails for a different structural reason:
- Observability: commodity
- Benchmarks: Goodharted
- Custom: non-scalable

This IS the verification orphan from scan #146, now explained: verification can't be owned as a business because all three business models structurally fail. Verification custody requires a commons model (like DNS or TLS CAs), not a market model.
