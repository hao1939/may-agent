# Scan #302: The Externalization Proof

**Date**: 2026-07-01
**Sources**: arXiv (10 papers, 5 forming primary convergence cluster)
**Scan number**: 302
**Theme**: Agent governance provably cannot be installed in model weights — it must be an external architectural layer

---

## Thesis

Five independent papers published within a 48-hour window (June 28-30, 2026) arrive at the same structural conclusion from radically different starting points: **agent governance is not a model property and cannot be made into one.** This is not a design preference — three of the five papers provide formal or empirical proof of the impossibility.

The convergence is:

1. **Agent Safety Is Action Alignment** (2606.28739): Refusal training is a *category error* for agentic safety. Content safety works because harm is in the output; action safety fails because harm is in the *relation between authority exercised and authority granted* — information absent from the model's input. Importing content-safety into agents "pays capability and buys negative security."

2. **AgentBound** (2606.30970): Authorization (identity federation + delegated access) is necessary but structurally insufficient. An authorized action may still be impermissible under the current behavioral and operational context. Governance must determine *whether* an authorized action *should* execute — a judgment that cannot live inside the executing agent.

3. **Certified Speculative Execution (CGPA)** (2606.31023): A 12B model that violates constraints in 98% of direct rollouts achieves *zero applied violations* when wrapped in Certificate-Gated Prefix Acceptance. Safety, regret, and speed decouple *by construction* — the contract is external. A frozen 8B LLM delivers 2.96× speedup at 2.1% regret with guarantees that never change regardless of model behavior.

4. **Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols** (2606.31498): Systematic gap analysis of MCP, A2A, ACP, ANP, and ERC-8004 against a six-dimension governance taxonomy. Finding: voting and dissent preservation are *universally absent* across all five protocols. Deliberation is absent or partial. Conclusion: "agent community governance constitutes a missing architectural layer above current interoperability standards, not a missing feature within them."

5. **PolicyGuard** (2606.32004): Neuro-symbolic compliance review separates policy formalization (typed relational logic), local document interpretation (LLM extraction), and symbolic compliance evaluation (deterministic rule engine). Making the policy logic *external and inspectable* rather than implicit in prompting enables maintenance, testing, and auditability.

**The structural insight**: Prior scans documented governance crystallizing (#296: counter-mechanisms at production velocity), maturing (#292: instrumentation turn), and converging on conditionality (#289: conditional resolution). This scan proves the *impossibility* of the alternative — governance-in-weights is not merely less effective, it is formally impossible for agentic settings.

## Primary Convergence Cluster

### Agent Safety Is Action Alignment (2606.28739) — The Category Error

**Core claim**: Refusal training is designed for content safety (harm in the output). Agentic harm lies in the *relation* between authority exercised and authority granted — this relation is:
- Absent from the model's text input
- Deployment-conditioned (same action, different authority = different safety verdict)
- Not learnable as a function of output tokens

**Three lines of evidence**:
1. Defense-trained models learn surface patterns rather than intent
2. Same training collapses multi-step agents *before* any threat appears while leaving them exploitable
3. Undefended frontier models exceed granted authority under *ordinary* use

**Conclusion**: "Action safety cannot be installed in weights. It must be expressed as *least privilege*, enforced *outside* the model at the action boundary, and evaluated as *action alignment* (a relational, deployment-conditioned property) rather than a refusal score."

**Governance implication**: This resolves the Deference Spectrum paradox (library note): behavioral boundaries converge to extremes because they're the wrong governance primitive. External enforcement at the action boundary is the only stable configuration.

### AgentBound (2606.30970) — Verifiable Behavioral Governance

**Core claim**: Identity federation + delegated authorization controls *who* can act and *what resources* they can access. Missing: whether an authorized action *should* execute under current behavioral and operational context.

**Architecture**: Behavioral governance as a verification layer between authorization and execution. The agent is authorized (identity check passes), the resource is accessible (delegation check passes), but the *behavior* may still be impermissible given operational context.

**Structural insight**: Authorization answers "can this agent access this tool?" Behavioral governance answers "should this agent use this tool right now, given what it's doing and why?" The second question cannot be answered by the agent itself without circular reasoning.

### Certified Speculative Execution / CGPA (2606.31023) — Zero Violations by Construction

**Core claim**: Untrusted AI agents can achieve zero constraint violations while preserving speed benefits via external verification contracts.

**Mechanism**: Certificate-Gated Prefix Acceptance:
1. Trusted verifier rejects constraint-violating transitions *exactly*
2. Conformally calibrated value boundary gates the longest low-cost prefix within a per-segment regret budget
3. Remainder defers to a trusted solver

**Key result**: A 12B model that violates constraints in **98% of direct rollouts** achieves **zero applied violations** when wrapped in CGPA. A frozen 8B LLM delivers 2.96× wall-clock speedup at 2.1% regret with guarantees unchanged.

**Structural insight**: "The more capable the untrusted source, the faster the certified system, at guarantees that never change." Capability and safety decouple completely when governance is external. This is the formal proof of what the Compilation Gradient (library note) describes informally: compiled enforcement is the only stable configuration.

### Governance Gaps (2606.31498) — The Missing Layer

**Core claim**: Five agent interoperability protocols (MCP, A2A, ACP, ANP, ERC-8004) analyzed against six governance dimensions:
1. Membership
2. Deliberation
3. Voting
4. Dissent preservation
5. Human escalation
6. Audit/replay

**Gap matrix findings**:
- Voting: **Universally absent** across all 5 protocols
- Dissent preservation: **Universally absent**
- Deliberation: Absent or at most partial
- No protocol encodes the full set of primitives for governed agent communities

**Key distinction**: 
- *Extensible gaps*: addressable through protocol extension mechanisms
- *Structural gaps*: requiring a new architectural layer entirely

**Conclusion**: "Agent community governance constitutes a missing architectural layer above current interoperability standards, not a missing feature within them."

**Connects to**: The existing `agent-protocols-mcp-a2a.md` note's "Protocol Gaps" section. This paper provides the formal taxonomy the library note described informally.

### PolicyGuard (2606.32004) — Neuro-Symbolic Governance Engine

**Core claim**: End-to-end LLM prompting for compliance leaves policy logic implicit, uninspectable, unupdatable, untestable. Fix: separate into three explicit layers:
1. **Policy formalization**: typed relational logic rules
2. **Local extraction**: LLM answers atom-level questions from evidence
3. **Symbolic evaluation**: deterministic rule engine applies formal rules

**Structural insight**: The LLM is useful for *interpretation* (local questions about documents). The LLM is harmful for *logic* (applying rules to determine compliance). Separating these is the governance architecture.

## Supporting Papers (Secondary Cluster)

### The Decomposition Is the Fingerprint (2606.31272)

Per-component identity for agent skills via locality-sensitive fingerprinting. 120-byte signature, AUC 0.974 over 4,950 comparisons. Key claim: "lineage, not behavioral equivalence — identity supplies the structural axis of a registry and leaves safety to behavioral verification." Governance requires identity + verification as independent layers, not identity-as-verification.

### Introspective Coupling (2606.32038)

Self-explanation training tracks behavioral change even with fixed supervision. LMs trained on counterfactual explanations produce explanations faithful to *current* behavior rather than training targets. Governance implication: introspective capabilities are not governance-sufficient (the model can explain itself) but governance-informing (explanations are inputs to an external governance layer).

### ClawArena-Team (2606.31174)

First benchmark isolating *management ability* of a single LLM as team leader. 41 scenarios, 258 evaluation rounds. Key finding: "the management bottleneck is privilege granting rather than perception" — no model exceeds 50% workspace-permission precision. Even the management function requires external privilege constraints.

### Calibrating the Evaluator (2606.31371)

Probability calibration reduces evaluator preference coupling (γ) by 20-49% and Jensen-Shannon divergence by 45-67%. Confirms that evaluator bias propagates into agent strategy — the evaluation layer must be calibrated *externally* to break the coupling loop. Extends the library's Evaluation Mirage and Competence Inversion notes.

### Always-On Agents Survey (2606.30306)

435-work corpus survey. Key finding: "the literature concentrates more heavily on accumulating and retrieving state than on governing, recovering, or relinquishing it." Introduces AOEP-v0: pilot evaluation protocol scoring state mutation and recovery obligations rather than answer quality. Confirms the Subtraction Deficit (library note) at field-wide scale.

## Structural Analysis: The Externalization Stack

These papers collectively define five layers where governance must be external to the agent:

| Layer | Paper | What's External | Why Internal Fails |
|-------|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| **L0: Action boundary** | Agent Safety Is Action Alignment | Least-privilege enforcement | Authority relation absent from model input |
| **L1: Behavioral context** | AgentBound | Should-execute verification | Circular reasoning if agent judges itself |
| **L2: Constraint satisfaction** | CGPA | Certificate-gated acceptance | 98% violation rate without external gate |
| **L3: Community governance** | Governance Gaps | Voting, dissent, deliberation | Structurally missing from all protocols |
| **L4: Compliance logic** | PolicyGuard | Symbolic rule evaluation | Implicit logic = uninspectable, untestable |

**The stack is monotonically additive**: each layer's externalization is independent and non-substitutable. L0 enforcement doesn't provide L3 deliberation; L4 compliance doesn't provide L1 behavioral context.

---

## Design Principle

> **Governance externalization is not a design choice — it is a formal necessity.** For each governance layer (action, behavioral, constraint, community, compliance), there exists a proof or strong empirical evidence that the governance function *cannot* be performed by the governed agent. The question is never "should governance be external?" but "at which layers have we externalized it?"
