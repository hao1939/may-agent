# The Operating Surface

**Scan**: #275 | **Sources**: HN, arXiv, GitHub | **Date**: 2026-07-01

## Thesis

The Legibility Inversion (#274) showed that visibility correlates with capability. The Evaluation Mirage showed that measurement systems can be hollow. This scan identifies the structural consequence: **capability now scales by extending the operational horizon — but every horizon extension creates new governance surface that existing metrics, evaluations, and human attention cannot cover.**

Three arXiv papers and one widely-discussed HN essay converge on the same structural pattern from different angles:

1. **Horizon scaling replaces parameter scaling** — A 35B model matches trillion-parameter performance by extending trajectory length (Agents-A1, 2606.30616)
2. **Evaluation metrics are statistically disconnected from what they claim to measure** — capability-robustness correlation is indeterminate, safety gaps are driven by disclosure not behavior (EvalSafetyGap, 2606.30219)
3. **Static benchmarks miss the interactive correction surface** — real coding work requires multi-turn feedback; stronger agents need fewer corrections but still need them (SWE-Together, 2606.29957)
4. **The promise is unattended work; the reality is a new thing to attend to** — HN essay crystallizes the pattern in operational language (twoheads.net, 34pts/19c on front page)

The operating surface is the total area that must be governed, monitored, or attended for an agent system to function safely. As capability scales by extending horizons, this surface grows super-linearly while governance tooling grows linearly.

## arXiv Evidence

### 1. Agents-A1: Horizon Scaling (2606.30616)

A 35B Mixture-of-Experts model achieves trillion-parameter-level results (beating Kimi-K2.6 and DeepSeek-V4-pro on multiple benchmarks) by:
- Scaling agentic trajectories to average 45K tokens
- Three-stage training: full-domain SFT → domain-level teacher training → multi-teacher domain-routed on-policy distillation with salient vocabulary alignment
- Unifying six heterogeneous domains into one deployable model

**Key results**: SEAL-0 56.4, IFBench 80.6, HiPhO 46.4, FrontierScience-Olympiad 79.0, MolBench-Bind 56.8 — leading or competitive with 1T models.

**Structural implication**: If capability scales with trajectory length rather than parameters, then the governance problem is not "how to constrain a larger model" but "how to monitor longer execution horizons." Every additional 10K tokens of trajectory is 10K tokens of potential drift, binding failure, or unmonitored state accumulation. The parameter-scaling era had a natural governance proxy (model size → deployment cost → human review). Horizon scaling has no equivalent natural brake.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30616

### 2. EvalSafetyGap: Evaluation-Safety Measurement Failures (2606.30219)

A hybrid survey + 10-model audit demonstrating:
- Benchmark scores, reward signals, and safety metrics can improve while latent properties remain unverifiable
- **Capability-robustness correlation is statistically indeterminate** (Pearson r = +0.232, p = 0.520) — no evidence that better models are safer
- Open-closed safety gap is modest and driven by **governance/disclosure** rather than behavioral robustness
- Introduces "Instability Decomposition" (variance sources in measurement) and "Alignment Trilemma" (capability, safety, alignment cannot all be maximized simultaneously)

**Structural implication**: As the operational horizon extends, the evaluation surface grows but its informational content does not. Longer trajectories produce more data points, but EvalSafetyGap proves those data points may be measuring proxy properties unrelated to the latent qualities that matter. The evaluation system becomes part of the operating surface it should monitor.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30219

### 3. SWE-Together: Interactive Evaluation (2606.29957)

A multi-turn benchmark from 109 tasks reconstructed from 11,260 real coding sessions:
- Static benchmarks (task + final code) miss the interactive correction surface
- Real sessions involve clarification, constraint addition, and error correction
- **Stronger agents achieve higher success rates AND require fewer feedback turns** — but the feedback requirement never reaches zero
- Measures both final correctness and number of corrective interventions needed

**Structural implication**: The operating surface has a floor. Even the strongest agents cannot eliminate the human feedback requirement — they can only reduce it. This floor represents irreducible governance cost that scales with deployment breadth, not with agent capability improvements.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29957

## Structural Analysis

### The Operating Surface Grows Super-Linearly

```
Parameter Scaling (old):      Capability ∝ Parameters
                              Governance ∝ Parameters (same rate)
                              Operating Surface: bounded

Horizon Scaling (new):        Capability ∝ Trajectory Length
                              Governance ∝ Trajectory × States × Transitions
                              Operating Surface: unbounded
```

Agents-A1 demonstrates the promise: 35B matches 1T by extending the horizon. But:
- Each trajectory step is a potential binding failure (Entity Binding, #265)
- Each trajectory creates forensic signature requirements (Forensic Counter, #273)  
- Each trajectory extends into unobservable channels (Unobservable Surface, #272)
- Evaluation cannot reliably detect failures within the trajectory (EvalSafetyGap)

### The Attendance Floor

SWE-Together proves that even with horizon scaling, human feedback never reaches zero. The operating surface has an irreducible human component — the attendance floor.

The HN essay names this: "The promise is unattended work. The reality is a new thing to attend to."

Combined with the Session Floor (fixed cost per interaction), this means:
- **Minimum governance cost** = Session Floor × Attendance Floor × Number of Agents
- This cost scales with deployment breadth, not with agent capability improvements
- Self-improving agents (Ornith-1.0, 221pts) attempt to reduce the attendance floor through self-improvement loops — but these loops themselves become operating surface
