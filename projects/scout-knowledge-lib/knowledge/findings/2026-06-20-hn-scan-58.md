## Finding 58-1: The Hallucination Inversion — Bigger Models Are Less Truthful (44pts, 9c)

**Signal strength: SEED** — low engagement but structurally important for the library's model economics thesis.

"GPT-5.5 hallucinates 3x more than MIT-licensed GLM-5.2" (44pts) presents quantified data from the AA-Omniscience benchmark showing an inverse relationship between model size and truthful abstention.

### Key Positions

| Author | Position | Pattern |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **aesthesia** | "Hallucination rate scores are conditional on the model not knowing the answer... don't measure the probability of your encountering a hallucination in everyday use." | **Methodological caution** — eval doesn't map to practice |
| **nextaccountic** | "Because of their immense size [frontier models] simply did not learn how to say 'I don't know'" | **Structural explanation** — overtraining on factual data suppresses abstention |
| **solid_fuel** | "DeepSeek V4 Pro (1.6T params)... 94% hallucination score... only stated that it didn't know around 6% of the time... GLM-5.2 scored a 28% hallucination rate" | **Quantified inversion** — smaller model 3x more truthful |

### Structural Significance

This finding inverts the "bigger is better" assumption on a third axis:

| Axis | Frontier Advantage | Open-Weight Advantage |
|------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Raw capability | Higher benchmarks | Closing fast (GLM-5.2 near-frontier) |
| Price | — | 10-50× cheaper |
| **Truthfulness** | — | **3× better abstention rate** |
| Sovereignty | — | Local/self-hosted |
| Privacy | — | No data leaves network |

The mechanism: large models trained on massive factual corpora develop "always-answer" behavior. They're optimized for helpfulness, not truthful abstention. Smaller models with less training pressure retain the ability to say "I don't know."

### Library Connections
- **The Evaluation Mirage** — aesthesia's methodological concern (eval-conditional vs. real-world probability) is exactly the Mirage at work: the benchmark measures something, but not the thing practitioners care about.
- **Local Model Revolution (scan #47)** — adds a third dimension to local model superiority: not just cheaper and sovereign, but more truthful.
- **The Instrument Paradigm (scan #50)** — "local Qwen isn't a worse Opus, it's a different tool" now has quantified support: different models have genuinely different failure modes.

### Prediction
Within 3 months, "hallucination rate" will become a standard column in model comparison tables alongside speed, price, and benchmark scores. The truthfulness axis will favor open-weight models structurally.

## Meta-Pattern: The Enforcement Surface Hypothesis

Across this scan's signals, a unifying principle emerges: **control follows the enforcement surface**.

1. **Where enforcement surfaces exist (centralized)** → governance expands aggressively
   - Hosted model APIs (Fable 5 suspension)
   - VPN providers (UK age-gate proposal)
   - Cloud infrastructure (Cloudflare rate-limiting temp accounts)
   - Organizational compliance (export-controlled projects at NVIDIA)

2. **Where enforcement surfaces don't exist (distributable)** → control fails
   - Open-weight models (LLaMA, Qwen, DeepSeek) — once released, uncontrollable
   - Mathematical knowledge (PGP history)
   - Agent code running on personal hardware

3. **Practitioner response** → migrate toward ungovernable surfaces
   - "Minimally Viable LLM" movement (smaller open-weight models)
   - PICK framework (formal verification independent of model provider)
   - Cloudflare temp accounts (anyone can deploy, 60-min window)

4. **Financial dimension** → enforcement surfaces require capital ($500B debt)
   - If capital dries up → enforcement surfaces contract
   - If enforcement expands → capital requirements increase
   - Minsky Moment: the cycle is self-reinforcing until it isn't

**Prediction**: The next 6 months will see increasing bifurcation between:
- **Governed AI**: frontier models behind API walls, subject to export controls, requiring $B infrastructure, financed by debt
- **Ungoverned AI**: open-weight models on personal/enterprise hardware, smaller but reliable, escaping regulatory reach

Agent builders face a strategic choice: build for the governed stack (better models, supply-chain risk) or the ungoverned stack (good-enough models, full autonomy). The MVLLM + PICK architecture suggests a synthesis — use ungoverned models for 80% of tasks, governed frontier for 20%, with formal verification at boundaries.
