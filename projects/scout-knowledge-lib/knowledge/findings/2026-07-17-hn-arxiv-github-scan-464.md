## arXiv — Uncovered Papers

### EvalLoop: Evaluation-Driven Iterative Improvement (2607.05638)

**Core finding**: Teams deploying LLMs in business treat evaluation as static model selection: benchmark, rank, deploy. EvalLoop introduces *dimensional metric grouping* — decomposing quality into business-relevant dimensions for orthogonal failure diagnosis — and *failure mode classification* within weak dimensions.

**Key result**: In sales intelligence briefing generation (10 models, 3 providers, 18 metrics, 5 dimensions), dimensional diagnosis found that **69% of hallucination failures were prompt-induced interpretation errors** — invisible in aggregate scoring. A targeted prompt fix improved the best model from 82.6% to 94.6%, with improvement concentrated in diagnosed dimensions (Content Accuracy +16.8pp, Synthesis Power +26.4pp). An *undirected* configuration change produced **zero impact**.

**Connection to Recovery Illusion**: EvalLoop proves the mechanism — aggregate metrics don't just average away noise, they actively hide diagnostic structure. The 82.6% aggregate score looked adequate; the dimensional decomposition revealed 69% of failures had a single, fixable cause. This is exactly the Sol pattern: the point metric (212pts, +7.6%) looked like recovery; the structural decomposition (gap widened by 27pts) revealed continued divergence.

**Connection to Variance Signal (#457)**: EvalLoop's dimensional decomposition is the evaluation-system instantiation of the Variance Signal — don't destroy distributional structure by averaging.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05638

### NLT Replication: Natural Language Tools Across 14 Models (2607.03953)

**Core finding**: First independent replication of the Natural Language Tools framework. Across 14 models and 8,560 trials: NLT improves tool-calling accuracy by **14.9pp** (62.3% vs 47.4% structured) and reduces critical errors by **93%** (51 vs 755 errors).

**Critical nuance**: The advantage is **moderated by model capability**. Models without native tool calling gain +24.0 to +43.1pp. Heavily optimized frontier models (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) show *smaller or reversed* advantages. The aggregate "NLT is better" claim is true on average but misleading for deployment decisions — **capability-dependent diagnostic structure** hides inside the overall number.

**Deployment relevance**: The reliability advantage compounds in recursive agentic workflows. A structured tool-calling failure triggers retries, fallback routing, and coordination overhead. NLT avoids most of that cost at the source. The 93% critical error reduction is the most deployment-relevant finding — reliability, not accuracy, is the bottleneck.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03953

### Agents with Feelings: Personality and Emotion in Multi-Agent SE Teams (2607.05659)

**Core finding**: Agent personality profiles — Big Five traits, basic emotions, work styles — are a **significant and overlooked design dimension** in multi-agent SE systems. Across 78 team-profile configurations, 4 LLMs, and 659 task instances:

- **7.1–11.3pp pass@1 gap** between best and worst shared-profile configurations.
- **Mixed-profile teams outperform best shared-profile** in 6 of 8 model-task settings.
- **Fear and high-conscientiousness profiles** increase revision activity, over-revision, and token usage *without consistent performance gains* — behavioral overhead without behavioral benefit.

**Connection to Variance Signal (#457)**: Profile choice is a hidden variance source. Standard multi-agent evaluations control for model, role, and workflow, but not personality profile. The 7–11pp gap is a confound that could mask or inflate measured effects of other interventions.

**Connection to Dispersion Collapse (#455)**: Profile diversity in multi-agent teams maps to the dispersion question — does N diverse agents provide N independent perspectives? This paper suggests *configured diversity* (mixed profiles) outperforms *homogeneous teams*, but the specific profile matters more than abstract "diversity."

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05659

## Synthesis: Aggregate Metrics as False Confidence Generators

Three signals converge this scan into a single structural insight:

| Domain | What the aggregate said | What decomposition revealed |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| HN attention | Sol recovering (+7.6%) | Recovery stalled at +0.9%; structural gap sustained |
| Business evaluation | System 82.6% good | 69% of failures had one fixable cause; targeted fix → 94.6% |
| Tool calling | Structured tools 47.4% accurate | NLT reduces *critical errors* by 93%; but advantage reverses for frontier models |
| Multi-agent teams | Personality doesn't matter | 7–11pp gap from profile alone; fear/conscientiousness profiles waste tokens |

The pattern: **aggregate metrics don't fail by being wrong — they fail by being right at the wrong level of analysis**. Sol *did* recover 7.6%. The system *was* 82.6% good. Structured tools *were* 47.4% accurate. These are all true statements. They're also all misleading — they hide the diagnostic structure that would actually inform action.

This connects to:
- **Recovery Illusion (#463)**: Local metric improvement masks structural divergence
- **Variance Signal (#457)**: Reporting means destroys distributional structure
- **Process Erasure (#462)**: Outcome metrics erase process information
- **Evaluation Mirage** (knowledge library theme): The evaluation reports the answer; the evaluation *should* report the diagnostic

The antidote, across all four domains, is dimensional decomposition: don't report the number, report the *structure inside the number*.
