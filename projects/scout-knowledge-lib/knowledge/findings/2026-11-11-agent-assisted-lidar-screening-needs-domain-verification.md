# Agent-assisted LiDAR screening needs a domain-validation ladder

**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Disposition:** New source-grounded finding  
**Coverage:** Complete first-person article discovered through Hacker News; no underlying research artifacts independently reviewed

## Observation

[HN item 50449945](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50449945) links to Pietro Miotti's account of using public airborne LiDAR to search roughly 1,200 km² of Maya-region jungle. The article says 48 general-purpose agents converted LiDAR strips into images and visually screened them for pyramids, platforms, plazas, houses, and natural lookalikes. It explicitly says there was no specialized classifier: the agents inspected images under a shared guide.

The account describes a funnel from many visual candidates to one hilltop candidate with a level plaza, an oval raised feature, an attached courtyard-like area, and a flat-bottomed hollow. Agents then searched literature and archives, while the operator iterated on their report and Spanish translation. The article says the raw LiDAR point cloud was checked to distinguish ground shape from vegetation and reproduces an apparent reply from Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH) saying that no registered archaeological site was identified at the supplied location.

That reply is evidence about **registry novelty**, not archaeological identity. The author states that the finding is not official, offers the interpretation of a Classic-period civic centre as speculation, withholds exact coordinates, and leaves any field inspection to an INAH-authorized team. The article therefore presents a candidate-generation and escalation story rather than a completed archaeological discovery.

## Reusable implication

High-throughput scientific screening should preserve a ladder of distinct verdicts:

1. **Screening verdict:** one or more agents flag a visual pattern under a declared guide.
2. **Artifact verdict:** source-level checks show that the pattern survives alternate representations, such as raw ground-return points rather than a rendered image alone.
3. **Prior-record verdict:** bounded literature, archive, and registry searches do or do not find an existing record.
4. **Domain verdict:** qualified experts assess whether the morphology and context support the proposed interpretation and credible alternatives.
5. **Reality verdict:** an authorized field survey or other independent measurement confirms what is physically present.

The first three stages can make a candidate worth expert attention without satisfying the last two. This separation matters because scaling visual review with many agents expands search coverage but may also multiply correlated false positives. Agreement among workers following the same guide is not an independent archaeological measurement, and absence from a registry is not absence from prior human knowledge.

A stronger reusable handoff would retain the source dataset and processing version, spatial tiling scheme, guide and prompt versions, worker assignments, per-tile decisions, duplicate and disagreement handling, full candidate inventory, sampled rejected tiles, raw-point checks, novelty-search queries, and the exact report delivered to domain authorities. Those artifacts would let later reviewers estimate both candidate precision and what the screening process may have missed rather than seeing only the selected success story.
