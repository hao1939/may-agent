# Scan #405 — The Governance Depth Problem
**Date**: 2026-07-11
**Source**: arXiv API
**Extends**: Cost Inversion Threshold (#404), Evidentiary Gap (#339), Oversight Inversion (#382), Verification Substrate (#377), Operational Record Thesis (#379)

## Theme

Six independent arXiv papers — spanning organizational management, legal compliance, planning repair, multi-agent deliberation, hardware enforcement, and context engineering — converge on a single structural claim: **governance that operates at the wrong depth fails silently, and the failure is worse than no governance at all because it creates the illusion of control.**

Each paper discovers the same pattern at a different layer:

| Paper | Surface governance (what's convenient) | Actual failure layer (what's needed) | Consequence of mismatch |
|-------|---------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|------------------------|
| Risk Architecture (2607.01421) | Team-level risk frameworks | Organizational boundary where probabilistic → deterministic | Worst failures invisible to team governance |
| Evidentiary Adequacy (2607.00941) | Tamper-proof logs, provenance chains | Typed legal relations (authority, temporal validity) | Records exist but can't answer legal determinations |
| WM-SAR (2607.01767) | Symptom scanning + local LLM repair | Causal amplifier subgraph identification | Full-graph replay wastes context; symptom repair doesn't stabilize |
| InfoDelphi (2607.01661) | Identical-evidence multi-agent debate | Designed information asymmetry per agent | Deliberation collapses into herding (no better than 1 agent) |
| FPGA Coordination (2607.02376) | Software-mediated coordination | Hardware-native semantic gating | Unbounded latency, non-deterministic coordination in safety-critical |
| Self-GC (2607.00692) | Chronological pruning / text cleanup | Indexed object lifecycle with dependency awareness | Heuristics blind to future dependencies; 23-37% higher impact rate |

**Design principle**: Before instrumenting governance, identify where the failure actually lives. If governance operates at layer N but the failure lives at layer N-1, governance creates false confidence without reducing risk.

## Paper Analysis

### 1. Risk Architecture for AI-Native Teams (2607.01421) — CRITICAL

**Core contribution**: A seven-dimension profile distinguishing pure SE, hybrid, and AI-native teams, plus a six-cluster failure-mode taxonomy including a novel category: **dependency-boundary determinism mismatch**.

**Key finding**: Risk architecture coverage degrades monotonically as teams move from pure SE to AI-native, but the degradation is not uniform. The most severe failures arise **not inside AI-native teams but at the organizational boundary where their probabilistic outputs are consumed by determinism-assuming dependencies.** A downstream service that treats agent output as deterministic (same input → same output) will not trigger any risk escalation when the agent produces a different result for the same query — because the boundary lacks the concept of probabilistic variance.

**Three assumptions violated simultaneously**:
1. **Deterministic behavior**: Agent outputs are probabilistic, breaking severity-based escalation
2. **Discrete change events**: Risk surface mutates silently between deployments (no diff to review)
3. **Component-to-owner mapping**: Agent actions span tool boundaries, orphaning accountability

**Structural insight**: Existing AI risk frameworks operate either too high (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 = policy) or too low (OWASP agentic guidance = threats). Neither addresses the **engineering manager layer**: roles, decision rights, escalation structures. This is the governance depth problem expressed organizationally — the most needed governance layer is the one no framework targets.

**Connection to library**: Directly extends Oversight Inversion (#382): if oversight cost hasn't adjusted to collapsed production cost, the *organizational* version is that risk frameworks haven't adjusted to non-deterministic output. The Judgment Tax (#389) predicted that judgment — not code — is the binding constraint; this paper shows that risk *architecture* hasn't caught up either.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01421

### 2. From Runtime Records to Legal Findings (2607.00941) — CRITICAL

**Core contribution**: A formal evidentiary-adequacy criterion: a runtime record can answer a legal determination **only if** it carries (a) a **typing** mapping recorded events to legally operative categories and (b) the **relation** (provenance, authority, derivation, temporal validity) on which the determination depends.

**Key insight**: Tamper-proof logs, generic process frameworks, and provenance chains **alone** cannot establish legal findings. The criterion distinguishes between:
- **Record integrity**: "Did event X happen?" (logs can answer)
- **Legal determination**: "Was delegated authority valid at the moment of use?" (requires typed temporal-validity relation)

**Worked example against EU AI Act**: The paper instantiates the criterion against specific oversight obligations, showing that common compliance artifacts (audit trails, process documentation) fail the adequacy test for binary findings of fact like "did protected data cross a boundary?" — because they lack the typed relation mapping.

**Theoretical grounding**: Connects to requisite variety (Ashby), the Good Regulator Theorem, and the trace-vs-hyperproperty boundary of runtime verification. A trace-level property ("event X occurred before event Y") can be monitored at runtime; a hyperproperty ("no execution of this system leaks information") cannot be verified from any single trace — it requires typing that spans the relationship between traces.

**Connection to library**: Extends the Evidentiary Gap (#339) and Operational Record Thesis (#379). The Record Thesis says "design the record first." This paper specifies *what the record must contain*: not just events, but typed events carrying the relations needed for legal determinations. It transforms the Record Thesis from an architectural preference into a legal necessity.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00941

### 3. Repair the Amplifier, Not the Symptom: WM-SAR (2607.01767) — HIGH

**Core contribution**: For persistent agent workflows (thousands of steps), a world-model corrector that works **backward from subgraph amplification** instead of scanning for visible symptoms.

**The problem**: As agent planning scales from short tool chains to persistent workflows with thousands of steps, failures occur inside large planning graphs. Two approaches:
1. **Full-graph replay**: Consumes large context budgets, exposes LLM to irrelevant symptoms, degrades long-context retrieval. Not scalable.
2. **Symptom scanning** (common engineering approach): Scan nodes/edges, choose suspicious region, ask LLM to repair locally. Can help with large contexts, but misidentifies the failure source.

**WM-SAR approach**: Instead of symptom scanning, identify the nodes and edges that **keep re-amplifying error** — the causal subgraph — and send only that compact region to the LLM. Under realistic token budgets, WM-SAR substantially outperforms engineering correctors and achieves near-whole-graph stabilization.

**Governance depth insight**: This is the planning-layer version of the governance depth problem. Symptom-level repair is surface governance — it addresses what's visible, not what's causal. The amplifier subgraph is the deeper layer where the failure actually lives. Repairing the amplifier with a small context window outperforms repairing symptoms with a large one — **depth of diagnosis beats breadth of observation**.

**Connection to library**: Extends Atomic Task Graph (#377): ATG exposes dependencies for targeted repair; WM-SAR adds causal amplification diagnosis — not just "which subtask failed?" but "which subtask *keeps amplifying* the failure?" Also extends Action Compulsion (#401): agents that can't stop acting propagate errors through the planning graph; WM-SAR identifies where that propagation amplifies.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01767

### 4. InfoDelphi: Multi-Agent Deliberation Under Information Asymmetry (2607.01661) — HIGH

**Core contribution**: When all agents receive identical evidence, multi-agent deliberation collapses into **herding** — convergence without genuine belief revision, performing no better than a single agent. Designed information asymmetry (partitioning evidence into shared public + disjoint private subsets) is the structural fix.

**Quantitative result**: On PolyGym (375 binary forecasting questions from real prediction markets), InfoDelphi outperforms strongest single-agent and multi-agent baselines by **12–18% in Brier score** and **4–8 percentage points in accuracy**. Removing information asymmetry eliminates most deliberation gains — confirming that **diversity of input, not diversity of model, is the key enabler**.

**Three-component framework**:
1. **Relevance-aware evidence routing**: Private evidence assigned by topical relevance, not randomly
2. **Rationale-based iterative deliberation**: Agents share reasoning traces, not just conclusions
3. **Confidence-weighted aggregation**: Final integration weights by expressed confidence

**Governance depth insight**: This is the deliberation-layer governance depth problem. Surface governance = "use multiple agents." Deeper governance = "ensure each agent has exclusive knowledge that can only reach others through deliberation." Without designed asymmetry, the governance mechanism (multi-agent review) creates false confidence — you believe multiple perspectives reviewed the decision, but they all saw the same evidence and herded to the same conclusion.

**Connection to library**: Directly validates BOUNDARY_SYNC (2607.01600): that paper showed agents converge representationally (CAF=0.803) when they communicate — the herding mechanism. InfoDelphi provides the structural fix: information asymmetry counteracts representational coupling. Also connects to Chinese community signals — XHS Scan #67 documented the 禁止main agent干任何活 pattern (prohibit main agent from doing any work) — a form of designed asymmetry where the main agent only deliberates, never executes.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01661

### 5. Hardware-Enforced Semantic Coordination (2607.02376) — MODERATE

**Core contribution**: In safety-critical real-time systems, software-mediated coordination has **fundamental limitations** — unbounded latency, non-deterministic behavior, unenforceable timing guarantees. Selected coordination semantics (temporal synchronization, semantic gating, authorization constraints) should be mapped to FPGA primitives for deterministic enforcement, while semantic reasoning stays software-driven and adaptive.

**TB-CSPN architecture**: Topic-Based Communication Space Petri Net framework separates:
- **Semantic reasoning**: Adaptive, software-driven (can change)
- **Coordination semantics**: Deterministic, hardware-enforced (cannot be violated)

**Governance depth insight**: Software governance can be circumvented by software. Hardware governance cannot. For safety-critical domains, the governance depth problem has a physical dimension — governance must be enforced at a layer that the governed system cannot modify. This is the most extreme expression of the depth principle: push governance below the software stack entirely.

**Connection to library**: Extends Verification Substrate (#377): that scan identified verification as substrate, not quality gate. Hardware enforcement takes this literally — the governance substrate is silicon, not code. Also connects to the Sovereignty Stack (scans #255-258): hardware sovereignty (South Korea $1T chip investment) is the deepest layer; this paper shows it's also the most governance-resistant layer.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02376

### 6. Self-GC: Self-Governing Context for Long-Horizon Agents (2607.00692) — HIGH

**Core contribution**: Context management is **runtime lifecycle control over indexed, recoverable objects**, not post-hoc text cleanup. Self-GC turns user turns, tool spans, and skill state into indexed objects with a side-channel planner that proposes fold/mask/prune actions, while the harness enforces safe commit boundaries and cache-aware recovery.

**Quantitative results**:
- **Hard Set** (33 sessions): 43.95% token pruning, 84.85% future continuations unaffected (vs 54.55-69.70% for heuristic baselines)
- **Production suite** (332 sessions): 91.27-94.58% no-impact rate across three planner backbones (vs 77.71-87.46% for baselines)
- **Online deployment**: 10-15% average input token reduction, peak ~20%

**Governance depth insight**: Heuristic context management (chronological pruning, tool-output masking, near-limit summarization) operates at the text surface — it can see what's longest or oldest but not what's *needed by future decisions*. Self-GC operates at the object lifecycle layer — it can reason about dependencies, recoverable sidecars, and commit boundaries. The depth gap: heuristics have 23-37% higher impact rates (pruning tokens that will be needed) because they govern text, not objects.

**Connection to library**: Extends Accumulation Paradox (#387): DRIFTLENS showed accumulated memory corrupts reasoning trajectories. Self-GC offers architectural resolution — context objects with lifecycle management don't accumulate blindly; they're governed by dependency-aware lifecycle policy. Also extends Cost Inversion Threshold (#404): when token costs approach engineer compensation, 10-15% token reduction has direct economic value. Self-GC makes the sovereignty stack cheaper to operate.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00692

## Structural Synthesis: The Depth Principle

### Five Governance Layers (Deepest to Shallowest)

1. **Hardware** (2607.02376): Deterministic, physically enforced. Cannot be circumvented by software. Appropriate for safety-critical real-time coordination.
2. **Legal/Evidentiary** (2607.00941): Typed relations mapping events to legal categories. Records must carry the relations needed for determinations, not just event logs.
3. **Organizational** (2607.01421): Decision rights, escalation structures, boundary contracts between probabilistic and deterministic systems.
4. **Computational/Causal** (2607.01767, 2607.01661): Causal amplifier identification for repair; designed information asymmetry for deliberation. Operates on the structure of the problem, not its surface.
5. **Object Lifecycle** (2607.00692): Indexed, recoverable context objects with dependency-aware pruning. Governs information persistence and disposal.

### When Surface Governance Fails Silently

The pattern across all six papers:

| You think you're governing... | But actually... | The failure is... |
|------------------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| AI team risk | Boundary where probabilistic meets deterministic | Most severe failures at the organizational seam |
| Runtime records | Events without typed legal relations | Records exist but can't answer legal questions |
| Agent errors | Visible symptoms | Causal amplifier keeps re-generating symptoms |
| Multi-agent review | Identical-evidence herding | No genuine belief revision occurs |
| Software coordination | Non-deterministic timing | Safety-critical latency is unbounded |
| Context management | Text surface pruning | Future-dependency-blind deletion |

### Design Principle

**Governance depth audit**: Before deploying any governance mechanism, ask:
1. At which layer does the failure actually occur?
2. At which layer does the governance mechanism operate?
3. If they differ, the governance creates false confidence.

This is a more rigorous version of the Verification Substrate principle (#377): verification is not a quality gate — it must live at the same layer as the failure it's meant to prevent. The Governance Depth Problem generalizes this: every governance mechanism has a "depth" (the layer it controls), and every failure has a "depth" (the layer it occurs at). Mismatch = silent failure.
