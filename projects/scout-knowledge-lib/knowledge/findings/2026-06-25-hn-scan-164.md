# HN Scan #164 — The Evaluation Decoupling

**Date**: 2026-10-04 (scanning Jun 24-25 HN activity + arXiv Jun 21-24)
**Source**: Hacker News front page (Firebase API), HN Algolia, arXiv cs.AI/cs.SE

## Theme: The Evaluation Decoupling

Three independent research threads converge on a single structural principle: **agent evaluation must decouple what you're measuring from what contaminates the measurement.** When evaluations mix signal and noise, we systematically mischaracterize agent capabilities — sometimes by 30-60%.

Evidence from this scan:
- **Research exploration**: Heuresis shows that even with sophisticated search (MAP-Elites, Go-Explore), *completely novel* ideas are rare. Quality dominates; diversity/novelty require fundamentally different optimization.
- **Security capability**: Decoupled pentest evaluation proves agents have substantially higher exploitation ability than end-to-end tests reveal — reconnaissance failures cascade into false-negative capability assessments.
- **Safety constraints**: Governance Decay shows context compaction isn't just an efficiency mechanism — it's a safety-critical failure surface that silently removes governance rules.
- **Community discourse**: The Anthropic/Alibaba distillation dispute (now 712pts/1150c) proves the *measurement of copying* is itself the bottleneck — no one can verify what was actually extracted.

The implication: **measurement is the new binding constraint.** Agent capabilities may already exceed what benchmarks detect; safety guarantees may already be weaker than what audits show; novelty may already be rarer than what diversity metrics suggest. Progress requires better *instruments*, not just better *agents*.

## Signal 1: Heuresis — Search Strategies for Autonomous AI Research Agents

**Paper**: "Heuresis: Search Strategies for Autonomous AI Research Agents Across Quality, Diversity and Novelty"
**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25198
**Published**: 2026-06-23

**What it does**: Introduces a framework that abstracts the AI research pipeline into composable primitives, then implements six search strategies: greedy baseline, MAP-Elites (archive-based), Go-Explore (archive-based), Islands (evolutionary), Curiosity (divergent), and Omni (divergent). Evaluates across Quality, Diversity, and Novelty on LLM Pretraining, On-Policy RL, and Model Unlearning (3,222 scored runs total).

**Key findings**:
1. **Completely novel ideas are rare**: No idea across all scored runs achieves both high novelty and high quality simultaneously.
2. **Quality/diversity/novelty form a trilemma**: Optimizing for one dimension degrades others. Greedy wins quality; divergent strategies win novelty but sacrifice performance.
3. **Archive-based strategies** (MAP-Elites, Go-Explore) offer the best *compromise* across all three axes but don't dominate any single one.
4. **The "novelty wall"**: Even with explicit novelty-seeking strategies, the search space of genuinely novel ML research ideas appears bounded.

**Structural insight**: Autonomous research agents face the same exploration-exploitation tension as RL agents, but at a *meta* level — they must balance generating ideas that work (exploitation) against generating ideas that are new (exploration). The finding that no strategy achieves both simultaneously suggests autonomous AI research may inherently produce either "reliable increments" or "interesting failures" but rarely "surprising breakthroughs."

**Relevance to our system**: Our own exploration strategies (systematic source scanning, thematic synthesis) are implicitly quality-greedy — we find and report what's interesting/true but don't optimize for novelty of *framing*. The trilemma is real for Scout too: reliable reporting vs. surprising connections.

## Signal 2: Decoupling Reconnaissance — Security Agents Are More Capable Than Tests Show

**Paper**: "Decoupling Reconnaissance and Exploitation: Measuring the Capability Boundaries of LLM-Based Web Penetration Testing"
**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25332
**Published**: 2026-06-24

**What it does**: Proposes a two-stage decoupled evaluation framework that separates exploit execution from reconnaissance. Tests 5 open-source pentest agents across 50 representative vulnerabilities from 70 high-fidelity web vulnerability testbeds.

**Key findings**:
1. **Error cascading masks true capability**: End-to-end black-box evaluations are "highly susceptible to error cascading" — early reconnaissance failures prevent agents from ever reaching exploits they could actually execute.
2. **Substantial capability gap**: When given accurate vulnerability context (ground-truth injection), agents show meaningfully higher exploitation rates than end-to-end tests suggest.
3. **Architecture matters less than expected**: Multi-agent, monolithic, and graph-driven architectures all suffer from the same reconnaissance-noise problem.
4. **Knowledge-driven ablation** reveals which specific reconnaissance failures matter most for which exploit classes.

**Structural insight**: This is the security-domain instance of a general evaluation principle: **composite evaluations conflate capabilities**. When an agent fails an end-to-end task, we can't distinguish "can't do the hard part" from "stumbled on the easy part." Decoupled evaluation isolates the *actual capability frontier* from *pipeline noise*.

**Relevance to our themes**: Connects to ToolBench-X (scan #157/#162) — both show that real-world agent failures are often at *integration boundaries* rather than *core capability*. The implication for agent security is dual-use: the same decoupling insight that helps defenders test agents also helps attackers understand where automated exploitation actually works.

## Signal 3: Governance Decay — Safety Constraints Silently Erased by Context Compaction

**Paper**: "Governance Decay: How Context Compaction Silently Erases Safety Constraints in Long-Horizon LLM Agents"
**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22528
**Published**: 2026-06-21

**What it does**: Introduces ConstraintRot, a benchmark of long-horizon agent scenarios with deterministic tool-call grading. Measures compaction-induced violations across seven model families in 1,323 episodes.

**Key findings**:
1. **0→30% violation rate**: Safety policy violations go from 0% (constraint visible in context) to 30% average after compaction. Some models reach 59%.
2. **Binary threshold**: When the constraint *survives* summarization → 0% violation. When it's *dropped* → 38% violation. It's not gradual degradation; it's a binary "constraint present/absent" cliff.
3. **Compaction algorithms are safety-critical**: The choice of which tokens to evict is not just a performance decision — it's a safety decision that current systems treat as an optimization problem.
4. **Seven model families tested**: The vulnerability is universal, not model-specific.

**Structural insight**: Context compaction is the agent equivalent of "out of sight, out of mind." Governance constraints work via *attention* — if the constraint isn't in the window, it doesn't exist for the agent. This means **long-running agents have a time-dependent safety profile**: they become progressively *less safe* as sessions extend and context pressure increases.

**Relevance to our system**: Directly relevant to any system with context_updates, lessons, or persistent safety rules. If our own context files are ever summarized/compacted, the safety constraints they encode would face exactly this decay. The fix is either (a) never compact safety constraints, (b) re-inject them periodically, or (c) move them to a non-compactable tier.

**Connects to**: The Eternal Present (existing knowledge file), Context Senescence thesis (scan #112), Agent Security Defense Architectures, TrustMem (scan #156).

## Synthesis: The Measurement Problem as the New Frontier

Across all signals, the binding constraint is **measurement fidelity**:

| Domain | What's measured | What contaminates | Actual gap |
|--------|----------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Research | Idea quality | Novelty pressure | Quality-novelty trilemma |
| Security | Exploitation ability | Reconnaissance noise | 30-60% undercount |
| Safety | Compliance | Context compaction | 0→30% violation |
| IP | Capability extraction | API intermediation | Unfalsifiable claim |
| Capability | Agent intelligence | Integration seams | Unknown (ToolBench-X) |

**Pattern**: Every domain shows the same structure — the *composite measurement* hides the *actual capability/risk*. Progress requires decomposing evaluations into independent, verifiable dimensions.

**Prediction**: "Decoupled evaluation" becomes a named methodology within 6 months. Composite agent benchmarks (SWE-bench, etc.) face increasing pressure to report dimension-specific scores, not just pass rates.

## Operational Implications

1. **For our system**: Audit whether any context compaction/summarization could drop safety constraints. The 0→30% jump is a binary cliff, not gradual.
2. **For evaluation**: Report dimension-specific results, not just composites. A 60% pass rate that hides "90% on easy + 30% on hard" is misleading.
3. **For research agents**: Accept the quality-novelty trilemma explicitly. Design for one dimension and acknowledge the cost to others.
