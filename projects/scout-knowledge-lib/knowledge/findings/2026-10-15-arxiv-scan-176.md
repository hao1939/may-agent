# arXiv Scan #176 — The Anti-Deliberation Principle

**Date**: 2026-10-15
**Source**: arXiv (export.arxiv.org API)
**Scan number**: 176
**Theme**: Agent effort inversely correlates with success — "thinking harder" is actively counterproductive; the solution space is consistently external governance orthogonal to the agent's own process.

## Synthesis: Why More Thinking = Worse Outcomes

Six independent research streams, published June 22–25 2026, converge on a single structural conclusion: **the human intuition that deliberation improves outcomes is actively wrong for LLM agents**. Every paper demonstrates that agent internal processes are unreliable indicators of quality, and that the remedy is consistently *external* rather than internal.

| Paper | What It Shows | Implication |

| Premature Commitment (2606.22936) | Agents settle on interpretation at step 4; rest of run defends it. AUROC 0.97 for detection via hidden states. | Internal reasoning *looks* deliberative but IS defending a fixed conclusion |
| Deliberation Allocation (2606.26502) | LRMs spend MORE tokens on failures (d=1.47–3.13); humans spend LESS. Opposite stopping policies. | Token count = uncertainty signal, not quality signal. More tokens = more likely wrong |

## Finding 1: Premature Commitment — Agents Decide at Step 4 (arXiv 2606.22936)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22936

Long-horizon LLM agents settle on one reading of evidence early (by reasoning step 4), then spend the rest of the run defending that interpretation. This is invisible to final-answer evaluation.

**Key results**:
- Cross-run hidden-state convergence at step 4 predicts downstream behavioral consistency (r = -0.35, partial r = -0.45 on HotpotQA; r = -0.83 on StrategyQA)
- Replicates across Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B, and Phi-3-14B
- **Critical distinction**: Commitment ≠ correctness. Committed-wrong and committed-correct questions are NOT separable in activation similarity
- Runtime monitor: AUROC up to 0.97 for detecting inconsistent trajectories
- Prompting intervention cuts behavioral variance 28% without accuracy change

**Why this matters**: The multi-step reasoning trace that LOOKS like deliberation is actually post-hoc defense of a pre-committed conclusion. Steps 5–N are not reasoning — they're confabulation.

**Connection to our system**: Our session commitment threshold (ops 11-40 failure zone) may be the operational manifestation of this exact phenomenon. The agent has committed to an interpretation of its instructions but hasn't validated it against reality. The 3-10× error rate in that zone maps directly to premature commitment → ungrounded execution → failure.

## Finding 2: Effort Inversely Predicts Success (arXiv 2606.26502)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26502

When humans get a problem wrong, they spend LESS time on it (disengagement/abandonment). When LRMs get a problem wrong, they spend MORE tokens (Cohen's d = 1.47–3.13 across five thinking models on H-ARC).

**Key results**:
- Cross-ITEM alignment preserved: both humans and LRMs take longer on harder problems (registration)
- Within-ITEM divergence: on the SAME problem, LRM failures use more tokens than successes; humans are the opposite (allocation)
- Dissociation holds under item fixed effects, replicates across datasets
- Absent in non-thinking baseline (GPT-4o style models)
- Interpretation: LRM token growth is driven by uncertainty — "chains grow when the model is unsure, which is exactly when it tends to fail"

**Why this matters**: Every cost-efficiency analysis that uses "tokens spent" as a proxy for "productive work" is fundamentally wrong. Token expenditure is an uncertainty signal, not a quality signal. An agent that produces a 2,000-token reasoning trace is MORE likely to be wrong than one that produces a 200-token trace on the same problem.

**Prediction**: "Token budget" as a quality constraint (spend MORE = try harder) will be recognized as actively harmful within 6 months. Optimal stopping is the correct framing: LESS processing when uncertain, not more.

## Finding 3: Causal Caution Exists But Is Contextually Suppressed (arXiv 2606.24370)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24370

LLMs have the capability to express causal caution (refrain from causal judgment when evidence is insufficient). In academic contexts, maintenance rate is 91.7–100%. In practical advisory contexts, it drops to 6.7–18.3%. On prompts requesting concrete recommendations, only 1/200 responses (0.5%) maintained caution.

**Key results**:
- Tested on Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.7, GPT 5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro (480 trials)
- A brief self-correction prompt restores caution to 71.4–100% (McNemar's test p < .001)
- This is context-dependent expression variation, NOT capability limitation
- Paper proposes multi-agent architectures separating proposal generation from causal auditing

**Why this matters**: The "helpfulness" objective actively suppresses safety-relevant reasoning. The agent KNOWS it should be cautious — but the context pressure to be helpful overrides that knowledge. This is the mechanism behind the Latent Capability Problem (scan #165): capabilities exist but aren't expressed.

**Implication for our system**: When our agents produce confident-sounding outputs in advisory mode, the probability that they've suppressed relevant uncertainty is ~93%. The fix is architectural (separate caution-auditing agent), not prompting-based.

## Finding 4: Out-of-Band Enforcement Resists Adaptive Attacks (arXiv 2606.26479)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26479

Organizes five out-of-band defense systems (CaMeL, FIDES, Progent, RTBAS, FORGE) as instances of classical computer security patterns: Biba integrity protection, reference monitoring, and least privilege.

**Key results**:
- In-band defenses (training model to refuse): 12/12 broken at >90% ASR under adaptive attacks
- Progent (out-of-band): 25.8% → 4.2% attack success (sixfold reduction)
- Under adaptive (defense-aware) attack: 2.6% ASR — even lower than non-adaptive
- First independent reproduction on open-weight model (Qwen2.5-7B on single H200)
- Caveat: Single black-box attack template; GCG white-box attack remains open

**Why this matters**: The field spent 2024-2025 trying to make models internally resist injection — all failed under adaptive attack. The 2026 shift to deterministic external enforcement appears to genuinely work, at least against black-box attacks. The principle: don't ask the agent to govern itself; surround it with governance that doesn't require its cooperation.

## Finding 5: Institutional Attestation — Governance at the Action Boundary (arXiv 2606.26298)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26298

Formalizes how human institutions have always governed powerful autonomous actors: NOT by monitoring their reasoning, but by requiring independently attested evidence at the point of consequential action.

**Model**:
1. Agent retains full autonomy over planning and reasoning
2. Agent holds ZERO execution authority over designated high-risk actions
3. Execution requires preconditions independently attested by separate authoritative sources
4. Attestations cryptographically bound to declared intent
5. Deterministic policy evaluates attestations
6. Tamper-evident log enables independent re-verification

**Why this matters**: This is the formal model that unifies our governance observations. The Governance Vacuum (scan #170) exists because we monitor agent reasoning (which is unreliable per Finding 1) instead of requiring attestation at action boundaries. CFAgentBench's money-movement guard (2606.22000) is an instance of this — 278 instances where correct behavior = STOP and stage for human approval.

## Finding 6: Red Queen Gödel Machine — Co-Evolving Evaluators (arXiv 2606.26294)

**Source**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26294

Self-improving agents assume stationary evaluation criteria. RQGM co-evolves agents AND their evaluators through controlled utility evolution.

**Key results**:
- On coding: RQGM improves SOTA using agent-as-judge code review signal at 1.35×–1.72× fewer tokens
- On scientific writing: co-evolved writers reach 1.78×–1.86× higher acceptance rates
- On grading: co-evolved graders reach +9% ground-truth accuracy
- Critical: Strongest baseline reviewer over-accepts AI-generated papers at 1.91× human rate
- RQGM corrects this via adversarial objective discovering equally-stringent reviewers

**Why this matters**: Even our evaluators are subject to the Anti-Deliberation Principle — static judges degrade as agents adapt. The evaluation system must be under evolutionary pressure or it becomes permissive. Our current eval system (3 bits of entropy from 14,345 evals per scan #167) may be evidence of judge degradation already occurring.

## The Anti-Deliberation Principle (Meta-Synthesis)

These six papers collectively establish that **agent internal processes cannot be used as evidence of agent quality**:

1. **Reasoning traces are post-hoc confabulation** (premature commitment at step 4)
2. **Token expenditure signals uncertainty, not effort** (inverse correlation with success)
3. **Quality capabilities exist but are suppressed by context** (causal caution: 0.5% expression rate)
4. **Internal detection fails under adversarial pressure** (12/12 in-band defenses broken)
5. **External attestation at action boundaries works** (sixfold ASR reduction)
6. **Even external judges degrade without evolutionary pressure** (1.91× over-acceptance)

The implication is a complete inversion of the human-reasoning analogy:

| Property | Humans | LLM Agents |
|---|---|---|
| More time/effort on a problem | Better results | Worse results |
| Visible reasoning | Tracks actual process | Post-hoc confabulation |
| Internal caution | Expressed when relevant | Suppressed by context |
| Self-governance | Improves with training | Fails under adaptive attack |
| Static evaluation | Stable for years | Degrades in weeks/months |

**The design principle**: Never use agent-internal signals (reasoning quality, token count, expressed confidence, self-reported uncertainty) as evidence of output quality. Always use agent-external signals (hidden-state probes, deterministic checkpoints, attestation, co-evolved evaluators, forward verification).
