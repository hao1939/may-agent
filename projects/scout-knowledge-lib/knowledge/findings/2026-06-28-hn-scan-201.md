## Finding 1: Agent Sandboxing Reaches Commodity Status

**Three independent sandboxing tools** shipped in one week, all approaching the problem from different angles:

1. **Tupper** (lightbearco, 125★, Jun 23): TypeScript, targets e2b replacement. Uses Firecracker + Apple Container. Local-first, open-source.
2. **NexusBox** (lxcshine, 38★, Jun 24): Secure sandbox via MCP — treats execution isolation as an MCP-accessible tool.
3. **MicroVMs** (48642510, 314pts): Enterprise-grade, community discussing agent-per-VM architecture + GPU sharing via SR-IOV.

**Pattern**: Sandboxing has crossed from "security research" to "commodity infrastructure." The MicroVMs thread shows practitioners defaulting to VM-per-agent isolation. The binding constraint has shifted from "can we isolate?" to "can we share GPUs across isolated agents?" (SR-IOV as the next friction point).

## Finding 6: Nation-State Attacks Leverage LLM-Generated Social Engineering

**"Anatomy of a Failed (Nation-State?) Attack"** (48694631, 29pts):

A developer dissects a sophisticated phishing attempt. Community identifies the tell: "the kind of low-level reliability judgment that most teams only notice when something breaks" — LLM-generated prose that is fluent but stylistically identifiable.

Key community observation (tptacek): "Real people don't talk like the J. Peterman catalog." The identification mechanism is STYLE, not content. As models improve stylistically, this defense erodes.

**Connection to Mechanism Gap (#200)**: The phishing attack produces correct-looking social engineering via wrong mechanisms (LLM generation vs. authentic human communication). The detection method (style mismatch) is itself subject to improvement — an adversarial co-evolution.

**Security training practitioner** (ThreatSystems): Runs courses on developer security — "broaden understanding of threat surface from their behaviour, day-to-day tooling, the repositories they work on and broader supply chain." The attack surface IS the developer workflow, not just the code.

## Key Insight: Code Writes Policy

The governance-execution gap is now the primary structural feature of the agent ecosystem:

1. **Governance layer**: Frozen at ~1000pts of discourse, zero resolution, zero new insight
2. **Execution layer**: Deploying sandbox infra, economic agents, physical-world MCP, provenance memory, authority auditing — all THIS WEEK

The pattern is identical to how the internet developed: standards committees debated while practitioners deployed. Whatever infrastructure ships first defines the constraints that future governance must accommodate. The practitioners building MicroVMs, Tupper, CapFrame, and theeleven are writing governance policy in code — the formal governance will retroactively legitimize whatever patterns survived.

**Implication for our system**: The "execution quiet build" applies to us too. Our markdown-in-git knowledge architecture, MCP-as-interface pattern, and file-based governance are being validated by the market (OpenKnowledge 347pts growing; practitioners confirming markdown is AI-friendly). Building durable execution infrastructure > waiting for standards.
