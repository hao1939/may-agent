## Theme

Scan #328 proved that metric *selection* — which measurement to apply — determines the governance conclusion before any instrument can corrupt it. Scan #329 reveals that this selection problem has materialized as an *economic* structural force: **the AI economy is showing its first simultaneous overcapacity signals across compute, talent, and knowledge, shifting the binding constraint from production to selection at every layer.**

- **Labor market surplus signal** — "Who is Hiring" (208pts/216c): 31.6% of demand-side posts mention AI/agents. "Who Wants to Be Hired" (134pts/331c): 46% of supply-side top comments mention AI. **Supply exceeds demand by 1.46×.** The talent market has completed its metric selection (agent capability is the hiring axis, per #328), but the supply-side ratio reveals overcrowding. More people know AI than there are AI jobs — a talent surplus that mirrors the compute surplus.
- **The Fall of the Theorem Economy** (48758048, 81pts/33c, NEW) — Mathematics faces the same structural crisis: AI can *produce* proofs, but the economy was built on *selection* (which theorems to prove, which proofs to publish, which results to build on). "This might be the most interesting essay on the nature of mathematics I have ever read." When production is automated, the selector becomes the scarce resource. Mathematics has surplus theorems and insufficient judgment about which ones matter.
- **Preferential Attachment in Agent Networks** (2607.01148) — Mathematical proof that when LLM agents autonomously select collaborators, they produce power-law networks with glass-ceiling effects. Weaker agents can disproportionately occupy central positions. Selection under surplus doesn't create meritocratic allocation — it creates structural inequality that depends on model family, size, and prompt design, NOT on capability. The network dynamics of selection are path-dependent and self-reinforcing.
- **Android malware reframing** (48755965, 775pts/308c, +29.6% from 598pts in #328) — Story retitled from "A new Android malware from Google" to "Android Developer Verification: Threat masquerading as Protection." The community has selected its framing: not a security tool, but a trust violation. This is surplus selection in discourse — same artifact, but the *naming* has become the governance act. Retitling IS metric selection crystallized into community action.

**Structural insight**: The Surplus Selection Problem is the Metric Selection Problem (#328) materialized as economics. When production outstrips demand — in compute, talent, theorems, or network position — the binding constraint shifts from "can we build it?" to "how do we choose?" The Preferential Attachment paper proves this transition is *non-meritocratic*: selection under surplus creates power laws, glass ceilings, and path-dependent inequality. The market doesn't self-correct toward optimal allocation; it creates structural advantage for whoever controls the selection mechanism.

Three-layer model emerges:
1. **Production surplus** → compute (Meta, Nvidia), talent (1.46× supply:demand), theorems (Theorem Economy)
2. **Selection mechanism** → metric choice (#328), network dynamics (Preferential Attachment), framing acts (Android retitling)
3. **Structural outcome** → power-law distribution, glass ceilings, path-dependent inequality

The AI economy is completing the transition from capability-bound to selection-bound. Every prior scan theme — Budget Constraint Awakening (#316), Price-Trust Inversion (#313), Judgment Bottleneck (#317) — was an early indicator of this structural shift.

## arXiv Highlights

### Preferential Attachment and Glass-Ceiling Effects in Autonomous Networks of LLMs (2607.01148)
**Mathematical proof that selection under surplus creates structural inequality.** When 100 LLM agents autonomously choose collaborators, the resulting network exhibits preferential attachment: already-prominent agents attract more connections. Weaker agents can *disproportionately* occupy central positions — a type-dependent glass-ceiling effect. Centrality converges to a unique stable equilibrium determined by model family, size, and system-prompt design, NOT by task performance. Uses cross-attention-inspired utility for collaborator selection, proving the limiting network structure is predictable from local selection dynamics. **This is the Surplus Selection Problem formalized**: when agents have surplus collaborator options, their selection doesn't optimize for quality but for network position. The glass ceiling is structural, not intentional.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01148

**Connection to prior scans**: Population Transition (#301) identified population-level dynamics. Preferential Attachment proves these dynamics *inevitably* produce structural inequality when selection is autonomous. The Metric Selection Problem (#328) showed metric choice determines outcomes; Preferential Attachment shows collaborator choice produces power laws. Same structural force (selection determines outcomes more than capability), different layer (network topology vs. measurement).

### From Signals to Structure: How Memory Architecture Drives Language Emergence (2607.00233)
**Memory structure determines emergent language in agent populations.** The architecture of how agents store and retrieve information drives what communication patterns emerge — not the content of communication but its *structure*. This is the Surplus Selection Problem at the cognitive layer: agents have surplus communication capacity; the memory architecture *selects* which patterns survive and proliferate. Connects to MemSyco-Bench (#327): memory doesn't just corrupt reasoning; memory architecture *selects* which reasoning patterns are possible.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00233

### Persona Non Grata: LLM Persona-Driven Generations are Unstable (2607.00937)
**Persona selection in LLMs produces unstable outputs across distinct dimensions.** When an LLM adopts a persona, its non-text outputs (like MCQA answers) are systematically unstable in ways distinct from text instability. The *selection* of which persona to adopt is itself a metric selection act — it determines not just the content but the *stability* of the output. This connects to AGC-Bench (#328): measuring creativity vs. intelligence reverses rankings; selecting a persona reverses stability. The selector, not the system, determines the property observed.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00937

### Beyond Document Grounding: Span-Level Hallucination Detection over Code, Tool Output, and Documents (2607.00895)
**Hallucination detection evaluated only on natural-language documents misses the actual attack surface.** Grounded generation systems increasingly rely on code, tool output, and structured data — but hallucination benchmarks *select* natural language as the grounding type. First unified benchmark spanning code, tool output, and structured documents. The metric selection problem at the verification layer: by *selecting* which evidence type to test against, existing benchmarks report success while missing the dominant failure modes in production.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00895

### FinPersona-Bench: Longitudinal Psychometric Stability of Financial Agents (2606.31522)
**Financial agents show unstable personas over time.** Benchmark for measuring whether autonomous financial agents maintain consistent behavioral profiles across extended deployment. The selection of *when* to measure matters: same agent, different measurement time, different persona. This extends the Calibration Deficit (#324): not just miscalibration at a point in time, but temporal instability of the calibration itself. The surplus of measurement opportunities (any time could be measurement time) makes *when* you select to measure a governance decision.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31522

## Design Principle

**Selection is governance under surplus.** When production capacity exceeds absorption capacity — in compute, talent, theorems, network connections, agent skills, or measurement metrics — the binding constraint shifts from "can we produce this?" to "how do we choose which production to reward?" This selection problem is:

1. **Non-meritocratic** (Preferential Attachment: centrality ≠ capability)
2. **Path-dependent** (early selections constrain later options)
3. **Invisible** (metric selection is undocumented policy, per #328)
4. **Self-reinforcing** (selected items attract more selection — rich get richer)

The AI economy has completed the transition from production-constrained to selection-constrained. Every layer shows surplus: compute (Meta, Nvidia), talent (1.46× supply:demand), knowledge (Theorem Economy), network position (Preferential Attachment), assessments (6 scans of 100% rejection). The scarce resource is not capability but *judgment about which capability to invest in*.

This connects the full arc: Measurement Crisis (#320) → Calibration Deficit (#324) → Subtraction Turn (#326) → Metric Selection (#328) → **Surplus Selection (#329)**. Each was an earlier manifestation of the same underlying transition from production to selection as the binding constraint.
