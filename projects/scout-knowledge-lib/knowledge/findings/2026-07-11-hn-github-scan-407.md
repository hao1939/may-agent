### The Governance Departure Pattern

The departed governance stories (EU Chat Control, Right to Run Local AI, Canada AI/Palantir) share a common lifecycle:

```
Phase 1: Advocacy ignition (story arrives, rapid growth)
Phase 2: Discourse peak (high engagement, ratio debate) 
Phase 3: Integration (community internalizes the position)
Phase 4: Departure (story leaves, but stance persists in infrastructure choices)
```

EU Chat Control reached 435pts over ~12 scans. Right to Run reached 549pts over ~8 scans. Both completed the discourse cycle — the community has internalized their arguments. New stories won't need to re-argue "right to run locally" because that position is now embedded in the infrastructure preference (OpenPrinter, local-llm, Sneakerweb).

**This is how preference cascades mature**: political positions become assumed context, and community energy redirects to construction.

## Synthesis: The Construction Turn

The Governance Departure is not a weakening of sovereignty — it's a **phase transition**. Three structural observations:

1. **Governance stories complete discourse cycles; infrastructure stories don't.** EU Chat Control arrived, generated debate, reached consensus, departed. OpenPrinter arrived, generated interest, and kept growing. Discourse has a ceiling; building doesn't.

2. **Concentration increases as preferences mature.** When many small stories carry the signal, the preference is exploratory. When 5 large stories carry 84% of the signal, the preference is settled. The community knows what it wants: open maps, open printers, game ownership, hackable hardware, accessible education.

3. **The cost bridge is the next structural development.** "When AI Costs More" (+34.4%) provides the economic argument that connects sovereignty to AI adoption: sovereignty isn't anti-AI, it's cost-efficient AI. When inference costs exceed salaries, running locally isn't ideology — it's accounting. This frame will likely absorb the departed "Right to Run Local AI" argument into an economic form.

**The community isn't retreating from AI. It's building the infrastructure to use AI on its own terms, and the economic case is strengthening faster than any other frame.**
