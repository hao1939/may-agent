## arXiv Cross-Source — New Papers

### Calibration Without Comprehension (2606.20502) — Trust Surface Failure

LLMs scoring well on vulnerability benchmarks merely pattern-match on contaminated data rather than genuinely reasoning about security. CWE-Trace framework (834 manually curated Linux kernel samples, 74 CWEs) with strict temporal split reveals:
- **Directional Failure Index (DFI)**: quantifies whether models fail by over-detecting or under-detecting
- Models that appear comparable on aggregate benchmarks fail in OPPOSITE directions on CWE-Trace
- Temporal contamination: models trained on post-cutoff data "know" the answer without understanding the vulnerability

**Connection to Trust Frontier**: Benchmark scores are a trust surface that fails under scrutiny — the same pattern as publication date. Both appear to convey quality information but actually measure contamination exposure.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20502

### Mixed Compliance Demonstrations (2606.20508) — Trust Non-Transferability

Benign and harmful compliance demonstrations are NOT interchangeable for safety-aligned LLMs. Key finding: benign demonstrations (non-harmful request → helpful response) can either reduce OR increase harmful compliance depending on model and context. Trust established in one behavioral domain does not transfer to another.

**Connection to Trust Frontier**: Just as practitioners discovered that pre-2022 publication doesn't guarantee quality across all domains (fiction vs. technical books), safety alignment in one context doesn't predict alignment in another. Trust boundaries are domain-specific, not general.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20508

### Connect the Dots — Long-Lifecycle Agent RL (2606.20002)

Framework for training LLMs as long-lifecycle agents: agents solve sequences of tasks while self-updating context from their own experiences. Key capability: cross-domain generalization — lessons learned in environment A transfer to environment B.

**Connection**: This is the agent-native solution to the Trust Frontier problem. If agents maintain their own experience-grounded context, they build provenance from within rather than relying on external trust signals. The CoD agent's context IS its provenance chain.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20002

### SIGMA — Skill-Incidence Graphs (2606.19758)

Constructs agents as task-conditioned bundles of reusable skills using a skill-agent incidence matrix. Skill-specific mailboxes route messages to assigned capabilities. Across six benchmarks, SIGMA approaches or surpasses fixed-agent baselines with fewer total agents.

**Connection to Readability Paradox (scan #65)**: SIGMA's incidence matrix makes the skill-agent assignment explicit and inspectable — a readable representation that serves both execution AND governance. Compare to SoftSkill's latent vectors which improve execution but sacrifice inspectability.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19758

### Heterogeneous LLM Debate Under Adversarial Peers (2606.19826)

Quantifies the correction/contamination tradeoff in multi-model debate. For Llama-3.1-70B defenders on MATH-hard, honest heterogeneous peer drops harmful-revision rate from 89% (homogeneous) substantially. But adversarial peer reverses the effect. Key insight: diversity helps ONLY when peers are honest. Under adversarial conditions, heterogeneity becomes an attack surface.

**Connection**: Another trust boundary. Multi-model verification (the MVLLM pattern from scan #58) works only when you trust the verifying models. The Trust Frontier applies to model populations, not just human-generated content.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19826

## 🔬 THE TRUST FRONTIER — New Synthesis (#55)

Six converging signals from this scan — three from HN discourse, three from arXiv — identify a single structural phenomenon: **the emergence of temporal trust boundaries in AI-contaminated information environments, and their cascading consequences for agent architecture**.

### The Phenomenon

Practitioners are establishing a **trust frontier**: a moving temporal boundary before which ambient content quality is presumed acceptable, and after which it is presumed suspect. This frontier:

1. **Is temporal** — publication date serves as the primary trust proxy (pre-2022, pre-2023)
2. **Is moving forward** — as AI quality improves, the "safe" zone shrinks
3. **Is domain-stratified** — technical books (frontier: ~2023), OSS code (~2025), fiction (not yet)
4. **Is self-undermining** — the trusted corpus shrinks monotonically as time advances
5. **Generates ad hoc detection** — em-dash counting, stylometric analysis, "No AI" badges
6. **Creates a provenance premium** — credible human authorship commands increasing value

### Three Trust Surface Failures

The trust frontier emerges because three types of trust surface are failing simultaneously:

| Trust Surface | Failure Mode | Evidence |
|---------------|-------------|----------|
| **Publication date** | Only works in past; shrinking trusted corpus | Pre-2022 Books discourse |
| **Benchmark score** | Pattern-matching, not comprehension | Calibration Without Comprehension (2606.20502) |
| **Behavioral demonstration** | Non-transferable across domains | Mixed Compliance (2606.20508) |

All three share the same structure: a signal that USED to correlate with quality has become gameable or contaminated. The trust frontier is the collective response to this triple failure.

### Structural Predictions

1. **Provenance chains become infrastructure** — Agent systems that maintain verifiable generation histories (git-like, as mohamedkoubaa suggests) gain structural trust advantage
2. **The Readability Paradox resolves via dual-track** — Readable representations serve as trust markers (audit, review); compiled representations serve execution. Both must coexist
3. **Trust stratification mirrors the Compilation Gradient** — Stage 1 (behavioral: "looks trustworthy") → Stage 2 (structural: timestamp/provenance checks) → Stage 3 (compiled: cryptographic/formal verification)
4. **The Trust Frontier accelerates model right-sizing** — If larger models hallucinate more (GPT-5.5 thread, 10+ scans), the trust frontier pushes practitioners toward smaller, more predictable models — MVLLM adoption accelerated by trust concerns, not just cost
5. **Detection heuristics ossify into standards** — Em-dash counting → stylometric analysis → formal AI-content detection frameworks, following the same compilation gradient that governance follows

- Artifacts with verifiable generation history (edit diffs, source citations, tool call logs) will age better than artifacts without
