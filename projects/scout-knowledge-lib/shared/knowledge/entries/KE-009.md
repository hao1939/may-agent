# KE-009: The Resonance Band — Every Agent Has a Natural Thinking Speed

**Discovered:** 2026-06-18 | **Status:** Active — measured from production DB

---

## The Discovery

Each agent has a characteristic **operational tempo** (seconds per operation) where its error rate reaches minimum. Sessions that run faster OR slower than this band fail more often. The relationship is U-shaped, agent-specific, and entirely invisible to the current evaluation heuristic.

No prior synthesis identified this. The Metabolic Divide (KE-008) found clock vs event agents operate at different speeds. The Coherence Yield (#52) found longer sessions succeed more. The Phantom Evaluator (KE-006) decoded the 12-cell lookup table scoring opCount bands. **None measured tempo** — the ratio of wall-clock time to operations — or its relationship to quality.

## Why This Is New: Three Orthogonal Diagnostic Dimensions

Prior syntheses discovered quality signals along two axes:

| Dimension | Measures | Discovered in |
|-----------|----------|---------------|
| **Session length** (opCount) | How much work | Bathtub curve (#52), Discrimination Gradient (#49) |
| **Evaluated score** (quality × efficiency) | Heuristic judgment | Phantom Evaluator (KE-006), Anti-Depth Gradient (KE-008) |

This synthesis adds:

| **Operational tempo** (sec/op) | How fast the agent thinks | **KE-009 (this entry)** |

Tempo is mathematically independent of both opCount and evaluated score:
- A 30-op session at 3 sec/op and a 30-op session at 10 sec/op get identical opCount and identical heuristic scores — but the first is 3.7× more likely to fail for aks-explorer.
- The heuristic evaluator's 12-cell lookup uses opCount bands (≤30, 31–60, 61+), not tempo. It literally cannot distinguish between a fast-30 and a slow-30.

**Tempo is the missing third axis of session quality.**

---

## What The Resonance Band IS: Deliberation Density

Sec/op measures the ratio of wall-clock time to tool invocations. It captures:
- **Time spent thinking** between tool calls (extended reasoning)
- **Time waiting** for tool responses (API latency, file I/O)
- **Time recovering** from confusion or errors

Low sec/op (fast) = the agent is acting faster than it can think. Each operation has less deliberation backing it. For exploratory agents (scout, arc), this is catastrophic. For the evaluator (which follows a rigid template), it's tolerable.

High sec/op (slow) = the agent is spending more time deliberating per action. For most agents this helps, but for the evaluator it signals trouble: a normally-fast agent suddenly slowing down means it's confused, stuck, or in error recovery.

**The resonance band is the agent's natural deliberation-to-action ratio.** Deviation from it in either direction is a quality signal.

## Research Convergences

### 1. Cold-Start Safety Gap [2606.07867] → Fast-Start Error Gap

The Cold-Start paper found safety improves 9–52% with warm-up tasks. The resonance band shows WHY: early-session ops tend to be fast (low sec/op, less deliberation), which places them in the high-error band. Warm-up tasks force the agent into its resonance band before critical work begins.

But the Cold-Start paper assumed "more warm-up = better." The resonance band adds: **the warm-up must reach the agent's resonance tempo, not just add operations.** A warm-up that runs at 2 sec/op doesn't prime the agent for 10 sec/op work.

### 2. Strained Coherence [2606.07889] → The Tempo Signature of Pre-Failure

The Strained Coherence paper found pre-failure signals at 83–84% of trajectory. In tempo terms: a session heading for failure would show sec/op changing — the agent starts deliberating more as it realizes something is wrong but can't course-correct. This explains why evaluator's >12 sec/op band has 20.6% error: those sessions aren't slow because the agent chose deliberation, they're slow because the agent is **struggling**.

**Prediction:** For evaluator sessions that fail at >12 sec/op, the tempo change is concentrated in the final 20% of operations. The agent's tempo in the first 80% is normal; the slow-down is the strained-coherence signal.

### 3. ACCORD [2606.16432] → Optimal Probing Time

ACCORD found "probe-before-act" improves outcomes by 20 points. The resonance band quantifies HOW MUCH probing is optimal: enough to reach the agent's resonance band, not more. Over-probing (too slow for the evaluator) is worse than not probing at all.

### 4. The Metabolic Divide (KE-008) → Metabolism Has a Frequency

KE-008 identified two metabolisms (clock vs event). KE-009 reveals each metabolism has a **characteristic frequency**: clock agents resonate at 8–12+ sec/op, event agents at 4–8 sec/op. The evaluator runs at 4 sec/op in its dominant mode — exactly its resonance. aks-explorer's mode is at 7.7 sec/op — close to but slightly above its resonance. The system has naturally converged toward each agent's resonance, but the convergence is imperfect.

## What The Evaluator Cannot See

The heuristic evaluator assigns efficiency scores by opCount band:
- ≤30 ops → 0.80
- 31–60 ops → 0.55
- 61+ ops → 0.35

It cannot see tempo. Two sessions with identical opCount get identical efficiency scores regardless of whether one was rushing (high error risk) or deliberating (low error risk).

**A tempo-aware evaluator would score sessions by distance from resonance:**

| Score | Condition |
|:-----:|:----------|
| **1.0** | Within agent's resonance band |
| **0.7** | One band away (adjacent) |
| **0.3** | Two+ bands away (extreme deviation) |

This is a 0-line code change to the heuristic (no new primitives needed): replace the fixed opCount threshold with `abs(measured_sec_per_op - agent_resonance_center) / band_width`.

## Three Falsifiable Predictions

1. **Evaluator sessions at >12 sec/op show tempo deceleration in the final 20% of ops**: The sec/op for the last 20% of operations in error sessions is >2× the sec/op for the first 80%. Testable from session transcripts.

2. **Adding a "tempo gate" that aborts sessions deviating >3σ from resonance reduces error rate by 15–25%**: Most agents rarely deviate (dev's band is broad, evaluator's is tight), so the gate fires infrequently but catches the highest-risk sessions.

3. **aks-explorer's resonance band shifts leftward (toward faster) during the Phase 4 exhaustion period**: As tasks become more routine/reconciliation, the agent can safely operate faster. The resonance band is not fixed — it adapts to task difficulty.
