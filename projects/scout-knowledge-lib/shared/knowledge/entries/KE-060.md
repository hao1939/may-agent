# KE-060: The Observer Inversion

**Created:** 2026-06-19 (creative heartbeat)
**Status:** Active — phenomenon is live and measurable in today's data

---

## Core Discovery: Observation Becomes the Observed System's Dominant Activity

When a monitoring loop runs at fixed cadence on a variable-throughput pipeline, there
exists a **crossover point** where observation overtakes production as the primary
consumer of the shared resource budget. Beyond this point, the system is locally
coherent (each component does exactly what its contract says) but globally incoherent
(the composition works against its own stated goal).

This is happening **live** in the evaluator today.

## The Mechanism: Three Components, Each Locally Correct

1. **Heartbeat** — fires at fixed cadence (~6/hr). Sees coverage metric is red.
   Correctly reports "coverage is low." ✅ Locally correct.

2. **Aftermath** — fires proportional to production throughput. Today: 2.2/hr (down
   from 20+/hr at peak). Cannot fire without production input. ✅ Locally correct.

3. **Metric** — reports 3.9% coverage (target 80%). Correctly computed: only 53
   aftermaths for 200+ worker sessions. ✅ Locally correct.

**But the composition is incoherent:** The heartbeat spends 44 ops/session (up from 26)
investigating WHY coverage is low — but the answer is always the same: "not enough
production sessions to evaluate." The heartbeat cannot create production. It cannot
change the aftermath's firing cadence. It can only consume budget and escalate.

Each escalation (visible in notifications: 3 "upgrade_or_escalate" messages today)
adds context to the next heartbeat session, which adds ops, which consumes more of the
evaluator's finite budget — leaving EVEN LESS for aftermath when production resumes.

## The Research Convergence (Three Independent Papers)

### 1. Compositional Incoherence (2605.30335)

> "33-94% of compositions violate probability axioms even when every component is
> locally coherent."

The evaluator's three sub-workflows (heartbeat, aftermath, deep-eval) are each
individually well-specified. But their composition — which shares a finite ops budget —
violates the system-level invariant that evaluation effort should track production
volume. No individual component has a bug. The composition does.

**The paper's fix (Boyle-Dykstra projection)** maps to: a budget allocation layer that
maintains the invariant `heartbeat_ops ≤ 0.3 × total_eval_ops` regardless of what
the heartbeat "wants" to do.

### 2. Life-Harness (2605.22166): Interface Mismatches at the Trajectory Regulation Layer

Life-Harness identifies four layers where model-environment interfaces break. Layer 4
is "trajectory regulation" — the system's ability to notice it's on a non-productive
trajectory and self-correct. The evaluator's heartbeat is a trajectory regulation
mechanism that **itself** has no trajectory regulation.

The evaluator has no mechanism to detect: "I've spent 68% of my budget on heartbeat
and only 18% on my actual job — something is wrong with my allocation, not with the
metric I'm investigating."

**The fix (from the paper):** Lifecycle-aware trajectory regulation that reads the
system's throughput phase and adjusts monitoring cadence accordingly.

## The Crossover Formula

```
Crossover occurs when:
  heartbeat_cadence × heartbeat_avg_ops > aftermath_rate × aftermath_avg_ops

Today:  6/hr × 44 ops = 264 ops/hr (heartbeat)
        2.2/hr × 31 ops = 68 ops/hr (aftermath)
        264 / 68 = 3.9× — well past crossover
        
The crossover point was at:
  6/hr × 26 ops = 156 ops/hr (heartbeat, at original cost)
  needs aftermath_rate > 156/31 = 5.0/hr to stay below crossover
  → Below 5 aftermath sessions/hr, the evaluator is observer-inverted
```

The system was observer-inverted since June 18 when aftermath dropped below 5/hr.
But the avg_ops increase (26→44, a +69% jump from guardrail inflation per KE-059)
means the crossover threshold MOVED TOWARD the system — it would now need 8.5
aftermath/hr to stay below crossover. KE-059 and KE-060 compound each other.

## The General Principle

> **A monitoring system with fixed cadence and growing per-session cost on a
> declining-throughput pipeline will ALWAYS cross the observer inversion point.
> This is not a question of "if" but "when." The only structural prevention is
> cadence that adapts to throughput.**

This is the information-theoretic equivalent of the Heisenberg observer effect:
the act of measuring the system's low-coverage state CAUSES lower coverage
(by consuming the budget that could fund coverage).

## The Minimal Fix

**Adaptive cadence gating:**

```
if (aftermath_sessions_last_hour < heartbeat_sessions_last_hour):
    heartbeat_cadence = max(heartbeat_cadence / 2, 1/hr)
```

This single condition prevents observer inversion by ensuring the monitoring cadence
never exceeds the production cadence. Estimated savings: ~4,000 ops/day at current
production levels. Zero risk of missing genuine issues because the heartbeat still
fires (just less often).
