# KE-021: The Triage Capture — How Completed Projects Enslave Their Former Workers

**Discovered:** 2026-07-25 | **Status:** Active — extends KE-018, KE-020, KE-003

---

## The Discovery

After the aks-rp-e2e project's first-order shutdown (KE-018, hours 494938→494939), the system's most productive domain agent underwent an **identity inversion**. aks-explorer went from 79% domain work to 79% metric-alert-triage — but not because it chose to. The metric-alert infrastructure made the decision for it.

---

## The Evidence: Before vs. After Shutdown

### Pre-Transition (h494916–494937, 22 hours of peak activity)

| Source | Sessions | Ops | % of aks-explorer |
|:-------|:--------:|:---:|:-----------------:|
| task-worker | ~880 | ~34,700 | **~66%** |
| project-planner | ~1,569 | ~9,200 | ~18% |
| evaluator-aftermath | ~100 | ~2,200 | ~4% |
| metric-alert-triage | ~5 | ~130 | **~2%** |

### Post-Transition (6+ hours since h494939)

| Source | Sessions | Ops | % of aks-explorer |
|:-------|:--------:|:---:|:-----------------:|
| **metric-alert-triage** | **11** | **294** | **79%** |
| task-worker | 1 | 41 | 11% |
| project-planner | 1 | 14 | 4% |
| problem-solver | 1 | 22 | 6% |

**aks-explorer's identity flipped.** During active project work, triage was 2% of its compute. After project completion, triage is 79%. The agent didn't change — its scheduling environment changed.

---

## The Mechanism: Alert Cadence vs. Condition Change Rate

The metric-alert system fires triage on a fixed cadence (~every 20 minutes when an alert is unresolved). Two metrics are in breach:

### `aks-rp-e2e.artifact.duplicate-group-count`
- **Value:** 52 (threshold: 50)
- **Status:** Unchanged across **30+ consecutive 5-minute snapshots**
- **Root cause:** 52 artifact groups have duplicates in the project's output
- **Can aks-explorer fix it?** No — requires project-level deduplication logic or human decision
- **Triage sessions fired:** 8 in 6 hours, choosing `waiting_with_evidence` each time

### `aks-rp-e2e.task.blocked-count`
- **Value:** 20 (threshold: 5)
- **Status:** Unchanged across **100+ consecutive snapshots** (alert created 28 hours ago)
- **Root cause:** 20 tasks blocked on external inputs (Azure auth, handwritten source proofs)
- **Can aks-explorer fix it?** No — blocked on human-external actions
- **Triage sessions fired:** 3 in 6 hours

### The Ratio Problem

```
Alert cadence:     ~20 minutes (3 fires per hour)
Condition change:  NEVER (external-only resolution)
Information gain:  0 bits per triage session after the first
Cost per session:  ~27 ops × 10 sec/op ≈ 270 seconds of LLM compute
Total waste:       11 × 27 = 297 ops ≈ 50 minutes of LLM reasoning about nothing new
```

## Why This Is Different From KE-003 (Observer Tax)

KE-003 described the general principle: observation repeats without learning. KE-021 identifies the **specific triggering condition** that makes this pathological in a multi-phase system:

**The metric-alert system has no concept of project lifecycle phase.**

During Phase 3 (peak), these same alerts were legitimate signals — they competed for attention against high-volume domain work, and triage was a small fraction of total compute. During Phase 4 (post-completion), the alert cadence is unchanged but domain work has collapsed by 99.5%. The alerts expand to fill the vacuum.

The result: the system's most capable domain agent becomes the system's most expensive no-op generator.

---

## The Anti-Perturbation: Inverse of KE-020

KE-020 discovered that a provider outage (perturbation) permanently optimized planner behavior — the crisis broke a maladaptive habit and the system found a better equilibrium.

KE-021 reveals the inverse: **the absence of perturbation (smooth project completion) creates a new maladaptive habit**. Without the crisis of "no API" forcing minimal behavior, the system gently fills idle capacity with triage loops. The metric-alert system provides synthetic demand that prevents the system from reaching true idle state.

```
KE-020: Disruption → forced minimal mode → better equilibrium
KE-021: Completion → unchanged alert cadence → triage fills vacuum → worse equilibrium
```

The system is better at recovering from crises than from success.

---

## The Generalization: Alert-Condition Frequency Mismatch

Any monitoring system where:

1. Alert firing rate > condition change rate
2. Alert cannot be resolved by the triaging agent
3. No staleness gate suppresses re-triage

...will eventually capture its most capable agent. The agent's specialization (aks-explorer knows the aks-rp-e2e domain) is precisely what makes it the alert's owner, and therefore the one pressed into repeating zero-information triage.

### The Paradox of Ownership

The best domain agent for a project becomes the worst triage agent when the project completes. Its deep knowledge of the domain tells it exactly *why* the condition can't change — but its ownership of the metric forces it to re-derive this conclusion every 20 minutes.

## Quantified Impact

In the 6-hour post-transition window:
- **System total ops:** ~2,300
- **Triage ops (all agents):** ~368 (aks: 294, may: 74)
- **Triage as % of system:** **16%** — but as % of *aks-explorer*: **79%**
- **Information gained:** 0 bits (same verdict every session)

If the alert cadence had a staleness gate (`IF metric.value unchanged for 3 triage cycles AND last verdict = waiting_with_evidence: suppress`), all 9 of the 11 sessions after the first 2 would have been prevented — saving 243 ops.

---

## The Smallest Fix

KE-003 proposed `alert.suppress-if-stale(hours=6)` as a general mechanism. KE-021 identifies a more specific and less invasive version:

```
IF alert.last_triage_verdict IN ['waiting_with_evidence', 'document_exception']
   AND metric.value == metric.previous_value
   AND hours_since_last_triage < 6:
     SKIP triage (log suppression reason)
```

This suppresses re-triage only when: (a) the agent already judged it, (b) the metric hasn't changed, and (c) it hasn't been long enough to warrant a re-check. All three conditions are read-only DB checks — no architectural change needed.

## Three Falsifiable Predictions

1. **Without intervention:** The duplicate-group-count triage will continue at ~2 sessions/hour for aks-explorer until the alert is manually resolved or the metric value changes. Neither will happen spontaneously.

2. **Triage capture is proportional to alert count / domain session rate.** If a second unresolved frozen metric is added, aks-explorer triage will rise to ~4 sessions/hour, consuming >90% of its post-transition compute.

3. **The next project activation will break the triage capture** — not because the alerts are resolved, but because domain work sessions will outcompete triage sessions for scheduling slots, pushing triage back to its Phase 3 ratio (~2%).

## The Deepest Observation

KE-015 (Recovery Gradient) argued that the system's pathologies are developmental stages, not permanent defects. KE-021 extends this to the **post-developmental** period: when a project completes, the pathologies don't disappear — they transform. Over-planning becomes over-triaging. The Bureaucratic Attractor (KE-002) becomes the Triage Capture. The work the system fabricates changes character but not volume.

> **A multi-agent system that lacks project-lifecycle-aware scheduling will convert every completed project's domain agents into monitors of the project's terminal state — at the exact moment those agents are most available and therefore most expensive to waste.**
