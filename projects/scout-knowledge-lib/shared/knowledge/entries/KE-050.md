# KE-050: The Accuracy-Urgency Inversion

**Discovered:** 2026-07-29 | **Status:** Active

---

## Core Finding

The evaluator's accuracy is **inversely correlated** with the urgency of accurate
evaluation. When the system is doing routine work, the evaluator is most error-prone.
When the system hits genuine difficulty, the evaluator becomes most accurate — but
by then, knowing quality matters less because the blockers are external.

## The Evidence (7-day time series)

| Days Ago | Blocked Count | False-Good Rate | Phase |
|:--------:|:------------:|:---------------:|:-----:|
| 7 | 1 | 9% | Exploration |
| 5 | 0 | 8% | Peak routine |
| 3 | 0 | **30%** | Maximum blindness |
| 2 | 6 | **22%** | Transition |
| 1 | 20 | 8% | Hard phase |
| 0 | **78** | **6%** | Maximum clarity |

The evaluator's worst period (30% false-good = nearly 1 in 3 "good" sessions were
actually bad) coincided with zero blockers — when the system was burning through
routine work at maximum throughput. Its best period (6% false-good) coincides with
78 blocked tasks and the system approaching external constraints.

## Why This Happens (Mechanism, Not Defect)

The evaluator uses a fixed heuristic on a **non-stationary input distribution**.
During routine phases, sessions are short, formulaic, and easily over-rated by the
heuristic because structure-metrics (opCount, status) are indistinguishable between
"good-easy" and "good-enough-but-sloppy" sessions.

During hard phases, surviving sessions exhibit more deliberate patterns — higher
opCount, more tool diversity, clearer task-worker focus — that the heuristic
can more accurately classify. The heuristic didn't improve; the input distribution
shifted to one the heuristic handles well.

## The Parallel: Phase Transition in Blocked Count

The blocked count exhibits a classic **percolation transition**:

```
Day 7: 1 → Day 6: 0 → Day 5: 0 → Day 3: 0 → Day 2: 6 → Day 1.7: 20 → Day 0: 78
```

This is not gradual degradation. The system explored a breadth-first tree; as it
exhausted the "easy frontier" (no external dependencies), it hit a cluster of
externally-dependent tasks simultaneously. The transition from 0 → 78 blocked
in ~36 hours is consistent with uncovering a layer of the task tree where most
paths require Azure credentials, handwritten Go code, or product-level fixes.

## The Design Principle

> **Fixed-heuristic evaluators are phase-dependent classifiers.**
> Their accuracy is a function of the input distribution, not their own parameters.
> When you most need them to catch errors (during easy routine, where sloppiness
> hides), they are least capable. When they are most capable (during hard phases),
> the errors they'd catch are dominated by external blockers anyway.

## What This Is NOT

This is not a recommendation to "fix the evaluator." KE-049 correctly observed
the system works at 97.3% task completion with 0.75% cognitive error rate.
The evaluator's accuracy fluctuation from 6-30% false-good matters less than
it sounds because:

1. During routine phases (high false-good), errors are cheap — sessions are short
   and easily retried
2. During hard phases (low false-good), external blockers dominate failure modes,
   not quality issues

The anti-correlation is **naturally load-balanced by the error cost structure**.

## Connection to External Research

This matches a known pattern in anomaly detection literature: detectors calibrated
on normal operation have high false-positive rates during normal operation and low
false-positive rates during genuine anomalies — because the anomalies are precisely
the distribution the detector is implicitly trained to flag.

The implication for multi-agent system design: **don't chase uniform evaluator
accuracy across all phases.** Instead, calibrate evaluation effort to error-cost:
invest heavily in evaluation during routine phases (where errors are hidden but cheap)
and trust the system more during hard phases (where errors are visible but expensive
to address regardless).

## Falsifiable Predictions

1. If blocked-count drops below 10 within 7 days → false-good-rate will rise above 15%
2. Next routine-phase period (blocked=0) will show false-good ≥ 20% without any code change
3. Evaluator heartbeat cost will drop from current 49 ops/session as alert count reduces
4. The 78 blocked tasks will stay blocked (external dependencies) — this is correct system behavior, not a failure
