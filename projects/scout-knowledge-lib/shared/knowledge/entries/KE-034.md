# KE-034: The Completion Paradox — Why Quality Measurement Fails at the Finish Line

**Discovered:** 2026-07-26 | **Status:** Active

---

## The Discovery

Three independently-documented phenomena — prompt inflation (KE-033), heuristic
blindness (KE-031), and the emergent gate (KE-028) — are not separate problems.
They are three faces of a single **completion paradox**: as a project approaches
completion, the system's ability to evaluate its own work degrades in proportion
to how much evaluation matters.

## The Mechanism (Three Coupled Effects)

### Effect 1: Prompt Inflation Inverts the Cost Curve

As the aks-rp-e2e task tree reached 97% completion (1,879/1,931 leaves done),
the remaining tasks are structurally the hardest — blocked on external inputs,
requiring cross-feature reconciliation, or needing exact evidence. The task-tree
packet that every planner session receives still encodes the *entire* tree state.

| Session Type | Count | Avg Task KB | Total Task MB | Avg Ops | Ops/KB |
|:------------:|:-----:|:-----------:|:-------------:|:-------:|:------:|
| Gate (≤3 ops) | 940 | **30.5** | 27.99 | 1.7 | **0.06** |
| Light (4-10 ops) | 657 | **37.0** | 23.73 | 7.1 | 0.19 |
| Work (>10 ops) | 1,534 | 11.0 | 16.44 | 34.4 | 3.13 |

Gate and light sessions consume **75.8% of aks-explorer's total task-prompt 
bandwidth** (51.72 MB vs 16.44 MB) while producing only **5.1% of total ops**.

This is KE-033's finding at higher resolution: the work sessions are *cheaper* in
prompt terms because they're worker sessions with focused task descriptions (~11 KB).
The expensive sessions are planner sessions that receive the full tree packet
regardless of whether work exists.

### Effect 2: The Heuristic Evaluator Drifts Toward Optimism

Comparing the heuristic evaluator's behavior across time periods:

| Period | Good (0.85) | Acceptable (0.65) | NI (0.2+0.55) | Good Rate |
|:------:|:-----------:|:-----------------:|:-------------:|:---------:|
| Early (<1781200000000) | 801 | 937 | 681 | **33.1%** |
| Late (>1781500000000) | 1,815 | 513 | 457 | **65.4%** |

The good-rate **doubled** without any known calibration change in the heuristic
formula. The mechanism: as the task tree approached completion, the *types* of
sessions shifted. Early sessions included many quick planner exits and frontier
scans. Late sessions are dominated by deep worker sessions on remaining hard
tasks — these have higher opCounts and longer durations, which the heuristic
formula rewards with higher quality scores.

The heuristic was calibrated for a phase where many sessions were short exploratory
probes. Now that most sessions are serious deep work on blocked tasks, the same
formula produces inflated scores. **The quality distribution shifted not because
quality improved, but because the task mix changed.**

### Effect 3: The Sampling Paradox

Deep-eval sessions (non-heuristic) run at ~1.3% of total evaluations. The
selection logic routes sessions to deep-eval when the heuristic verdict is
suspicious or the session matches certain criteria. As the heuristic produces
more "good" verdicts, fewer sessions get routed to deep-eval.

This creates a sampling paradox: **the system reduces quality scrutiny precisely
when the remaining tasks are the hardest and most likely to produce subtle errors.**
KE-031 showed that deep-eval finds only 28.6% of aks-explorer sessions genuinely
good (vs 86.8% heuristic good-rate). The gap is likely *larger* now because the
remaining tasks involve complex evidence reconciliation where quality failures
are semantic, not structural.

## Why This Is a Completion-Specific Pathology

This paradox only manifests near project completion. During active exploration:
- Many sessions, short average ops → heuristic produces spread of verdicts
- High frontier velocity → errors self-correct in subsequent sessions
- Low per-task cost → evaluation errors are cheap

Near completion:
- Few sessions, high average ops → heuristic converges to "good"
- Zero frontier velocity → errors persist in blocked/review state indefinitely
- High per-task cost → evaluation errors are expensive

The information value of each evaluation *increases* with completion (fewer
sessions, each more consequential) while the system's evaluation *accuracy*
decreases (heuristic drifts toward constant "good").

## The Broader Pattern: Goodhart's End-State

The heuristic evaluator optimized for opCount as a quality signal. This was
roughly correct during active exploration: more tool calls ≈ more work done.
But at project completion, more tool calls ≈ harder residual problem ≈ higher
risk of semantic error. The metric flipped from correlated to anti-correlated
with quality, and the system can't detect the flip because the metric itself
is the detection mechanism.

This is Goodhart's Law in its terminal form: the proxy metric doesn't just
diverge from the target — it *inverts*, producing maximum confidence at the
point of maximum uncertainty.

## The Self-Referential Test

This KE entry describes a system that becomes blind to quality problems near
completion. The KE library itself is approaching a similar state: 33 entries,
most still marked "active," with no mechanism to evaluate which entries are
still true. The completion paradox applies to the KE library's own quality:
each new entry adds to context cost (KE-023) while the system's ability to
evaluate whether old entries are still valid decreases.

## Falsifiable Predictions

1. **Heuristic good-rate will exceed 75% within 30 days** if no recalibration
   occurs, because the remaining task mix is increasingly dominated by deep
   worker sessions on hard problems (high opCount → high heuristic score).

2. **Deep-eval disagreement rate will increase**: if deep-eval is randomly
   applied to sessions the heuristic rates "good," the LLM good-rate will
   be ≤35% — a wider gap than KE-031's 58pp because remaining tasks are
   harder to evaluate.

3. **aks-explorer prompt bandwidth will not decrease** despite fewer sessions,
   because the full task-tree packet size is constant regardless of frontier
   emptiness. Total prompt MB will stay above 60 MB/48h until either the tree
   is pruned or the packet is delta-encoded.

## The Practical Implication

The system needs **completion-aware evaluation**: as a project approaches its
terminal state, evaluation should *intensify* (higher deep-eval sampling) rather
than relax (lower sampling from optimistic heuristic). This is the opposite of
what currently happens.

A simple fix: when a project's done-rate exceeds 90%, set the deep-eval sampling
rate to ≥20% for that project's sessions. This catches the quality inversion
before it matters.

> **A system that evaluates itself by volume becomes blind at the finish line.
> The last 3% of work carries 97% of the residual risk, but receives the least scrutiny.**
