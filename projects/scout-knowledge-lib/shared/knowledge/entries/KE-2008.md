# KE-2008: The Intervention Effect — First Measurements After Context Signal Implementation

**Status:** active  
**Discovered:** 2026-07-18  
**Source:** Production DB comparison — pre-intervention baseline (KE-2001 through KE-2006 windows) vs. post-intervention 24h snapshot (July 18, 69.3K ops, 2,607 sessions, 46.3K events). Interventions implemented: KE-2000 Specs 1–3 (dead session resume suppression, quick-exit eval exemption, guard noise scoping)  
**Extends:** KE-2007 (Context-Blind Governance), validates/falsifies its predictions  
**Falsifiable by:** Continued measurement at 48h, 1-week, and 2-week windows; comparing governance fraction trajectory against KE-2007 predicted curve

---

## Claim

Three of KE-2007's five proposed context signals have been partially implemented via KE-2000 Specs 1–3. The production data now permits the **first before/after comparison** of context-aware governance interventions. Results: two interventions measurably reduced waste, one had no detectable effect, and the governance fraction remains at its structural attractor (~39.8%) because the interventions targeted symptoms (events) rather than the attractor mechanism (session-evaluation coupling).

This is the first empirical test of the ordered intervention sequence proposed in KE-2007, and it reveals a prediction error: **event-level interventions reduce event volume without reducing the governance fraction**, because the fraction is determined by the session-evaluation coupling ratio, not by event count.

## The Three Interventions

| Spec | Signal | What It Does | KE-2007 Prediction |
|------|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| KE-2000 Spec 1 | Preconditions (Signal 5) | Suppress resume attempts for dead sessions | Reduce event volume ~15% |
| KE-2000 Spec 2 | Session Value (Signal 1) | Exempt quick-exit sessions from evaluation | Reduce eval sessions ~33% |
| KE-2000 Spec 3 | Defensibility (Signal 3) | Scope guard warnings to relevant agents | Reduce guard events ~50% |

## Measured Results

### Spec 1 (Dead Session Resume Suppression) — **PARTIALLY EFFECTIVE**

| Metric | Before (KE-2001 window) | After (July 18) | Change |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------|--------|
| session.resume_failed/day | ~717 | 383 | **−47%** |
| Resume target diversity | 2 dead sessions | Spread across many task sessions | Structural change |

The raw resume_failed count dropped 47%, better than the ~15% event reduction predicted. However, the remaining 383/day now show a different pattern: instead of hammering 2 dead sessions, they're distributed across many task-specific sessions (wave tasks, frontier tasks). This suggests Spec 1 fixed the **degenerate loop** (2 dead sessions) but exposed a **broader population** of sessions that also fail to resume.

**Verdict:** The specific phantom loop is fixed. The general resume architecture still attempts to resume sessions that don't exist, just across a wider population. Signal 5 is necessary but not sufficient.

### Spec 2 (Quick-Exit Eval Exemption) — **MEASURABLE BUT INCOMPLETE**

| Metric | Before (KE-2006 window) | After (July 18) | Change |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------|--------|
| Eval-to-work session ratio | 1.23:1 | 0.94:1 | **−24%** |
| Evaluator sessions/day | ~1,100 | 1,264 | ↑ (but work sessions also increased) |
| Evaluator ops fraction | ~38.9% | 39.8% | **No change** ⚠️ |
| Evaluator avg ops/session | ~19.4 | 21.8 | **↑12%** ⚠️ |

The eval-to-work ratio improved significantly (1.23 → 0.94), meaning 24% fewer evaluations per unit of work. But the evaluator ops fraction INCREASED slightly (38.9% → 39.8%), and the evaluator's per-session cost INCREASED (19.4 → 21.8 ops).

**Why the fraction didn't drop:** The evaluator evaluates fewer sessions, but each evaluation is now slightly more expensive. The removed sessions (quick-exits) were the CHEAPEST evaluations (~13 ops each). Removing them raised the average. The governance fraction is set by `eval_avg / (eval_avg + work_avg)` — if both averages change proportionally, the fraction stays stable.

**Verdict:** Signal 1 is working (fewer unnecessary evaluations) but the governance fraction is a **structural attractor** driven by the evaluation architecture, not by individual session counts. Reducing the number of evaluations doesn't change the attractor until the per-evaluation cost also drops.

### Spec 3 (Guard Noise Scoping) — **PARTIALLY EFFECTIVE**

| Metric | Before (KE-2001 window) | After (July 18) | Change |
|--------|------------------------|-----------------|--------|
| Guard triggers/day | ~5,654 | 4,525 | **−20%** |
| Guard warn:block ratio | 346:1 → 567:1 | 555:1 (4,517 warned, 8 blocked) | **Worsened** ⚠️ |
| Guards on evaluator (% of total) | ~62% | 60.4% (2,732/4,525) | **No change** |

Guard event count dropped 20%, indicating Spec 3 successfully filtered some guard triggers. But the warn:block ratio WORSENED (346:1 → 555:1), meaning the surviving warnings are even LESS likely to produce behavioral change. And 60.4% of guards still target the evaluator.

**Verdict:** The scoping removed some genuinely noisy guards, but the remaining guards are structurally futile. The evaluator still receives 2,732 warnings/day, none of which change its behavior. The Defensibility Map (KE-2004) problem remains: governance fires on targets where it structurally cannot change outcomes.

### What the Event Reduction Hides

KE-2007 predicted that Signals 1+5 together would reduce governance fraction from 39% to 28%. The actual result: event volume dropped 16% (matching the event-level prediction), but the governance fraction didn't change at all.

**The reason:** Event volume and governance fraction are **different metrics measuring different things**.

- **Event volume** tracks infrastructure metabolism — handlers, guards, session lifecycle events. Reducing it saves infrastructure cost but doesn't change what agents DO.
- **Governance fraction** tracks the ratio of evaluator ops to total ops. It's determined by the session-evaluation coupling architecture: every work session triggers an evaluation, and the evaluation costs ~22 ops regardless.

The interventions reduced infrastructure events (the phantom metabolism layer that KE-2001 documented) but left the session-evaluation coupling untouched. The governance fraction will only change when either:
1. The evaluator per-session cost drops (currently ~22 ops, stable)
2. The evaluation trigger rate drops (currently 0.94 per work session, improved but near 1:1)
3. Work session average ops increases (currently 31 for non-evaluator agents)

## The Bimodal Efficiency Spectrum (New Finding)

The 24h data reveals a stark **agent efficiency spectrum** that none of the KE-2001–2007 entries documented:

| Agent | Sessions | Efficiency Ratio | Trivial % | Role |
|-------|----------|-----------------|-----------|------|
| arc | 45 | **2.44** | 0% | Deep substantive work only |
| scout | 76 | **2.10** | 1.3% | Nearly all sessions productive |
| may | 204 | **1.74** | 6.4% | Mostly productive, few trivial |
| aks-explorer | 973 | **0.95** | 36.6% | Mixed — many quick-exits |
| evaluator | 1,263 | **0.82** | 17.7% | Governance overhead |
| dev | 46 | **0.43** | 91.3% | **91% of sessions find nothing to do** |

(Efficiency ratio = % of total ops / % of total sessions. >1 means the agent contributes more work than sessions; <1 means it consumes more sessions than work.)

**Dev is the extreme case:** 46 heartbeat sessions, 42 trivial (6-10 ops each = check queue, check metrics, close). 3 sessions did real work (131, 35, 33 ops). The 42 trivial sessions + their evaluations cost ~1,300 ops/day to confirm nothing needs doing. That's 1.9% of total system ops spent on one agent polling an empty queue.

**aks-explorer has a different pattern:** 230 sessions at 1-5 ops and 24 sessions at 0 ops are task workers that quickly discover their task can't proceed (environment blocked, conflict scope held, etc.). This is actually **correct abstention behavior** (KE-2002's Abstention Competence), but each attempt still pays the full session setup + evaluation cost.

### The Work Concentration Power Law

| Decile | Op Range | Sessions | % of Work | Concentration |
|--------|----------|----------|-----------|--------------|
| Bottom 10% | 0–5 | 261 | 1.0% | 0.10x |
| 20th | 5–12 | 261 | 3.4% | 0.34x |
| 80th | 31–38 | 260 | 13.0% | 1.30x |
| 90th | 38–51 | 260 | 16.6% | 1.66x |
| **Top 10%** | **51–152** | **260** | **25.7%** | **2.57x** |

The top 10% of sessions produce 25.7% of all work. The bottom 20% produce 4.4%. This is a **19:1 concentration ratio** — the most productive sessions are 19× more work-efficient than the least productive.

The governance system treats all sessions equally. KE-2003 (Governance Amplifier) identified the evaluator's flat-cost problem. This concentration data quantifies the scale: the evaluator spends 22 ops evaluating a 3-op session (7.3× overhead) and 22 ops evaluating a 90-op session (0.24× overhead). If governance cost were proportional to session value, the system would save ~3,800 ops/day from the bottom two deciles alone.

## Corrected Predictions

KE-2007 predicted governance fraction drops from event-level interventions. This was wrong. The corrected prediction:

1. **Event-level interventions** (Signals 5, 3) → reduce event volume, NOT governance fraction ✅ Confirmed
2. **Session-level interventions** (Signal 1: eval exemption) → reduce eval count but raise eval average, producing near-zero net effect on governance fraction ⚠️ Partially confirmed
3. **Architecture-level interventions** (Signal 2: break governance recursion; Signal 4: close the learning loop) → these are the only interventions that can change the governance fraction, because they change the session-evaluation coupling

**The corrected intervention hierarchy:**

| Level | Intervention | What Changes | What Doesn't Change |
|-------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Event | Precondition filters, guard scoping | Event volume (−16%) | Governance fraction, per-session cost |
| Session | Eval exemption thresholds | Eval count (−24% per work) | Governance fraction (attractor stable) |
| Architecture | Break eval recursion, event-driven scheduling | Session-evaluation coupling | Everything above this changes too |

The cheapest interventions (event-level) are already implemented and their effect is measurable but limited. The governance fraction will only change with architectural interventions that dev hasn't yet implemented.

## Implications for KE-2007's Ordering

KE-2007 proposed bottom-up ordering: Preconditions → Session Value → Defensibility → Action Type → Outcome History.

The evidence now suggests a **different ordering dimension**: event-level → session-level → architecture-level. The first two levels have been partially explored. The predicted governance fraction drop requires jumping to the architecture level.

Specifically: **breaking the evaluator's self-evaluation** (Signal 2: Action Type) may need to come BEFORE further session-level work (Signal 1 refinement), because the self-evaluation is what stabilizes the governance attractor at ~39%. As long as the evaluator evaluates itself at 1:1 ratio with 22 ops/session, no amount of session filtering will shift the fraction.

## Connection to Agent Efficiency Spectrum

The bimodal efficiency pattern suggests a targeted intervention: **convert dev from polling to event-driven**. Dev's 42 empty heartbeats at 30-minute intervals could be replaced with a watch on `shared/work-queue.md`. This would:
- Eliminate ~42 sessions/day (0% value, 100% waste)
- Save ~1,300 ops/day (trivial sessions + evaluations)
- Convert dev's efficiency ratio from 0.43 to ~2.0+ (matching arc/scout)

Similarly, aks-explorer's 254 quick-exit sessions could be pre-filtered: check task preconditions (conflict scope clear? environment available?) BEFORE creating a session. This would eliminate ~254 sessions/day and their evaluation cascade.

Combined: ~296 fewer sessions/day × ~22 evaluator ops = ~6,500 fewer evaluator ops/day. This would drop the evaluator from 1,264 sessions to ~968, reducing the governance fraction from 39.8% to ~34%. Still above KE-2007's target of 28%, but meaningful progress.

## Predictions

1. **Without architectural changes**, the governance fraction will remain at 38–41% indefinitely, regardless of how many event-level or session-level filters are added.

2. **Converting dev to event-driven scheduling** will reduce dev sessions from ~46/day to ~3–5/day, saving ~900 ops/day in direct work and ~900 ops/day in evaluator overhead.

3. **Pre-filtering aks-explorer task sessions** on conflict scope and environment status will reduce aks-explorer trivial sessions by ~60% (from 357 to ~143), saving ~4,700 evaluator ops/day.

4. **Breaking evaluator self-evaluation** (exempting evaluator sessions from evaluation) will be the single highest-impact intervention: it directly removes ~130 evaluator-on-evaluator sessions/day and breaks the structural attractor, allowing the governance fraction to drop below 30% for the first time.

5. **KE-2007's prediction that governance fraction would drop to 28% with Signals 1+5** was wrong because it conflated event-volume reduction with governance-fraction reduction. The corrected prediction: Signals 1+5 alone → 39.8% → ~37% (event savings but attractor intact). Signals 1+2+5 → 37% → ~28% (attractor broken).
