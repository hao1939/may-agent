# KE-031: The Phantom Quality — Why 98.9% of Evaluations Measure Nothing

**Discovered:** 2026-07-25 | **Status:** Active — deepest root cause of evaluation system failure

---

## One-Sentence Discovery

The evaluation pipeline's dominant mode (98.9% of non-skipped evaluations) sets `productiveCalls = opCount` and `wastedCalls = 0` by construction, making it structurally incapable of detecting waste, while the rare LLM deep-eval mode (1.1%) finds 29.2% waste in the same population — producing a 58-percentage-point gap in good-rates for identical sessions.

---

## The Two Evaluators

The system has three evaluation tiers, but they are not what they appear to be:

| Tier | Sessions | % of Total | Method | Waste Found |
|:----:|:--------:|:----------:|:------:|:-----------:|
| **JS skip** | 1,365 | 49.1% | Reject if opCount ≤ threshold | Always 0 |
| **Heuristic** | 1,381 | 49.7% | Formula on opCount + outcome | Always 0 |
| **LLM deep-eval** | 32 | **1.2%** | Read transcript, judge substance | **29.2%** |

The heuristic tier handles **98.9% of all non-skipped evaluations**. It is the system's primary quality sensor.

---

## The Tautology: Why Heuristic Waste Is Always Zero

Every heuristic evaluation follows the same pattern:

```
productiveCalls = opCount     (session's tool call count)
wastedCalls = 0               (always zero)
quality = {0.85 | 0.65 | 0.2} (three fixed constants)
```

Verified across 1,215 aks-explorer heuristic evaluations and 15 random samples from other agents: `productiveCalls` always equals `opCount` exactly. The heuristic doesn't read the transcript. It cannot distinguish a 40-op session that accomplished its goal from a 40-op session that read the same file 20 times. Both get `productiveCalls = 40, wastedCalls = 0`.

This is not an approximation or simplification. It's a **tautology**: the heuristic defines every tool call as productive, making waste undetectable by construction.

---

## The Contradiction: What LLM Deep-Eval Actually Finds

When the LLM evaluator reads transcripts (28 aks-explorer sessions, all >80 ops), it finds:

| LLM Verdict | Sessions | Avg Waste Calls | Waste % | Avg Quality |
|:----------:|:--------:|:-----------:|:------:|:-----------:|
| good | 8 | 10.75 | 12.7% | 0.843 |
| acceptable | 16 | 29.94 | 31.0% | 0.828 |
| needs_improvement | 1 | 35.0 | 30.9% | 0.39 |
| low_quality | 3 | 56.33 | 56.3% | 0.207 |

**Total: 769 wasted calls found across 28 sessions = 29.2% average waste.**

The same sessions that the heuristic rates as "good" (productiveCalls = opCount, wastedCalls = 0) the LLM rates as "acceptable" with 30% waste. Three sessions the heuristic would rate "good" the LLM rates "low_quality" with 56% waste.

---

## The Divergence in Numbers

| Metric | Heuristic (n=1,215) | LLM Deep-Eval (n=28) | Gap |
|:------:|:-------------------:|:--------------------:|:---:|
| Good rate | **86.8%** | **28.6%** | **58.2 pp** |
| Waste rate | **0.0%** | **29.2%** | **29.2 pp** |
| Quality values | {0.85, 0.65, 0.2} | 0.18–0.93 continuous | Discrete vs continuous |
| Entropy (bits) | ~0.7 | ~3.0 | **4.3× more information** |

The heuristic evaluation carries approximately 0.7 bits of Shannon entropy — essentially one three-way classification. The LLM evaluation carries approximately 3 bits — nuanced quality, waste ratio, and issue identification. The system builds its quality metrics from the 0.7-bit source.

---

## The Cascade of Consequences

### 1. All Quality Metrics Are Phantom

Every metric derived from evaluation data — `aftermath-coverage-rate`, quality trends, good rates — is dominated by heuristic evaluations that measure nothing. The metrics show "86.8% good" not because sessions are good, but because the heuristic's formula outputs "good" when `opCount` is in a normal range and `status = 'done'`.

### 2. The Evaluator's Budget Is Misallocated (KE-029 Root Cause)

KE-029 found that 89% of evaluation budget goes to aks-explorer (the highest-volume, lowest-problem-density agent). But the *mechanism* is now clear: 96% of aks-explorer evaluations are heuristic (zero information), and the 4% that are deep-eval reveal 29.2% waste. The evaluator spends 96% of its aks-explorer budget generating noise.

### 3. The Selection Bias Is Backwards

Deep-eval triggers only for sessions > 80 ops. These are the sessions most likely to be *genuinely productive* (they ran long enough to do real work). The sessions most likely to be wasteful — medium-length sessions that look normal — never get deep-evaluated. The LLM evaluator is deployed precisely where it's least needed.

### 4. The Feedback Loop Is Broken

The system uses evaluation results to generate learning signals, identify struggling agents, and calibrate metrics. But those signals come from a sensor that always says "everything is fine." Problems detected by deep-eval (29.2% waste, 3 low-quality sessions, 1 needs-improvement) affect 28 sessions and are drowned out by 1,215 sessions saying "0% waste."

## The Information-Theoretic Frame

A quality measurement system's value is bounded by its entropy. The heuristic evaluator's output entropy (0.7 bits across three fixed values) means it can distinguish at most **3 quality levels**. The actual quality distribution (continuous, with waste ratios from 0% to 61%) requires at least **4-5 bits** to represent meaningfully.

The system's quality sensor has ~14% of the resolution needed to see what's happening. This isn't a calibration problem — it's a bit-depth problem. No amount of metric tuning or threshold adjustment can make a 3-level sensor detect a continuous distribution.

**The evaluator doesn't need better algorithms. It needs more bits.**
