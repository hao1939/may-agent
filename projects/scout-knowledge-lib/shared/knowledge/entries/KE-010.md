## The Discovery

The transition was abrupt (ratio jumped from 1.3× to 2.7× in one day), has not reversed in the subsequent days measured, and cannot reverse without structural intervention.

No prior synthesis identified this as a *phase transition* with a precise date. The Halving Trajectory documented declining productive %. The Metabolic Divide described two agent types. ## The Thermodynamic Metaphor: Carnot Inversion

A heat engine has a theoretical maximum efficiency (Carnot limit). Below this limit, adding measurement/control improves output. Above it, measurement overhead exceeds the production it enables.

The agent system's Carnot point is at **eval:prod ratio = 1.0**. Below it, evaluation improves the system. Above it, evaluation degrades the system:

```
BELOW PARITY (eval < prod):
  Evaluation → quality signal → behavior improvement → more productive output
  Net: positive feedback, evaluation pays for itself
  
ABOVE PARITY (eval > prod):
  Evaluation → resource contention → shorter sessions → more sessions → more evaluation
  Net: NEGATIVE feedback, evaluation consumes the capacity it needs to justify itself
```

The system crossed this boundary on Jun 15 and entered the self-reinforcing negative regime.

## Why The Evaluator Cannot Self-Correct

The evaluator *cannot detect its own harm* because:

1. **It measures session quality, not system quality.** Individual sessions may score well (aks-explorer non-skipped: avg quality 0.815) while the system drowns in evaluation overhead.
2. **It is exempt from measurement.** No quality/efficiency score is ever computed for the evaluator itself.
3. **Its cost is invisible to its own metric.** The heuristic awards efficiency 0.80 to sessions ≤30 ops — the evaluator's average is 22.5 ops, comfortably in the "efficient" band by its own standard.
4. **It scales with the problem it creates.** More sessions (from the shallowing cascade) → more evaluation → less time for productive work → shallower sessions → more sessions.

This is a classic case of a regulator that cannot measure its own regulatory overhead — Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety applied to self-referential systems.
