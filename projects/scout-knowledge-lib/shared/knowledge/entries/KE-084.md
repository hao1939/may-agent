## The Mechanism: Helpfulness Optimization Kills Abstention

**nextaccountic** (HN #58) identified the model-level mechanism: "Because of their immense size [frontier models] simply did not learn how to say 'I don't know.'" Training on massive factual corpora rewards always-answering; the abstention reflex atrophies.

The same mechanism operates at the workflow level through a different channel:
- Heartbeats are **contracted to investigate** — every invocation produces a full investigation
- The planner is **contracted to route** — it can discover "nothing to route" and exit cheaply
- The difference isn't intelligence; it's whether the contract includes a valid null-output

At the community level: paradigm namers are **rewarded for naming** (engagement, followers, identity). The "nothing new to name" output has no reward signal.

## The Quantified Asymmetry

From live 48h data (96,163 total system ops):

| Workflow Category | Sessions | Total Ops | Abstention Rate | Avg Cost |
|:------------------|:--------:|:---------:|:---------------:|:--------:|
| Planner (CAN abstain) | 1,504 | 10,834 | 52.1% | 7.2 |
| Heartbeats (CANNOT abstain) | 577 | 24,902 | 0.5% | 43.2 |

The three zero-abstention heartbeats consume **25.9% of system budget** and have produced zero state changes in this window. The planner, which CAN abstain, processes 2.6× more sessions at 43.5% of the cost.

**The 42× cost ratio**: A planner gate session costs 1.67 ops. An arc heartbeat session costs 71.1 ops. Both check whether action is needed. One can conclude "no" cheaply; the other cannot conclude "no" at all.

## Why This Isn't Just KE-063/KE-068 Restated

Prior KEs identified the monitoring overhead (KE-060), the re-derivation cost (KE-068), and the dormancy spike (KE-070). All proposed state externalization as the fix. KE-084 identifies something more fundamental:

> **The root cause isn't missing state registers. It's missing null-output contracts.**

Even with perfect state externalization, a workflow that's contracted to "always investigate" will always investigate. The planner doesn't abstain because it has better state — it abstains because its **contract permits null output**. The heartbeats don't fail to abstain because they lack state — they fail because their **contract mandates investigation output**.

The distinction matters for the fix: state registers reduce the COST of non-abstention (from 71 ops to maybe 15). Null-output contracts eliminate unnecessary sessions entirely (from 393 sessions to maybe 50 that actually detect changes).

## The Fix Hierarchy

| Level | Fix | Effect | Already Proposed? |
|:------|:----|:-------|:-----------------|
| 1. Contract | Add `skip` as valid heartbeat output | Eliminates unnecessary sessions | **No** (novel to KE-084) |
| 2. Dispatch | Don't invoke if input unchanged | Eliminates unnecessary invocations | KE-063 (20-line code change) |
| 3. State | Provide pre-computed state diff | Reduces per-session cost | KE-068, KE-076 |

Levels 2-4 reduce cost. Level 1 reduces **volume**. The Hallucination Inversion teaches that Level 1 is primary — GLM-5.2 isn't better at answering, it's better at NOT answering.

## The Universal Principle

> **Systems optimized for production will lose the ability to abstain from production.
> This loss is invisible until measured against a system that CAN abstain.**
>
> **Abstention is not the absence of capability. It is a capability that must be
> explicitly designed, contracted, rewarded, and maintained.**
