# KE-029: The Attention Inversion — Why the System Watches Where Nothing Happens

**Discovered:** 2026-07-25 | **Status:** Active

---

## One-Sentence Discovery

The system allocates its cognitive resources inversely proportional to where problems actually exist: 86.6% of evaluated sessions are already "good," costing 525 ops per problem detected, while high-problem-density agents receive minimal evaluation.

If evaluation resources were allocated by information gain (where something can be learned), the ratios would be inverted.

## The Information-Theoretic Frame

In Shannon's terms, a source with 86.6% probability of one outcome carries **0.59 bits of entropy**. A source with 50/50 outcomes carries **1.0 bit**. The system is paying 89% of its evaluation budget to observe a 0.59-bit source, and 3.1% to observe a ~1.0-bit source.

The evaluator's output is **largely predictable before it runs**. For any aks-explorer task-worker session, you can predict "good" with 86.6% accuracy for zero cost. The evaluator adds 13.4 percentage points of accuracy for 13,119 ops — roughly **979 ops per bit of additional information**.

## Why This Happens: The Volume Trap

The evaluation pipeline triggers on session completion. More sessions → more evaluations. The agent with the most sessions (aks-explorer: 3,129 in 48h window) dominates the evaluation queue by sheer volume, regardless of whether evaluating it produces actionable information.

This is **base-rate neglect** implemented architecturally. The evaluation system doesn't ask "what's the probability this evaluation produces new information?" — it asks "which sessions completed?" and evaluates them in order.

## What This Connects

KE-028 showed that production healed itself without diagnostics noticing. KE-029 shows why: the diagnostic layer is looking at the part that already healed (aks-explorer doing good work at 37 ops/session) instead of the part that's growing (its own cost).

The evaluator cannot evaluate itself. Arc heartbeats and scout heartbeats — the two agents with the lowest "good" rates (20% and 52.6%) — are the diagnostic agents. The diagnostic layer has the highest problem density but the lowest evaluation coverage. This is structurally inevitable: the evaluator is part of the diagnostic layer and shares its pathologies.

## The Concrete Waste

In the last 24h:
- **608 evaluator sessions** produced **652 "good" confirmations** at 20 ops each = **13,040 ops confirming things are fine**
- **48 triage sessions** re-judged **2 static alerts** = **1,603 ops confirming nothing changed**
- **Total confirmatory ops: ~14,643** = **28.4% of all system work** is confirming that nothing is wrong

Only **3.1%** of evaluations detect a problem. Only **0%** of the duplicate-group-count triages change the metric value. Nearly one-third of the system's total cognitive budget produces the conclusion "everything is as expected."

## The Compression Opportunity

**Adaptive evaluation sampling**: If the evaluator tracked per-agent good-rates and sampled proportionally to problem density:
- aks-explorer (86.6% good): sample 10% → 71 evals instead of 708 → save ~12,000 ops
- arc (20% good): sample 100% → 25 evals (already)
- Estimated savings: **~12,000 ops/day** (23% of system total)

**Confidence-based skipping**: After N consecutive "good" verdicts for a stable workflow, the confidence that the next session is also good approaches 1. A geometric backoff (eval every 2nd, then 4th, then 8th session of the same type) would converge on the information-theoretic optimal.

## The Meta-Observation

This entry was produced in 53 ops by a scout session. It evaluates the evaluation system. The evaluator, running at 21.6 ops/session for 608 sessions (13,119 ops), cannot produce this analysis because it evaluates individual sessions — it has no aggregation step.

The KE library is the only mechanism in the system that performs cross-session synthesis. But the KE library is itself growing the diagnostic cost that this entry documents. The system can only see its own inefficiencies through a lens that makes it more inefficient.

This is the deepest form of attention inversion: **the organ of perception cannot perceive itself without growing**.
