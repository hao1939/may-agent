# KE-051: The Lifecycle Contamination — How Scheduling Artifacts Dominate Quality Signals

**Discovered:** 2026-07-29 | **Status:** Active

---

## Core Finding

The evaluator's quality signal is dominated by **session lifecycle state**, not agent
competence. Three measurable artifacts — interruptions, gate-session length, and
scheduling pre-emption — account for the majority of "needs_improvement" verdicts
and create a structural quality penalty exclusive to the production agent.

## The Evidence

### The Interruption Monopoly

Only one agent in the system experiences interruptions: aks-explorer.

| Agent | Sessions (24h) | Interrupted | Interrupted % |
|:-----:|:-------------:|:-----------:|:-------------:|
| aks-explorer | 9,350 | 937 | **10.0%** |
| evaluator | 9,510 | 0 | 0% |
| scout | 994 | 0 | 0% |
| arc | 639 | 0 | 0% |
| may | 1,270 | 2 | 0.2% |

937 sessions — 10% of all production work — are interrupted. **100% of evaluated
interrupted sessions receive "needs_improvement" verdicts** (791/791). Yet their
average opCount is 29 ops (identical to successful medium sessions), meaning
substantial productive work was completed before external termination.

### Interruption Source: Scheduling, Not Quality

| Source | Interrupted Sessions | Avg Ops |
|:------:|:-------------------:|:-------:|
| workflow:project-planner (step 2) | 625 | 31 ops |
| workflow:task-worker | 281 | 25 ops |
| Other | 31 | 36 ops |

The planner step-2 sessions (planning-update) are the primary interruption target.
These are complex planning sessions that get pre-empted by scheduler timeouts or
concurrent session limits — not by quality failures.

### The 60% Contamination

Interrupted sessions account for **60% of all needs_improvement verdicts** for
aks-explorer (791 of 1,316). The verdict category that should signal "agent did
poor work" is majority-populated by "scheduler killed a working session."

### The Actual-Success vs Evaluator-Pass Gap

| Session Type | Actual Success Rate | Evaluator Pass Rate | Gap |
|:------------:|:------------------:|:------------------:|:---:|
| Gate (1-3 ops) | **97.1%** | 4.7% | **92.4pp** |
| Quick (4-5 ops) | **97.9%** | 39.6% | **58.3pp** |
| Short (6-10 ops) | **95.2%** | 20.7% | **74.5pp** |
| Moderate (11-20 ops) | 82.3% | 79.1% | 3.2pp |
| Heavy (21-50 ops) | 85.1% | 75.6% | 9.5pp |
| Very-heavy (60+) | 95.6% | 88.7% | 6.9pp |

The evaluator only agrees with reality for sessions >10 ops. Below that threshold,
it systematically rates successful sessions as failures — creating a gap of up to
92 percentage points. Gate sessions (planner quick-checks, 1-3 ops) succeed 97.1%
of the time but the evaluator rates only 4.7% as passing.

## The Three Contaminants

### 1. Lifecycle Contamination (interruptions → needs_improvement)
Sessions killed by scheduler/timeout get rated as quality failures.
Impact: 791 false-negatives per 24h window. Exclusive to production agent.

### 2. Length Bias (short success → low quality)
Efficient sessions that achieve their goal quickly get penalized.
Impact: 1,694 successful light sessions rated 84.5% "bad" (only 15.5% pass).

### 3. Asymmetric Exposure
Only aks-explorer faces interruptions. Meta-layer agents never experience
scheduling pre-emption. This means quality comparisons between agents are
structurally invalid — aks-explorer carries a ~10% quality penalty that
other agents never face.

## The Quality Function Decomposition

What the evaluator actually measures:

```
quality_score = f(opCount) + penalty(lifecycle_state) + noise
```

Where:
- `f(opCount)` = step function: 0.077 at 1-3 ops, 0.224 at 4-10, 0.637 at 11-30, 0.706 at 31-60
- `penalty(interrupted)` = always needs_improvement regardless of work done
- `penalty(short + done)` = punish efficient completion

What it does NOT measure:
- Whether the agent's actions achieved the task goal
- Whether tool calls were productive vs exploratory
- Whether the outcome advanced the project

## Design Implications

1. **Interrupted sessions should not be evaluated for quality.** They should receive
   a distinct lifecycle verdict ("interrupted", "pre-empted") rather than being rated
   on the quality dimension. A session killed at op 29 while doing productive work is
   not a quality event.

2. **Gate sessions should have their own evaluation track.** A 2-op planner gate that
   correctly decides "no work needed" is a success, not a quality failure. The evaluator
   should recognize the task type (gate vs worker) and apply different quality criteria.

3. **Cross-agent quality comparisons are invalid** until lifecycle contamination is
   removed. Any quality-based resource allocation or agent ranking that includes
   aks-explorer's scores is comparing signal (other agents) to signal+noise
   (aks-explorer with its 10% interruption penalty).

4. **The 60% contamination makes the needs_improvement category nearly useless.**
   Of 1,316 needs_improvement verdicts for aks-explorer: 791 are interrupted (lifecycle),
   83 are done-gate (length bias), and only 258 are actual errors. The signal-to-noise
   ratio for "this agent did poor work" is approximately 1:3.

## Falsifiable Predictions

1. If interrupted sessions are excluded from evaluation → aks-explorer's avg quality
   rises from ~0.5 to ~0.65 (matching scout/arc)
2. If gate sessions (1-3 ops, status=done) are excluded → aks-explorer light-band
   quality rises from 0.147 to >0.5
3. Interruption rate will remain exclusive to aks-explorer because it's the only
   agent scheduled by the project-planner workflow with step-2 sessions subject to
   concurrent-session limits
4. No structural change to the evaluator will occur because interrupted sessions
   have never been flagged as a separate category in any alert or metric
