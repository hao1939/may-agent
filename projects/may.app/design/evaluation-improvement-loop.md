# Evidence-to-improvement pilot

The pilot is a composition of small ordinary Task stages, not one improvement
controller. Evaluation findings and observed execution failures share owner
judgment and the later improvement stages; they keep different evidence readers.
Normal execution recovery remains in the original work.

The [Alex proposal](../../may-agent.app/docs/proposals/alex-improvement-app.md)
places new system-improvement coordination with App `alex`, owned by agent
`alex`, using the same Task and evidence mechanisms. It is unimplemented;
May's current intake and retained ownership described here stay in place until
the separately verified transition. The proposed operating target enables only
May, Alex and Scout; useful Evaluation/Gym and maintenance capabilities move
under Alex as part of the breaking migration.

Evaluation publications, review-lag breaches and Evaluation operation breaches now
route to ordinary May `evaluation-intake` work in App source. Each
report keeps its immutable publication identity and original event/caller context.
The standing responsibility has a fixed intent, so changed evidence reuses the
Task without silently revising requirements. Duplicate transport reuses its
admission. A closed intake still requires explicit authorized reopening.

The Conversation handler is independent of this route. May verifies findings,
reuses fitting investigation work and returns ordinary Task judgments. Summary,
facts and results remain quiet unless the agent decides a message is owed.
This fixes delivery of a diagnosis to executable work; it does not prove that
Evaluation selects fewer repeated semantic reviews or that live recovery has
occurred. The connected regression is `evaluation-routing.test.ts`; the broader
[Task communication proposal](../../may-agent.app/docs/proposals/tasks-and-communication-resources.md)
retains the common execution change.

The [metric-review source candidate](../../evaluation.app/design/metric-pattern-review.md)
uses the same judgment and improvement boundary for `{appId, metricId}` reports.
May's intake verifies immutable publication and rereads the current metric. May
then records an owner, next action or justified wait and review boundary for unresolved
concerns, reusing linked work when its scope fits. Neither a delivered alert nor
an improved aggregate proves the original input recovered. The general observation
loop leaves Host retries independent; source verification is not live adoption.

The [systemic pattern-review proposal](../../evaluation.app/design/systemic-pattern-review-20260927.md)
refines recovered-finding disposition: May may settle an individual incident and
still investigate a repeated cause, material cost or coverage gap. A systemic
concern is evidence for value judgment, not an instruction to reopen the subject
or prepare a candidate. Reuse linked investigation work and preserve the exact
population window. The aggregate evidence and periodic selection path is deployed
locally; its [connected trial](../../evaluation.app/experiments/systemic-pattern-review-20260927/README.md)
found recurrence and also an unsupported deadline claim. May must inspect exact
current evidence before adopting a report's interpretation. Automatic report
intake is proven for that sample; full unattended candidate-to-application success
is not established by it.

The shared [behavior review method](../../may-agent.app/docs/2b-manual/workflow-authoring.md#checking-app-behavior)
uses existing App tests, bounded agent trials and Evaluation. May judges a
report's material interpretation against its evidence, including a claimed
healthy wait or no-op. A useful correction returns to the original owner;
an independently valuable implementation change follows the existing improvement
path. Routine no-concern reports retain a bounded review cost. The
[convergence review](../../may-agent.app/docs/proposals/app-behavior-convergence-20260925.md)
records source gaps separately from changed guidance and live behavior.

Repeated waits/no-ops without progress, health/outcome contradictions and
recurring similar fixes can justify the shared
[selective deeper review](../../may-agent.app/docs/2b-manual/workflow-authoring.md#when-to-deepen-a-review).
Evaluation reports the signal and bounded evidence gap; May judges value and
asks the existing owner to trace the failed decision and try a contrasting case.
Use ordinary Task input, current reports and the existing improvement path.
There is no additional daily review, fixed retry threshold or remediation queue.

## Recover current work; improve future work

Use the common [recovery rule](../../may-agent.app/docs/2a-design/system-boundary.md#recovery-uses-evidence-before-repetition).
May first helps the accountable owner restore its promised outcome within
existing authority. A corrected request, verified existing result, safe alternate
method or concrete owner action may be sufficient. This must not wait for an
Evaluation report, Gym comparison or a permanent source repair when authorized
recovery is already possible. If the defect prevents every safe route, the
repair can remain a real dependency of the original Task.

Evaluation independently checks outcomes, unresolved work and recovery claims;
it does not own immediate execution recovery. Its bounded selection and budgets
are not a delivery deadline or proof that every failure was examined. May judges
which confirmed problems merit further investigation; Bob/tech-lead contribute
bounded design and implementation work through the existing team arrangement.
Gym supplies a comparison when that adds evidence. No new incident App,
recovery agent, queue or standing model call is introduced by this distinction.

A retained recovery result should explain what now works, what remains owed,
the supporting execution/evidence references, and any remaining defect. Reuse
ordinary result/facts and links rather than adding a second status record.
Successful recovery does not erase a recurring defect; an improvement candidate
does not prove the original task recovered. May weighs recurrence, impact,
recovery cost and the simpler alternative before pursuing a permanent change.
Existing final human approval for generated improvements remains in force.
Already authorized corrections do not acquire a new approval requirement.

When human action is actually necessary, retain the exact supported human
Condition and deliver its concrete request through the existing Conversation/
Telegram path so it is also visible in `/todo`. A quiet wait or prose request
alone is not proof of delivery. Preserve all unrelated obligations.

## Improvement stages

1. **Source evidence:** Evaluation's immutable `evaluation.task_report_published` names subject App/Task, evidence hash, execution, report/facts paths and both content hashes. Publication can race its durable marker; May retries the same ordinary work and never trusts staged bytes. A supported execution failure instead returns through its exact caller relationship and is read from the accepted Task result plus its Task/attempt/operation identity and retained receipts. It is not converted into a synthetic Evaluation publication.
2. **Disposition (owner/May):** May uses Evaluation's published reader for Evaluation evidence and current Task/execution evidence for execution failures. The integrity intake returns the already checked judgment unchanged when it fits the bounded result, or an explicit omission with exact report identity when it does not; an omitted judgment cannot support no-action. Model identity does not change this intake boundary. Routine DeepSeek screening reports are leads: for a complete no-concern judgment May reads that judgment with the canonical current-subject result, checks the material wait/no-op premise, and records a bounded no-action when the evidence supports it. Focus the check on the decision rather than reconstructing every successful delivery claim or requiring a second full Evaluation. Material allegations, stale relevance, missing evidence, and proposed repair claims still require independent checks. May classifies each such claim as confirmed, rejected, or uncertain and never treats no-finding as certified health. "No code candidate" is only a repair disposition: a verified useful correction may still be owed to an existing accountable owner through its ordinary Task/input correlation. May reads the delivery receipt and accepted owner consideration; delivery or acknowledgment is not resolution, and any requested behavior recheck remains separate. This transport alone does not justify a duplicate Task or scan. She records impact, accountable owner, disposition, action or genuine wait, next step and evidence/repair links in the accepted Task result. An ordinary Task result may retain the structured intake. A Conversation result cannot add that raw object because its closed schema admits only the normal Conversation fields, so it retains exact publication subject/hashes/report references, handoff key and disposition in `facts`. Those facts must match the current publication before duplicate reuse; the live pilot observed one false reuse when a consumer treated a prior result for another publication as proof that the new input was handled. This is a documented prompt/procedure limitation, not a new Host field, registry, or enforcement mechanism. During a bounded screening rollout May also records useful impact and total verification cost/latency, so attempt allowance is not mislabeled as effective capacity. Expected failures and ordinary recovery create no repair. A subject becoming `done` or condition-free, or gaining deployment receipts, does not by itself prove recovery of a separately required independent review: the owner must retain that obligation until the exact review is admitted, returned, and judged. Duplicate transport reuses accepted work; semantic repair reuse remains owner judgment, and changed evidence is reconsidered without inheriting stale approval. A separate repair Task exists only for an independent outcome or lifetime.
3. **Candidate (responsible App):** existing isolated development prepares and commits one candidate with diff and verification receipts. Evaluation does not own this decision; Gym does not build it. A new error never selects a workflow automatically.
4. **Comparison (when useful):** an ordinary Gym goal carries a bounded `context.comparison`; Gym freezes exact baseline/candidate/benchmark/scorer/model/scenario/trial scope and returns a content-addressed receipt. Mechanical Host failures remain Host tests, not model training data. If Gym adds no value, retain a truthful comparison-decision artifact that says why it was not used, names the deterministic and local-agent evidence used instead, and states the limits. The approval packet hashes this artifact; it is not a fabricated Gym success receipt.
5. **Approval (human):** a packet binds baseline and candidate commits/tree, complete diff, verification and comparison hashes, activation scope and rollback. `prepare-improvement-approval` rereads the clean target and pinned evidence, applies May's necessity and candidate-value judgments, and returns an ordinary human-owned Condition containing the full readable proposal. An authenticated literal Telegram reply to that exact card is the primary decision path; it is bound to the provider message, Task generation, Condition and displayed bytes.
6. **Source application (May/operator fallback):** `scripts/apply-improvement.ts` rereads the trusted journal event, current owner Task/Condition, actual Git and evidence identities before fast-forwarding the exact source candidate. The retained receipt includes the authorizing event/proposal/Task and all input references. Missing, rejected, stale, superseded or mismatched approval makes zero mutation. The packet-hash operator route remains a fallback.
7. **Activation and outcome follow-up:** source approval is source-only unless the displayed packet separately includes the exact activation. For App-definition activation, `improvement-activation-follow-up` rereads the trusted journal decision, current owner Task/Condition and source receipt, then emits the existing `runtime.reload.requested` operation with the approved source commit pinned. The correlated result records the actual loaded source commit; drift, failure or an inconclusive identity remains open. Only after verified adoption and readable post-activation execution does the same owner request ordinary Evaluation of a concrete new subject Task. The Evaluation dependency is pinned to that generation/evidence; its result wakes the owner, which records the independent disposition without turning it into an automatic value claim. Reviewing the activation Task itself does not show that the intended improvement helped. Host-binary deployment and any scope not shown in the proposal remain separate explicit operator work.

Each stage has an explicit input/output and retained evidence naming its failure stage. Candidate readiness is not integration, approval, activation, or deployment. The accountable owner performs one reconciliation from current Task detail at each return: it reuses the retained selected subject and admitted `appdep_…` request rather than resampling or assuming the workflow-local dependency label is the public result identity. Outstanding activation, post-adoption subject selection, and Evaluation return remain visible through the same Task's Conditions and accepted result until the exact next fact arrives. Present that state using the common [current-first Task guidance](../../may-agent.app/docs/2a-design/human-control-surfaces.md#shared-resource-interface); the same document defines generic trusted-ingress, Condition, and operation-identity boundaries.

the initial installation and the [September 19 live verification](evaluation-improvement-rollout-20260919.md)
for the current deployed identities, real no-op intake, synthetic approval
coverage, retained failures and remaining limits.

## Routine review and human communication

Evaluation publications and linked Task returns are background inputs, not new
human questions. May retains a disposition in the accepted `summary` and `facts`,
with evidence and existing work links. Routine no-change decisions, unchanged
approval waits and background handoffs omit `response`. No new notification
queue or delivery policy is introduced.

The `metric-response` helper preserves an optional `conversation.response`
chosen by May. It supplies no default acknowledgment. Human input still requires
an answer; a new concrete decision or material change can merit a short reply.
Existing human Conditions and `/todo` retain pending approvals independently.

This requires the paired Host schema correction allowing a background `followUp`
without `response`. Deploy that correction before loading the App helper change.
The Host owns reliable acceptance and transport; May judges which findings are
useful to tell the human. Telegram activity controls belong to Telegram-origin
turns, so routine system reviews do not emit working/Stop cards.
