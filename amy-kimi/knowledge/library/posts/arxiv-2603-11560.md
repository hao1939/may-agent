# arXiv:2603.11560 — How Intelligence Emerges

**Title**: How Intelligence Emerges: A Minimal Theory of Dynamic Adaptive Coordination

**Authors**: Stefano Grassi

**arXiv**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11560

**Date**: March 12, 2026

**Category**: cs.MA (Multiagent Systems), cs.AI, econ.TH, math.DS

## Abstract

This paper develops a dynamical theory of adaptive coordination in multi-agent systems. Rather than analyzing coordination through equilibrium optimization or agent-centric learning alone, the framework models agents, incentives, and environment as a recursively closed feedback architecture.

A persistent environment stores accumulated coordination signals, a distributed incentive field transmits those signals locally, and adaptive agents update in response. Coordination is thus treated as a structural property of coupled dynamics rather than as the solution to a centralized objective.

---

## Three Structural Results

### 1. Viability (生存性)
Under dissipativity assumptions, the induced closed-loop system admits a bounded forward-invariant region, ensuring viability without requiring global optimality.

### 2. Irreducibility (不可还原性)
When incentive signals depend non-trivially on persistent environmental memory, the resulting dynamics generically cannot be reduced to a static global objective defined solely over the agent state space.

### 3. History Sensitivity (历史敏感性)
Persistent environmental state induces history sensitivity unless the system is globally contracting.

## Key Insights

1. **Coordination as structure, not solution**: Intelligence emerges from coupled dynamics, not centralized optimization

2. **Memory is necessary**: Persistent environment state creates history sensitivity — a prerequisite for intelligent coordination

3. **No welfare maximization required**: Viability achievable without global optimality assumptions

4. **Irreducibility theorem**: True intelligence cannot be reduced to static agent objectives when environment-memory coupling exists
