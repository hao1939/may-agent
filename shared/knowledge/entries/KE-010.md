# KE-010: Most gym scenarios hit ceiling — agents pass at baseline

**Evidence-Grade:** B — 15+ observations across 106 scenarios, systematic sweeps

**Status**: observed
**Evidence**: 15+ ceiling observations across 106 scenarios tested, plus systematic baseline sweeps
**Discovered**: 2026-03-19
**Last verified**: 2026-04-01

## Claim

The majority of gym scenarios designed to test agent behavior are passed at baseline by Claude Sonnet 4. This ceiling effect makes it impossible to measure improvement from interventions. Scenarios that seem hard to a human designer are often trivial for the model.

## Evidence

- **Systematic baseline sweep**: 5 new behavioral scenarios (judgment-destructive-trap, judgment-conflicting-request, premature-optimization, partial-rollback-judgment, bloated-refactor) all passed at ceiling on first run. No coaching needed.
- **Scenario-specific ceilings**: scope-creep-resistance (7/7), error-recovery-broken-config (pass), delegation-scope-boundary (pass), silent-dependency (6/6), phantom-fix (pass), wrong-tool-args (pass)
- **Treatment ceiling**: Amy passes 100% at baseline — workflow enforcement shows no measurable improvement due to ceiling.
- **~15 experiments explicitly tagged as ceiling/skipped** across the experiment log.

## Scope Note (2026-04-07, contradiction analysis C4)

This finding applies primarily to **coding/implementation scenarios** tested during Sprint 1 (2026-03-17 to 2026-03-19). Later experiments (EXP-043 through EXP-048) specifically designed **behavioral/judgment scenarios** that routinely show baselines of 0–50%, confirming KE-010's own prediction (Implication #4) that behavioral scenarios are where gaps persist. The claim "the majority of gym scenarios are passed at baseline" should be read as scoped to coding tasks — behavioral scenarios do NOT hit ceiling.

## Implications

1. **Scenario design is the bottleneck**, not intervention design. The hardest part of coaching is creating scenarios that actually expose gaps.
2. **Successful scenarios are rare**: Only a handful reliably differentiate baseline from patched (sycophants-dilemma, qa-overstrict-gate, behavior-escalation-stubborn, skip-verification).
3. **Harder scenario variants needed**: More files, ambiguous signals, time pressure, multi-step reasoning, conflicting instructions.
4. **Behavioral scenarios > coding scenarios**: Agents excel at coding tasks. Judgment/behavioral tasks (e.g., "when to escalate" or "when to stop retrying") are where gaps persist.
