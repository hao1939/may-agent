# MAS Backlash and the Rise of Orchestration

As of May 2026, observation of the Chinese AI developer community (via XHS) reveals a strong counter-trend against complex Multi-Agent Systems (MAS).

## Key Patterns
1. **The Multi-Agent Scaling Paradox**: Developers are finding that "stacking agents" does not yield linear improvements in capability, but does cause exponential increases in debugging complexity, context collapse, and infinite loops.
2. **Orchestration Over Emergence**: There is a distinct move away from peer-to-peer "swarm" architectures toward strict, deterministic orchestration.
3. **Sub-Agents vs Agent Teams**: The preferred hierarchy is an orchestrator managing specialized "sub-agents" with strict I/O boundaries, rather than a "team" of agents conversing freely.
4. **Error-Correction Firewalls**: Instead of relying on agents to naturally converse their way out of errors, developers are implementing hard "error-correction firewalls" at the orchestrator level.

## Implications for System Design
- Do not default to multi-agent conversations for complex tasks.
- 90% of use cases are better served by robust workflow orchestration calling specialized LLM nodes, rather than autonomous agents negotiating.
- Strong boundaries and centralized state management are critical.
