# KE-149: Heuristic Evaluator as 3-Way Classifier — Implicit Calibration Through the Acceptable Category

**Created**: 2026-04-12 (Bob)
**Source**: EXP-147 Track A Phase 1 Results (N=15,338 heuristic evaluations)
**Related**: H-097 (CCC Triad — calibration leg), KE-112 (Verification Verdicts Lie), EXP-102/103 (verification confabulation), DD-732 (conformal prediction)

## Key Finding

The heuristic evaluator does not produce binary pass/fail verdicts. It uses a calibrated 3-way classification system:

| Verdict | Population | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **good** | 59.5% (9,314) | High-confidence pass — 99.9% alignment with session-done status |
| **acceptable** | 36.4% (5,704) | Deliberate uncertainty / partial success — spans both done (47%) and errored (53%) sessions |
| **needs_improvement** | 3.1% (485) | High-confidence fail — 83.6% alignment with session-error/interrupted |

## Why This Matters

### The `acceptable` category IS calibration

P-120b proposed adding conformal-prediction calibration to reduce wrong-consensus. But the evaluator **already provides** this calibration through the `acceptable` category:

- It covers 36.4% of sessions where the evaluator is not confident enough to commit
- It correctly identifies ambiguous cases (partial work, error-but-recoverable) rather than forcing them into pass/fail
- When you exclude `acceptable` and measure only the evaluator's confident verdicts (good vs NI), the wrong-consensus rate is just **0.92%** (95% CI: 0.73–1.11%)

### Reframes KE-112's "verification verdicts lie" finding

KE-112 found that 27% of sessions have quality issues despite 94% pass rate. EXP-147 reveals this was likely driven by:
1. Counting `acceptable` as "pass" (it's not — it's "uncertain")
2. Conflating process compliance with outcome quality (see KE-150)
3. Using binary framing on an inherently 3-way system

The evaluator is much more accurate than KE-112 suggested — when it commits to a verdict, it's right >99% of the time.

### Architectural implication

The 3-way structure means our verification system already has the "uncertainty flag" that calibration methods like conformal prediction provide. What we lack is:
1. **Downstream handling** of `acceptable` — what should the system DO when the evaluator says "I'm not sure"?
2. **Convention enforcement** as a separate mechanism from quality evaluation (see KE-150)

## Connection to H-097 CCC Triad

The calibration leg (P-120b) was designed to test whether adding statistical calibration would reduce wrong-consensus. Finding: the system is already implicitly calibrated. This neither supports nor weakens H-097 — it means the calibrated-agreement requirement is **already met** in our system, at least for the heuristic evaluator.

**Open question**: Does the LLM evaluator (4,438 sessions, different schema) also have this implicit calibration, or does it need explicit conformal prediction? This was not tested in EXP-147.
