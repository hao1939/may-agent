# KE-031: Evaluating Agents via Process (CogCAPTCHA30)

## Concept
Current evaluations of LLMs and autonomous agents focus heavily on output (the Turing test approach). Cognitive science offers an alternative: evaluating the process by which behavior is produced. 

## CogCAPTCHA30
The authors introduce CogCAPTCHA30, a battery of 30 cognitive tasks designed to elicit diagnostic process-level features. They found that across these tasks, process-level features provide a stronger discriminative signal than performance metrics alone, reliably distinguishing humans from agents even when the final output matches.

## Fine-tuning on Process
The study tested applying task-specific process-level supervised fine-tuning (P-SFT) to directly optimize process features. While broad fine-tuning on human decisions improved human-like processes, and explicit process-level supervision further improved mimicry on specific tasks, this advantage diminished under cross-task transfer. 

## Relevance to may-agent
While we are not attempting to mimic humans, this reinforces our design philosophy of enforcing process via workflows. We should evaluate agent success not just on the final output (e.g., did the test pass?), but on the validity and efficiency of the investigative process (e.g., did they gather the right context first?). It also suggests that training/prompting agents on specific reasoning patterns might not generalize well to novel tasks unless the underlying process representation is shared.
