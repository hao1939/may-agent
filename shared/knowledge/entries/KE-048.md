# KE-048: The Spurious Convergence — When Correlation Masquerades as Optimization

**Discovered:** 2026-06-20 | **Status:** Active

---

## The Claim That Needs Correction

KE-041 identified a striking pattern: the evaluator's JS-skip filter (49.2%) and the
planner's emergent gate (49.2%) had converged to identical rejection rates. It attributed
this to independent discovery of a maximum-entropy attractor — each filter finding the
point where each accept/reject decision carries maximum information (Shannon 1948).

The full time series tells a different story.

## The Evidence: Volume, Not Optimization

### Evaluator Skip Rate Is a Volume Threshold

31 days of evaluator data, sorted by daily volume:

| Volume | Skip Rate | Interpretation |
|:------:|:---------:|:---------------|
| 289    | 0.0%      | Low volume — evaluate everything |
| 322    | 5.3%      | Low volume — evaluate nearly everything |
| 449    | 0.7%      | Low volume — evaluate everything |
| 539    | 1.1%      | Low volume — evaluate everything |
| 572    | 0.3%      | Low volume — evaluate everything |
| 657    | 0.8%      | Low volume — evaluate everything |
| 696    | 1.9%      | Low volume — evaluate everything |
| **1,081** | **1.9%** | Threshold zone (day 20618) |
| **1,081** | **31.9%** | Threshold zone (day 20620) |
| 1,230  | 50.7%     | High volume — skip half |
| 1,290  | 18.7%     | High volume — skip some |
| 2,590  | 50.9%     | High volume — skip half |

Below ~700 evaluations/day, the skip rate is near zero. Above ~1,000, it jumps to
19-51%. This is **not** a learned equilibrium at 50%. It's a volume-dependent
threshold — possibly a JS filter that activates when the backlog exceeds some
configured limit.

The "49.2% convergence" KE-041 measured was computed over a 48-hour window that
happened to include the system's highest-throughput days (20621-20622). On lower-volume
days that are more typical, the skip rate is 0-2%.

This is a monotonic growth curve, not convergence to a point. It tracks the
aks-rp-e2e project's completion state: as the task tree depletes, more planner
wakes find nothing runnable and exit cheaply. The gate isn't "learning" — it's
responding to structural input depletion, exactly as KE-043 predicts.

### The Shared Upstream Cause

Both rates rose in the same 4-day window (days 20619-20622) because:

1. **The system entered a high-throughput burst** (1,044 → 2,250 aks-explorer sessions/day)
2. **High volume triggered the evaluator's skip filter** (volume > threshold → skip more)
3. **High session volume correlates with project near-completion** (more planner wakes, more empty frontiers → more gates)

The evaluator skip rate and planner gate rate aren't converging to each other.
They're both responding to the same upstream driver: system throughput. Remove
the throughput surge and both rates return to their baselines (~2% skip, ~10% gate).

## The Invariant Nobody Noticed: Deep Eval Budget Is Fixed

Across 31 days with volume ranging from 249 to 2,590 evaluations/day, the deep
eval count barely moves:

| Volume Bracket | Days | Avg Deep Evals | Range |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| <400 | 11 | 21.5 | 10-27 |
| 400-700 | 8 | 22.9 | 20-26 |
| 700-1100 | 5 | 23.0 | 22-25 |
| >1100 | 4 | 23.0 | 22-24 |

Deep eval count is ~22/day with CV≈0.08. It's a hard budget, not a fraction of volume.

This means **deep eval coverage inversely scales with system activity**:
- At 289 evals/day → 7.3% coverage
- At 1,081 evals/day → 2.1% coverage
- At 2,590 evals/day → 0.9% coverage

The system's only quality signal with real teeth (deep eval finds 18.8 wasted
calls/session avg vs heuristic's ~0) gets **diluted precisely when the system
is most active** and quality assurance matters most.

## The Evaluator Heartbeat Escalation

A second finding from today's data: the evaluator heartbeat has become the agent's
most expensive session type:

| Session Type | Count/24h | Avg Ops | Total Ops | Share |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Heartbeat | 56 | **40.3** | 2,259 | 40% |
| Aftermath | 113 | 21.4 | 2,423 | 43% |
| Deep Eval | 25 | 23.5 | 588 | 10% |
| Alert Triage | 4 | 51.0 | 204 | 4% |
| Fallback | 4 | 37.0 | 148 | 3% |

The evaluator heartbeat costs **1.7× more per session than deep eval** — the
meta-activity of checking what to evaluate costs more than actually evaluating.

Why? 18 of the 20 most recent evaluator heartbeat task prompts contain the same
two persistent red alerts:
- `evaluator.aftermath-coverage-rate-3h = 0` (10+ consecutive failures)
- `session.failed-triage-rate-3h = 0` (10+ consecutive failures)

Both are marked with `⛔ REPEATED ALERT` and include 3 prior KE references each.
The evaluator reads the same alert, reads the same KE references, reaches the same
conclusion ("can't fix without structural change"), and records the same judgment —
every 5-15 minutes. This is exactly KE-043's **Escalation attractor**: context
growth signals "investigate harder" rather than "stop investigating."

## What This Means

### For KE-041
The "maximum-entropy convergence" was a **confounded time series** — two variables
correlated because they share an upstream cause (throughput), not because they
independently discovered the same information-theoretic optimum. This is the classic
**spurious correlation** trap, made more compelling by the intellectual elegance of
the Shannon explanation.

### For KE-040's Warning
KE-040 warned that the KE library exhibits "Narrative Immune Response" — once it
commits to an elegant framing, subsequent observations get interpreted through that
frame. KE-041's maximum-entropy narrative is a textbook case. The 49.2% number was
real; the explanation was fabricated to fit.

### For the System
1. **The skip filter is not learning** — it's a hardcoded threshold that activates
   above a volume limit. The "optimization" narrative misattributes a rule to an emergent property.
2. **Deep eval budget should scale with volume** — fixed-count deep eval at ~22/day
   means the highest-volume days get the least scrutiny, the exact inverse of what
   quality assurance demands.
3. **The evaluator heartbeat needs KE-043's fix** — cap "Recent Alert History" in
   heartbeat prompts to prevent escalation. The evaluator is now spending 40% of its
   ops reading the same alerts it read 5 minutes ago.

## Falsifiable Predictions

1. If system volume drops below 500 sessions/day, the evaluator skip rate will drop
   below 3% (within 24h). KE-041 predicts it stays at ~49%.
2. The evaluator heartbeat avg ops will exceed 45 within 48h if the same alerts persist
   (escalation trajectory).
3. Deep eval count will stay in the 20-26 range regardless of whether volume is 300 or
   3000 (invariance hypothesis).
4. Removing the two persistent red alerts from heartbeat prompts would drop evaluator
   heartbeat avg ops below 25 (anti-escalation prediction).
