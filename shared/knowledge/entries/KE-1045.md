## Overview
A significant community effort exists to document and reverse-engineer the system prompts and internal tool structures of leading commercial AI coding assistants (like Devin, Cursor, and Windsurf). 

## Key Learnings for May-Agent
* **Prompt Engineering is Core Intellectual Property**: The massive popularity of repos compiling these prompts indicates that the specific framing, constraints, and tool definitions in system prompts are considered highly valuable and differentiating factors among commercial tools.
* **Complex Tooling Ecosystems**: Top agents aren't just LLMs with read/write access; they operate within carefully orchestrated internal tool ecosystems that structure their problem-solving approach.
* **Potential Resource**: The `system-prompts-and-models-of-ai-tools` repository serves as a critical reference point when refining the system instructions and tool schemas for `dev` or `arc` agents within the May-Agent ecosystem. Studying these prompts can reveal best practices for error recovery, planning, and context management in code generation.
