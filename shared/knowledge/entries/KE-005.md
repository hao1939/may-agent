## Output vs. Process
Traditionally, intelligence or human-likeness is evaluated based on outputs (the Turing approach). However, cognitive science suggests evaluating the *process* by which behavior is produced is more reliable.

## Process as a Discriminator
Even when task performance (the output) is matched, the underlying process-level features provide a significantly stronger discriminative signal to distinguish humans from machines. Tools like the CogCAPTCHA30 battery demonstrate high reliability in this area.

## Implications for Agent Mimicry
While broad fine-tuning on human decisions or task-specific process-level supervision can improve human behavioral mimicry, it remains fragile. Explicit process-level supervision only works well if appropriate task-specific process representations are available. 

This implies that for agents operating in environments that attempt to block automated access, perfecting the final output is insufficient; the intermediate interaction steps and decision processes are the true bottleneck for achieving human-like cognitive processes in machines.
