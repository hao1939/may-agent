As of May 2026, the Chinese AI developer community (via XHS) shows a maturation in how AI agents are conceptualized and built, moving away from unstructured prompts to rigorous software engineering principles.

## Key Trends

1. **Structured Capability Boundaries**: There is significant interest in defining clear boundaries for agent capabilities, often referred to as a "6-layer architecture." This suggests a move away from monolithic "do-it-all" prompts towards compartmentalized, predictable agent behaviors.
2. **Recursive Skill Composition**: The evolution path is increasingly viewed as `Prompt -> Skill -> Agent`. Developers are focusing on building granular "skills" that can be recursively combined, rather than trying to build complex behavior directly into the core agent loop.
3. **The "Harness" Upper Bound**: Evaluation continues to be a primary concern. The community is discussing "Agent Judges" and specific frameworks like "Harness," suggesting that an agent's performance ceiling is determined by the quality and rigor of the evaluation harness surrounding it.
4. **SLM Adoption**: Consistent with other platforms, there is a push to use Small Language Models (SLMs) for specific, optimized tasks (such as RL training loops) rather than relying solely on large, generalized models.
5. **Skepticism of Generic Agents**: There is high-engagement sentiment that the vast majority of generic AI agent startups will fail ("做AI Agent的99%会死掉"). The successful models are expected to be highly specialized or strictly orchestrated (e.g., large-scale distributed orchestration systems like "MegaFlow").

## Implications for May-Agent

- **Validation of Structure**: These trends strongly validate our internal architecture of distinct roles, well-defined tools/skills, and strict operational guards.
- **Evaluation Improvements**: We should investigate the principles behind the "Harness" evaluation concepts to see if they can reduce the procedural noise currently affecting our internal `agent-eval-agent` mechanisms (as noted in KE-1014 and KE-1026).
