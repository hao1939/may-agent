## Core Finding: Social Dilemma in Hybrid Populations

> "In hybrid populations where humans delegate strategic decision-making to autonomous agents, understanding when and how cooperative behaviors can emerge remains a key challenge."

### The Energy Load Management Social Dilemma

When consumers selfishly schedule appliances during low-price periods:
- **Individual rationality**: Schedule at preferred start time (PST) to minimize personal cost
- **Collective outcome**: Peak demand triggers expensive generators → higher costs for everyone
- **Social optimum**: Cooperative turn-taking (alternating schedules) reduces aggregate cost by 20%

| Scenario | Individual Cost | Aggregate Cost | Equilibrium? |
|----------|----------------|----------------|--------------|
| Both at PST | $500 | $1000 | Nash Equilibrium ❌ |
| Turn-taking | $250/$550 | $800 | Social Optimum ✅ |

### Mathematical Condition for Cooperation

For successful coordination (turn-taking) to be viable:

```
p < δ
```

Where:
- `p` = inconvenience cost of deviating from preferred schedule (0 < p < 1)
- `δ` = discount factor (continuation probability)

**Critical Insight**: Cooperation is only possible when the immediate cost of cooperation is less than the value of future interactions.

## Key Innovation: Intrinsic Reward via Globally Observable Signals

### Artificial Agent Design

Instead of purely selfish optimization, artificial agents incorporate an **intrinsic reward** based on:
- **Aggregate demand** (全局可观测)
- **Price signals** (全局可观测)
- **No preference sharing required** (保护隐私)

This shifts the evolutionary basin toward cooperation without centralized control.

### Experimental Results

Using two-population replicator dynamics and Monte Carlo simulations:
- Artificial agents **shift learning dynamics** toward cooperative outcomes
- **Entry resilience**: Unilateral adoption doesn't disadvantage early adopters
- **Partial adoption** can still improve aggregate outcomes

## Critical Finding: The Free-Rider Problem

> "Non-adopters may benefit disproportionately from the cooperation induced by adopters."

| Parameter Regime | Adopter Outcome | Non-Adopter Outcome | Strategic Issue |
|------------------|-----------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Most cases | Improved | Improved | Asymmetric but positive |
| Some regimes | Slightly improved | **Disproportionately improved** | Free-riding concern |

This asymmetry doesn't preclude beneficial entry but warrants consideration in deployment.
