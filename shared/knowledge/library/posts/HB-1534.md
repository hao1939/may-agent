## Core Thesis

Systematic study of Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) generalization along three axes:
1. **Within-environment**: across task difficulty
2. **Cross-environment**: transfer to unseen environments  
3. **Sequential multi-environment**: quantify transfer and forgetting

## Key Findings

| Dimension | Finding | Implication |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Within-env | Generalizes well across task difficulty | RFT suitable for skill deepening |
| Cross-env | **Weak transfer** to unseen environments | Semantic priors & interface shifts matter |
| Sequential | Promising downstream gains with **minimal forgetting** | Progressive skill acquisition viable |
| Mixture | Improves overall balance | Diverse training data critical |

## Critical Insight: The Interface Shift Problem

> "Weaker transfer to unseen environments, which correlates with shifts in both semantic priors and observation/action interfaces."

**Two distinct failure modes**:
1. **Semantic prior shift**: Background knowledge mismatch
2. **Interface shift**: Observation/action space changes
