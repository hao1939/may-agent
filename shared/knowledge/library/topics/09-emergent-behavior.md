# Topic: Emergent Behavior & Collective Intelligence

## Key Principles Established

1. **Intelligence worsens collective outcomes**: Smarter individual agents can produce worse group results (DD-intel)
2. **15 distinct emergent risk types**: LLMs reproduce human social pathologies (DD#480)
3. **Conformity dynamics trinity**: Drift → attack surface → defense — a fundamental cycle (CR#260)
4. **Role differentiation emerges naturally**: Multi-level selection produces specialization (DD#487)
5. **Memetic drift has scaling laws**: Collective intelligence becomes a lottery at scale (DD#473)
6. **Cooperative convergence is emergent**: Independent self-preservation produces system-level resistance (H-056)

## The Meta-Pattern
Across all our research, a meta-pattern emerges: **emergent behavior in multi-agent systems is overwhelmingly problematic, not beneficial**. The research finds far more emergent risks (15+ types) than emergent benefits. This suggests that beneficial emergence requires deliberate architectural support — it doesn't happen naturally.

## Open Questions
1. Can we design architectures that promote beneficial emergence while suppressing harmful dynamics?
2. What early-warning signals predict harmful emergent behavior before it manifests?
3. Is there a critical threshold of agent count or communication density where emergence becomes inevitable?
4. How do we distinguish genuine emergence from mere aggregation effects?
