# KE-411: Step-Start Priming Outperforms Post-Hoc Guards for Behavioral Compliance

**Created**: 2027-10-15 | **Source**: EXP-256, H-EXP-049
**Confidence**: High (225 measurements, controlled experiment)

## Finding

Injecting a 1-line reminder via `step_start` event (Tier 2 structural intervention) achieves **84% RBE compliance** vs **50% baseline** (context text alone or post-hoc recovery guards). This is a +34 percentage point improvement.

## Intervention Tier Hierarchy (validated)

| Tier | Mechanism | RBE Compliance | Evidence |
|------|-----------|---------------|----------|
| 4 | Context.md text (declarative) | ~50% | EXP-239 baseline |
| 3 | Post-hoc recovery guard | ~50% | EXP-254 |
| **2** | **Step-start priming (warn demand)** | **~84%** | **EXP-256** |
| 1 | Pre-tool-call hard block | Untested | EXP-255 (blocked on runtime) |

## Key Insight

The moment of intervention matters more than the content. A 1-line reminder at `step_start` outperforms a full recovery protocol injected after failure. This is because:
1. **Recency**: the reminder is the last thing read before acting
2. **Decision-point proximity**: fires at the moment the agent chooses its next action
3. **Low cost**: 1 warn per step vs multi-step recovery sequences

## Application

Use `step_start` warn demands for any behavioral compliance pattern where:
- The desired behavior is simple (1-2 steps)
- Agents know the rule but skip it under cognitive load
- Post-hoc correction is expensive (recovery steps)

## Limitations

- 84% is not 100% — some agents still skip despite the reminder
- Only Tier 1 (hard block) likely achieves >95%, but requires runtime support
- Tested primarily on RBE; generalizability to other behaviors unconfirmed
