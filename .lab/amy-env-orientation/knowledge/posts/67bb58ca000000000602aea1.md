🔍 问题背景：长期以来，人类语言能力与非语言思维能力（如逻辑推理或社会推理）之间的关系一直是学术界的热点问题。神经科学领域的研究表明，处理语言和非语言推理刺激时，人脑的活动模式不同，这暗示了语言与思维能力之间的分离。然而，这种分离是否也存在于大型语言模型（LLMs）中，仍然值得探讨。
💡 研究动机：为了探索LLMs中语言和计算能力的分离情况，研究团队聚焦于简单的算术技能（如1+2=？）作为非语言思维能力的代表，并分析这些技能在LLMs内部表示空间中的几何编码方式。
🚀 方法简介：本文通过线性分类器和聚类可分离性测试，研究了语言和算术输入在LLMs内部表示空间中的分布情况。研究团队使用了多个公开数据集，并通过控制实验来消除潜在的混淆因素。
📊 实验设计：实验在三个不同类型的LLMs上进行，包括Gemma-2-9b-it、Llama-3.1-8B-Instruction和Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct。实验数据包括通用语言文本、带数字的问题、简单算术方程及其拼写形式。实验结果显示，语言和算术输入在所有层中都被清晰地分隔成不同的聚类。

## Abstract

The association between language and (nonlinguistic) *thinking* ability in humans has long been debated, and recently, neuroscientific evidence of brain activity patterns has been considered. Such a scientific context naturally raises an interdisciplinary question — what about such a *language-thought dissociation* in large language models (LLMs)? In this paper, as an initial foray, we explore this question by focusing on simple arithmetic skills (e.g., 1+2=?) as a *thinking* ability and analyzing the geometry of their encoding in LLMs' representation space. Our experiments with linear classifiers and cluster separability tests demonstrate that simple arithmetic equations and general language input are encoded in completely separated regions in LLMs' internal representation space across *all* the layers, which is also supported with more controlled stimuli (e.g., spelled-out equations). These tentatively suggest that arithmetic reasoning is mapped into a distinct region from general language input, which is in line with the neuroscientific observations of human brain activations, while we also point out their somewhat cognitively implausible geometric properties.
