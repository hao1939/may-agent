## Full Text
📖标题：Outcome-based Exploration for LLM Reasoning
🌐来源：arXiv, 2509.06941
	
🛎️文章简介
🔸研究问题：在大语言模型（LLM）的推理过程中，如何有效地平衡准确性和生成多样性？
🔸主要贡献：论文提出了一种基于结果的探索方法，能改善LLM推理中的准确性与多样性之间的权衡。
	
📝重点思路
🔸研究将强化学习（RL）视为一种采样过程，分析其如何影响模型生成的多样性。
🔸区分历史探索与批量探索，提出批量结果基础探索以改善测试时间的生成多样性。
🔸采用上置信界（UCB）策略，将探索激励仅基于最终结果，从而提高模型的学习效率。
	
🔎分析总结
🔸研究发现，多样性损失不仅在测试阶段出现，训练过程中也会因集中于已解决的问题而导致多样性下降（称为多样性转移效应）。
🔸在可验证的推理任务中，结果空间的可处理性使得结果基础的探索方法能够有效优化模型，而避免了广义RL探索的复杂性。
🔸实验结果表明，所提出的探索方法在多个指标上均优于传统的强化学习基线，提高了多样性和准确性的平衡效果。
	
💡个人观点
论文将传统的RL探索方法重新构思为基于最终结果的探索策略，不仅有效增强了模型的多样性，同时能在训练过程中保持较高的准确性。

**Figure 1** Test performance comparison (averaged across `MATH-500`, `AIME2024/2025`, `AMC23`) between our exploration methods (`UCB-Con` and `Batch`) and the `GRPO` baseline, with `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct` on the easy dataset (left) and `Qwen-2.5-7B-Base` on the medium dataset (right). We report pass@k for k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32} on an early checkpoint (at timestep 100) and the final checkpoint (at timestep 700). We repeat each experiment with 3 different random seeds and plot the mean performance. The exploration methods outperform the baseline on nearly all metrics across the training process (except `Qwen-2.5-7B-Base` with `UCB-Con` on pass@1 on the early checkpoint due to exploration, but it has much higher pass@32 rate), and better exploitation-exploration trade-off and mitigation of overoptimization (note that the last checkpoint of Llama 3.1 8B with Vanilla RL has overall worse performance than its early checkpoint due to overoptimization).

**Figure 2** Comparison between RL training dynamics and base model sampling, on both easy and medium difficulty datasets, with `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct` and `Qwen-2.5-7B-Base`. Top row: number of questions solved so far; Bottom row: number of different answers sampled so far. The bottom x-ticks are the number of epochs *t* for training, and the top x-ticks are the corresponding *k* for sampling from the base model. We convert *k* = *nt* where *n* is the number of samples per epoch and *t* is the epoch index. We use *n* = 16 for pass@*k* comparison and *n* = 8 for diff@*k* comparison. In the diff@*k* comparison, solid lines denote the average number of different answers per all questions, and dashed lines denote the average number of different answers per unsolved questions (i.e., all answers are wrong so far). The fact that RL has lower diff@*k* on unsolved questions than the base model indicates the transfer of diversity degradation.
