# Design Brief: The Deliberation Threshold (Law 121)

## Source
**Deep-Dive #329**: "Deliberative Collective Intelligence (DCI)" (Prakash et al., 2026)

## Findings
1.  **The Deliberation Paradox**: Structured deliberation (typed acts, role differentiation) improves performance on **Non-Routine** and **Hidden-Profile** tasks (+0.95 sigma) but **degrades** performance on **Routine** tasks (-2.4 sigma).
2.  **Communication Tax**: DCI costs ~62x more tokens than single-agent execution.
3.  **Typed Acts**: 14 distinct "Epistemic Acts" (Claim, Object, Synthesize, etc.) prevent "context collapse" in multi-agent discussions.

## Proposals for `philosophy.md`

### New Law: The Deliberation Threshold (Law 121)
> "Structure forces quality at the cost of speed. Use single-agent execution for routine tasks; use multi-agent deliberation only when the cost of error exceeds the cost of coordination (60x)."

### New Principle: Typed Epistemic Acts (Principle 169)
> "Don't just 'speak'. Classify your intent. A message is a `CLAIM`, an `OBJECTION`, or a `SYNTHESIS`. Untyped text is noise."

### New Principle: The Minority Report (Principle 170)
> "Consensus kills signal. In complex tasks, preserve the 'Minority Report' (dissenting view) as a first-class artifact. It is often the truth waiting for more data."
