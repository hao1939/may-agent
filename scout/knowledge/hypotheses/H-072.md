# H-072: Belief-State Externalization Reduces Verification Gravity Wells

**Status:** proposed
**Source:** DD#860 (2512.20111 — ABBEL), EXP-099 (verification gravity well)
**Date:** 2026-04-22
**Author:** scout

## Hypothesis

If agents periodically externalize their belief state (a structured summary of what they know, what's uncertain, and what they're trying to verify) before taking verification actions, the rate of verification gravity well errors (FM-3.3 terminal attractor) will decrease because corrupted beliefs become visible and correctable before they cascade.

## Grounding

Lidayan et al. (2512.20111) show that belief bottleneck agents are **prone to error propagation** — errors in belief updating compound across steps. However, when beliefs are made explicit and graded for quality, performance improves beyond full-context baselines. Our EXP-099 identified that 20.3% of errors are verification gravity wells where agents get stuck in error-compounding loops. The connection: these loops may be driven by corrupted implicit beliefs that are never surfaced or checked.

## Connection to Existing Hypotheses

- **Extends EXP-099**: Provides a mechanistic explanation for why verification gravity wells form — the agent's implicit belief state becomes corrupted, and subsequent verification steps operate on the corrupted belief
- **Complements H-071** (Structured Disagreement): Multi-agent belief comparison could detect corrupted beliefs without RL training
- **Informs KE-106** (Guard Architecture): Guards check outputs; belief-state checks would catch errors earlier in the causal chain

## Testable Predictions

1. Adding a "state your current understanding" step before verification actions will reduce FM-3.3 error rate
2. Agents in verification gravity wells will show belief states that diverge from ground truth earlier than their outputs do
3. The benefit will be largest for long-horizon tasks where belief corruption has more steps to compound
