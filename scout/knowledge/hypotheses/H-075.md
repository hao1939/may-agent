# H-075: Surface Metrics May Mask Coordination Breakdown

**Status:** PARTIALLY-CONFIRMED
**Source:** DD782 (Superficial Success vs. Internal Breakdown, 2604.18951)
**Date:** 2026-06-28

## Hypothesis

Our current outcome-based metrics (hypotheses-generated, findings-used, coverage) may be showing "green" while actual inter-agent coordination quality is degraded — a form of illusory coordination.

## Evidence For
- DD782 empirically demonstrates this exact failure mode in adaptive MAS
- Our `findings-used: 0.541` is measured by citation presence, not by whether findings actually influenced decisions
- We lack any process-level coordination metrics

## Evidence Against
- Qualitative reviews of agent outputs suggest genuine engagement
- The system has produced novel insights that required cross-agent synthesis

## Testable Prediction
If we instrument deeper coordination metrics (e.g., tracking whether Bob's architectural decisions change after reading scout findings vs. when scout findings are withheld), we'll find that at least 30% of "used" findings had no measurable impact on decisions.

## Priority
HIGH — if true, our metrics are misleading us about system health

## Resolution (2026-06-28)

**PARTIALLY-CONFIRMED** by two experiments with divergent findings:

- **EXP-206** (retrospective KE audit, n=16): 56% of scout→bob citations are cosmetic, 25% strongly influenced decisions. **Confirms** at 30% threshold.
- **EXP-209** (session-level audit, n=10): Only 10% superficial, 60% causal. **Refutes** at 30% threshold.

**Reconciliation**: The discrepancy is methodological. EXP-206 audited *knowledge entries* downstream (many KEs are never referenced again). EXP-209 audited *sessions where bob actively cited scout* (selection bias toward engaged use). The truth is level-dependent: scout findings that bob actively chooses to cite are genuinely useful (90%), but many scout-derived KEs sit unused (56%). The metric is partially misleading — it overstates breadth of coordination while understating depth.

**Action**: Keep the `findings-used` metric but add a `decision-delta` metric that tracks whether cited findings changed a downstream decision.
