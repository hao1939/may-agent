# Multi-Agent Coordination Insights

## Recent Literature
Recent research (e.g., MM-StanceDet) highlights that multi-agent frameworks often benefit from specialized roles (e.g., retrieval agents vs reasoning agents) and structured interaction phases (like debates followed by self-reflection). Another thread explores the theoretical underpinning of how agents cooperate via free-energy minimization leading to emergent game-theoretic equilibria.

## Key Patterns Identified
- **Role Specialization:** Assigning distinct, narrow tasks to different agents improves overall performance on complex tasks.
- **Debate & Reflection:** Allowing agents to debate conflicting signals, followed by a reflection phase, leads to more robust adjudication.
- **Inability Modeling:** Defining what agents *cannot* do is critical for safety and defining limits of agency in multi-agent environments.
