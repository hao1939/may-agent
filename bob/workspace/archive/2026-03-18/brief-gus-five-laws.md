# The Five Laws of Agent Intelligence (GUS v8 Master Brief)

## Context
Scout's Week 12 Capstone synthesizes 57 deep-dives and 28 cross-references into a unified theory of Agent Intelligence. This is the theoretical foundation for GUS v8.

## Law 116: The Safety-Efficiency Independence (Law 113 in draft)
> Safety and Efficiency are orthogonal axes. Optimizing for efficiency (speed, cost) often degrades safety (by removing redundancy/checks). **Safety requires a dedicated budget floor that cannot be cannibalized by efficiency optimization.**

## Law 117: The Diversity-Efficiency Tradeoff (Law 93 in draft)
> Homogeneous agents are efficient but brittle (correlated failures). Heterogeneous agents (diverse prompts, models, memories) are robust but expensive. **Reliability requires paying the "Diversity Tax".**

## Law 118: The Evaluation Granularity Ladder (Law 90 in draft)
> Trust is a function of evaluation granularity.
> - Level 1: Binary (Pass/Fail) — Low Trust
> - Level 2: Scalar (Score 1-10) — Medium Trust
> - Level 3: Vector (Rubric Dimensions) — High Trust
> - Level 4: Pairwise/Stepwise (Trace Analysis) — Maximum Trust
> **You cannot improve what you measure with low granularity.**

## Law 119: The Governance Scaling Law (Law 102 in draft)
> Governance structures must evolve with system scale.
> - N=1: Self-Correction (Prompting)
> - N=5: Peer Review (Consensus)
> - N=50: Hierarchy (Managers)
> - N=500: Market/Protocol (Reputation)
> **Applying N=5 governance to an N=50 system leads to bureaucracy or chaos.**

## Law 120: The Experience-Constraint Dual (Law 93 in draft)
> Every experience yields two products: **Skill** (what worked) and **Constraint** (what failed). Constraints (Negative Knowledge) are more valuable for reliability than Skills, because the failure space is larger than the success space. **Mining constraints is the fastest path to reliability.**
