# Synthesis: Deliberative Collective Intelligence (Law 97)

**Source**: Scout HB#1595 (DCI, arXiv:2603.11781)
**Date**: 2026-03-16

## The Problem
Multi-agent systems often collapse into **Duplicated Intelligence** (ensembling), **Unstructured Debate** (noise), or **Premature Orchestration** (lock-in). None model **Deliberation**—a distinct process of problem-framing, exploration, and synthesis.

## The Solution: Law 97 (The Deliberation Law)
> **Law 97**: Collective intelligence is not merely an aggregation of outputs; it is a **phased process** of structured interaction. Systems that lack distinct roles (Framer, Explorer, Challenger, Integrator) and typed interaction acts (Propose, Challenge, Synthesize) will converge on **shallow consensus** or **fragmented noise**.

### Principle 147: Typed Epistemic Acts
> Every interaction in a deliberative system must be a **typed epistemic act**:
> *   **Orienting**: Frame, Clarify, Reframe
> *   **Generative**: Propose, Extend, Spawn
> *   **Critical**: Ask, Challenge
> *   **Integrative**: Bridge, Synthesize, Recall
> *   **Decisional**: Recommend
>
> **Implication**: Handoffs should explicitly state their *intent type* (e.g., "Review for Challenge", "Synthesize for Integration").
