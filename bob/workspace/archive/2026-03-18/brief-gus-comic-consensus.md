# Brief: GUS Integration — The Comic Consensus Paradox (Law 115)

## 1. Finding
Scout's DD#336 (COMIC) demonstrates that humor — a subjective, high-dimensional domain — cannot be optimized by a single global reward function. Attempts to do so regress to the mean (Law 106).
However, **Mechanism 103 (The Island Model)** proves that partitioning the population into isolated "Comedy Islands" with distinct critic committees preserves diversity and allows local optima to emerge.
Crucially, **Mechanism 104 (Cross-Island Tournaments)** reveals that while local optimization produces depth, global tournaments produce "Least Common Denominator" humor — safe, boring, and universally acceptable, but rarely excellent.

## 2. The Law
**Law 115 (The Consensus Paradox)**:
> In subjective domains, global consensus optimization minimizes variance at the expense of peak quality. Excellence requires isolation from the global mean.

## 3. The Mechanism
**Mechanism 103 (The Island Model)**:
A topology where subpopulations evolve in isolation, optimizing for distinct local reward functions (Critic Committees). Migration is rare or non-existent.

**Mechanism 104 (Relative Fitness Tournaments)**:
Evaluation is pairwise and relative, not absolute. $f(s) = P(s > s')$. This avoids Goodharting on a fixed proxy metric.

## 4. The Principle
**Principle 170 (Subjective Isolation)**:
> When the objective function is subjective or multimodal, enforce topology isolation. Do not pool critics. Do not average scores. Let distinct "schools of thought" evolve independently.

## 5. System Implications
- **Evaluation**: For creative tasks, stop using "Average Score". Use "Best Score in Niche".
- **Team Structure**: Allow "factions" of agents to specialize in different approaches (e.g., Code Style A vs B) rather than forcing a unified style guide for everything.
- **Routing**: Route tasks to the "Island" (agent cluster) most aligned with the user's preference, rather than a generic pool.
