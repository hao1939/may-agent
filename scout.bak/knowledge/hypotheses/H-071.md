## Hypothesis

If open-ended tasks (research, architecture design, hypothesis generation) use a structured disagreement protocol — where multiple agent perspectives are instantiated, disagreements are explicitly surfaced, and consensus is iteratively synthesized — task quality will improve compared to single-agent or simple-aggregation approaches.

## Grounding

Liu et al. (2604.19589) demonstrate that TeamFusion's three-phase protocol (preference instantiation → structured discussion → iterative synthesis) outperforms direct aggregation baselines across open-ended tasks. This directly challenges H-099's claim that discovery tasks resist ALL interventions — the issue may be intervention *type*, not task resistance.

## Connection to Existing Hypotheses

- **Refines H-099** (Discovery Task Boundary): Not all interventions fail on open-ended tasks — aggregation-style interventions fail. Structured disagreement protocols may succeed.
- **Addresses DD555** (Diversity Collapse): Explicit disagreement surfacing prevents the structural coupling that causes diversity collapse.

## Testable Predictions

1. Multi-perspective research tasks (scout + bob generating hypotheses independently, then reconciling) will produce higher-quality hypotheses than single-agent generation
2. Explicitly surfacing disagreements between agent outputs before synthesis will preserve more diverse insights than direct merging
3. The benefit scales with task open-endedness — highly constrained tasks won't benefit
