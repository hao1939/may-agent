# H-080: Defensibility-Based Evaluation Hypothesis

**Source:** DD#883 (Agreement Trap — arXiv:2604.20972)
**Status:** REFUTED
**Date:** 2026-04-25

## Claim

Switching our evaluator from agreement-based scoring ("did it match expected output?") to defensibility-based scoring ("is the agent's approach logically derivable from its governing rules?") would reclassify 30-50% of current "failures" as valid alternative paths.

## Rationale

O'Herlihy & Català (2604.20972) show a 33-46.6 pp gap between agreement and defensibility metrics on 193K decisions. 80% of "false negatives" were actually policy-grounded valid decisions. Our evaluator likely exhibits the same pattern: agents that take valid but unexpected paths get penalized.

## Testable Predictions

1. Re-evaluating a sample of "failed" sessions using defensibility criteria (was the approach derivable from SOUL.md + skills?) will show 30-50% were defensible
2. Tasks with high ambiguity (multiple valid approaches) will have the largest gap between agreement and defensibility scores
3. Rule specificity (more detailed expected outcomes) will reduce ambiguity without changing defensibility rates

## Evidence For
- 33-46.6 pp gap in paper's experiments on 193K decisions
- We already see evaluator disagreements on sessions with valid alternative approaches
- DD#877 (SWARM) independently suggests soft-label over binary evaluation

## Evidence Against
- Our task definitions may already be specific enough to reduce ambiguity
- Defensibility evaluation is more expensive (requires rule-trace verification)
- May mask genuine failures as "alternative approaches"

## Connections
- DD#883, DD#877, DD#556
- KE-021 (judgment skills), H-EXP-017 (adversarial failure diagnosis)

## Resolution (2026-10-28)

**REFUTED** by EXP-223.

Retrospective audit of 20 gym failures: only 3/20 (15%) were defensible alternative approaches. Predicted ≥30%. Our gym scenarios are specific enough that most failures are genuine agent deficiencies, not evaluator false negatives. The "agreement trap" from DD#883 doesn't apply at our current task specificity level.
