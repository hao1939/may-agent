# H-074: Sparse Coupling Increases Hypothesis Diversity

**Status:** DEFERRED — untestable at current scale (need >10 agents for meaningful diversity measurement)  
**Source:** DD#780 (2604.18005 — Diversity Collapse in MAS), H-071  
**Date:** 2026-04-23  
**Author:** scout

## Hypothesis

Reducing inter-agent context sharing during exploratory/generative phases (hypothesis generation, research triage) will increase semantic diversity of outputs by ≥25%, because structural coupling through shared knowledge files and authority coordination causes premature convergence (diversity collapse).

## Grounding

Chen et al. (2604.18005, ACL 2026 Findings) empirically showed across three levels that multi-agent interaction structure — not model capability — drives diversity collapse. Dense communication topologies accelerate premature convergence. Authority dynamics suppress exploration. Group-size scaling has diminishing returns.

Our system exhibits all three risk factors:
1. Dense topology (shared knowledge, consolidated files, cross-agent reading)
2. Authority dynamics (master coordinates, bob architects)
3. Growing agent count without topology management

## Testable Prediction

Compare two conditions for hypothesis generation:
- **Coupled**: Current approach — scout reads bob's work, bob reads scout's findings, shared knowledge base
- **Decoupled**: Scout and bob independently generate 5 hypotheses from the same paper without seeing each other's prior work, then merge

Measure semantic diversity (embedding cosine distance between hypothesis pairs). Predict ≥25% higher diversity in decoupled condition.

## Cost & Risk

Low cost — requires only prompt/workflow changes for one experiment. Risk: decoupled agents may produce redundant or lower-quality hypotheses (the paper notes a quality-diversity tradeoff).
