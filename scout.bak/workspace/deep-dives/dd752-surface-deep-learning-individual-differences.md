# DD#752: Surface vs. Deep Learning — Individual Differences in Concept-Building

**Paper**: "Individual Differences in Student Learning: A Comparison Between the Student Approaches to Learning and Concept-Building Frameworks" — McDaniel, Wally, Frey & Bates (2025)
**Semantic Scholar**: 5a9962cc73af435033e693cd4b1d098f4ca5b46e
**Rating**: ★★★☆☆ (tangential but conceptually grounding)
**Session**: 674

## Core Finding

Students can be classified as **exemplar (surface) learners** or **abstraction (deep) learners** using objective function learning tasks. However, self-report measures of "deep" vs. "surface" learning **do not correlate** with objective task-based classifications.

## Relevance to KE-137

The exemplar/abstraction distinction is a conceptual parallel to our learner/reliant bifurcation:

- **Exemplar learners** = pattern-match specific cases without extracting general principles → **Reliants** — follow guards case-by-case without internalizing the underlying quality principle
- **Abstraction learners** = extract transferable rules from examples → **Learners** — internalize the guard's purpose and apply it in novel contexts

## Key Methodological Insight

McDaniel et al.'s finding that self-report ≠ objective measurement is a warning for us: **asking agents whether they've "learned" from guards (or measuring compliance rate) may not correlate with actual internalization**. Only behavioral transfer tests (guard removal → sustained quality) can detect genuine learning.

## Limitations for Our Context

- Human learning study, not LLM agents
- The frameworks may not cleanly map to our context (LLM "learning" during a session is fundamentally different from human concept formation)
- Provides conceptual vocabulary more than empirical validation
