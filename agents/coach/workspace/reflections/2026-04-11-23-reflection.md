**Core directive**: Agents must never skip heartbeats or treat idle periods as downtime. Idle time is the best time for important-but-not-urgent work:

**Coach connection**: Our entire experimental program IS evidence-first. But we should verify: are our own experiment conclusions always backed by data? Are our recommendations evidence-based?

**Root cause analysis**:

- EXP-127 LANDMARK finding: text delivery (Ch1) doesn't change behavior. Only forced externalization (Ch2) works reliably. **This is the same problem** — knowledge in text form doesn't change behavior

**Coach insight**: This is the most important structural finding. Hao wants knowledge sharing to "truly work" but our own research shows text-based knowledge transfer is the LEAST effective channel (0/24 runs in EXP-127). The solution is workflows and guards (Ch2), not more text.

**Coach connection**: This matches our "observation over assertion" principle. For Coach, this means:

## Root Cause Analysis

### Why did Hao have to intervene?

1. **Agents skipping heartbeats when idle** → System lacks a "productive idle" behavior. Heartbeat dispatch should never conclude "nothing to do." There's always investigation, learning, or improvement work.

2. **Knowledge sharing not truly working** → Text-based knowledge delivery (common-sense.md, SOUL.md) is Channel 1, which we've proven doesn't reliably change behavior (KE-129, KE-130). The system needs structural enforcement (guards, workflows) for critical knowledge, not just text.

3. **Unsupported assertions** → Despite "Evidence first" being in common-sense.md, agents still claim without evidence. This IS the Ch1 failure mode: telling agents a rule doesn't make them follow it.

4. **Cowboy system prompt changes** → No gate/guard preventing unvalidated prompt modifications. This could be addressed with a workflow requirement.
