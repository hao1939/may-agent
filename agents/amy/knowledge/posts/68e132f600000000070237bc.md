## Content
Paper: **A Practitioner's Guide to Multi-turn Agentic Reinforcement Learning** (arXiv:2510.01132, Oct 2025)

### Core Revelation
LLMs becoming agents is easy; having them sustain multi-turn dialogue, continuous action, and long-term memory without making mistakes is hard.

### Three Mountains (core difficulties)
1. **Environment too complex:** Real tasks require dozens of chained decisions. Model must perceive, understand, output commands, AND remember previous steps.
2. **Reward too sparse:** Get scored once every 10+ steps? Model can't learn whether intermediate steps were correct. Many RL methods train hundreds of hours with no improvement.
3. **Policy too fragmented:** SFT = "textbook learning," RL = "combat practice." Wrong ratio → "learned patterns but can't adapt" (fake intelligence).

### DiffGuide Framework (proposed solution)
Three-pillar: Environment × Reward × Policy

**Core mechanisms:**
- Token-level reward assignment: Only reward <eos> action tokens, not whole sentences
- Credit assignment: Temporal difference method to calculate per-step contribution → model knows "which step went wrong"
- SFT + RL ratio: Found optimal balance empirically — maintain language stability while enabling generalization
- Algorithm choice: PPO/GRPO more robust; RLOO for small tasks. "Stable first, fast second."

### Key Insights
1. **Start small, then scale:** Learn basic logic in TextWorld first, then transfer to complex environments
2. **SFT isn't "more is better":** Small amount of high-quality demos + continuous RL = better "generalization from examples"
3. **Rewards should be dense, not sparse:** Milestone rewards significantly accelerate convergence

## Analysis Note
This paper CONFIRMS #1 (Iteration Is Poison) at the RL training level — multi-turn interaction is identified as fundamentally harder than single-turn, with three distinct failure modes that compound. The solution (dense intermediate rewards, credit assignment) is essentially engineering around the iteration poison problem: making each step individually accountable rather than relying on end-to-end feedback through many iterations. Does NOT falsify #1 — instead provides the mechanistic explanation for WHY iteration is poison (sparse reward + environment complexity + policy fragmentation = compounding errors).
